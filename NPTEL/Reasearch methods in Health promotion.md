## **Health**

* The WHO defines health as a state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity; the lecture also connects this with the ability to lead a socially and economically productive life. 
* The biomedical definition treats health as the absence of disease or disability. 
* The biomedical definition is easier to measure because it focuses on whether disease or disability is present or absent. 
* The WHO definition is broader and more holistic, but it is also criticized for being too broad and difficult to measure. 
* The transcript reinforces that the WHO definition is more useful for health promotion because it opens the way to understanding multiple determinants of health, not just disease status. 

## **Determinants of Health**

* Determinants are factors that decisively affect the nature or outcome of something. 
* Health is influenced by more than health care services; many other factors also shape health status. 
* The context in which a person lives is very important for both health status and quality of life. 
* Determinants of health are the factors that influence the health status of individuals or populations. 

### **Main Determinants Listed by WHO**

* **Social and economic environment**

  * Includes culture and traditions.
  * Shapes everyday life, opportunities, and health-related choices. 
* **Physical environment**

  * Includes safe water, clean air, healthy workplaces, safe houses, and good roads.
  * These conditions contribute directly to good health. 
* **Individual characteristics**

  * Includes genetics, gender, behaviors, and practices.
  * Examples given include diet, physical activity, smoking, drinking, and coping skills for life stress. 

## **Health Promotion**

* Health promotion gained importance as attention moved toward the social determinants of health and the broader social and environmental factors that influence health. This shift helped shape the Ottawa Charter for Health Promotion at the First International Conference on Health Promotion in Ottawa in 1986. 
* Health promotion is defined as the process of enabling people to increase control over and improve their health. 
* It goes beyond individual behavior and includes social and environmental interventions. 
* The lecture presents health promotion as a paradigm shift because earlier health thinking focused mainly on treating disease, while health promotion emphasizes improving health and well-being and preventing disease. 
* The transcript adds that health promotion is not only about intervention after illness begins; it also prevents disease in the pre-pathogenesis phase and supports healthy practices already present in communities. 

## **Health Promotion and the Determinants of Health**

* Health promotion works through **health education** and **health protection**, and health protection operates through healthy public policies and programs. 
* **Health education**

  * Involves educational efforts to change harmful behavior and lifestyles.
  * Examples include open defecation and addiction as behaviors/lifestyles that adversely affect health.
  * Encourages use of available health services.
  * Promotes participation in preventive services such as cervical cancer screening. 
* **Health protection**

  * Addresses policies and regulations that are preventive in nature.
  * Example given: a regulatory approach to prevent over-speeding. 
* The combined effort of health education and health protection is needed. 
* Health promotion improves health by influencing lifestyles, health services, and especially environments, including physical, cultural, and socioeconomic circumstances. 
* The transcript emphasizes that health promotion changes determinants in a positive direction so that healthy practices are encouraged and harmful practices are reduced. 

## **Health Promotion Approach to Address Health Disparities and Inequities**

* Health promotion reduces inequities by improving health literacy and creating supportive environments. 
* The two major approaches shown are:

  * **Health Education**: improving individual knowledge and skills.
  * **Advocacy for Public Health Policies**: creating a supportive environment. 
* These approaches modulate the determinants of health through health promotion. 
* Improved health outcomes and reduction in health inequities are linked; one supports the other. 

## **Scope of Health Promotion**

### **Empowerment**

* Empowerment is a central scope of health promotion.
* It means putting people in charge of their own health so they can actively make decisions affecting it. 
* Empowerment is connected with social and economic development and status. 
* The transcript gives a gender example: if only men make health-related decisions in a household, women are not empowered.
* Health promotion aims to reduce such inequities so that women can take decisions related to their own health, such as contraceptive choices. 

### **Need-Based Interventions**

* Health promotion carries out need-based policy, organizational, economic, regulatory, and environmental interventions. 
* Without policy support for social change, health education alone may not be enough to help people achieve their health goals. 
* The transcript gives examples of economic interventions such as incentives under health programs, where beneficiaries receive monetary support for performing desired practices. 
* Regulatory interventions include laws on speed limits, helmet use, and seat belt use. 
* Environmental interventions may involve reducing pollution or preventing industries from releasing harmful substances into air, water, rivers, or groundwater. 

### **Shared Responsibility**

* Health promotion fosters shared responsibility.
* It shifts the locus of health initiatives and control over determinants from medical institutions and health professionals to individuals, families, schools, and worksites. 
* Greater collaboration among sectors, organizations, and individuals is necessary. 

### **Participation**

* Participatory strategies make programs more relevant and appropriate for the people affected. 
* People become more committed to changes they helped design or adapt for their own circumstances. 
* The transcript stresses that participation should include all stakeholders, not only beneficiaries. 

### **Ecological Approaches**

* Ecological approaches view health as the result of interdependence between the individual and the ecosystem. 
* The ecosystem includes family, community, culture, and the physical and social environment. 
* Health promotion should apply integrated strategies appropriate to the local context. 
* The transcript adds that local stakeholders must be engaged so that strategies fit the actual setting. 

## **Key Value Driving Health Promotion**

* The fundamental value behind health promotion is that the highest attainable standard of health is a fundamental right of every human being. 
* This right applies without distinction of race, religion, political belief, economic condition, or social condition. 
* The lecture uses this principle to support equity and empowerment in health promotion. 

## **Lecture Summary**

* Health is best understood through the WHO definition because it is holistic, even though it is harder to measure than the biomedical definition. 
* Health is shaped by multiple determinants, including social, economic, physical, and individual factors. 
* Health promotion is the process of enabling people to increase control over and improve their health, and it goes beyond individual behavior to include social and environmental action. 
* It operates through health education and health protection. 
* Its scope includes empowerment, policy and program interventions, shared responsibility, participation, and ecological approaches. 
* The final aim is improved health outcomes, reduced inequities, and better quality of life, while treating health as a fundamental human right.
* The transcript closes by restating that the lecture is only the first step before moving into deeper concepts of health promotion and later into research methods in the subject.
