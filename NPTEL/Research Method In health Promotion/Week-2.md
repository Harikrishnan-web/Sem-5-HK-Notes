## **Health Behaviors**

* Positive, informed changes in health behaviors are the ultimate aim of health behavior change programs.
* In the broadest sense, health behavior includes the actions of individuals, groups, and organizations, along with the determinants, correlates, and consequences of those actions.
* Health behavior is not limited to personal action alone; it also includes social change, policy development and implementation, improved coping skills, and enhanced quality of life.

### **Definitions of Health Behavior**

* One definition describes health behavior as any activity undertaken by a person who believes himself or herself to be healthy, for the purpose of preventing disease or detecting it at an asymptomatic stage.
* Another definition describes health behavior as any activity undertaken to prevent or detect diseases or to improve health and well-being.

### **Types of Behaviors That Influence Health**

* Health-enhancing behaviors include exercise participation and healthy eating.
* Health-protective behaviors include vaccination against disease and condom use in response to the threat of HIV/AIDS.
* Health-detection behaviors include health screening, clinic attendance, and breast self-examination.
* Health-harming behaviors include smoking and excessive alcohol consumption, and health promotion also involves avoiding such behaviors.

### **Kasl and Cobb’s Three Categories of Health Behavior**

* **Preventive health behavior**

  * Any activity undertaken by a person who believes himself or herself to be healthy.
  * The purpose is to prevent disease or detect illness in an asymptomatic stage.
* **Illness behavior**

  * Any activity undertaken by a person who perceives himself or herself to be ill.
  * The purpose is to define the state of health and discover a suitable remedy.
* **Sick-role behavior**

  * Any activity undertaken by a person who considers himself or herself to be ill, for the purpose of getting well.
  * It includes receiving treatment from medical providers, usually involves dependent behaviors, and often leads to exemption from usual responsibilities.

## **Disciplinary Influences on Health Behavior Change over Time**

* Health behavior change has been addressed by multiple fields and professions, including health education, public health, psychology, social work, and various health and medical specialties.
* Clinical psychologists have traditionally focused on changing individuals.
* Social work tends to address individuals within their social and family contexts.
* During the 1970s and 1980s, health education emphasized individual behavior as a determinant of health status, which reduced attention to broader social determinants of health.
* Later, experts recommended that interventions on social and behavioral factors should link multiple levels of influence:

  * individual
  * interpersonal
  * institutional
  * community
  * policy levels

## **Settings for Health Behavior Change**

### **School**

* School-based health behavior change programs include classroom teaching, teacher training, and changes in school environments that support healthy behaviors.

### **Communities**

* Community-based health promotion draws on social relationships and organizations to reach large populations through media and interpersonal strategies.
* Community engagement and community mobilization help planners gain support and design suitable health messages and delivery mechanisms.
* Community participation is important because planners must understand community views, barriers, beliefs, and feasibility before designing interventions. The lecture stresses that community members must be included rather than having interventions imposed on them.

### **Workplace**

* The workplace is a source of both stress and social support because people spend so much time at work.
* Effective worksite programs can use social support as a buffer against stress and aim to improve worker health and health practices.

### **Health Care Settings**

* Health behavior change programs in health care settings include work with high-risk individuals, patients, their families, and the surrounding community.
* In-service training for health care providers is also part of health care-based health promotion.
* Primary care settings are especially important because they can reach a substantial number of people and improve population health.

### **Home**

* Health behavior change interventions can be delivered in homes through traditional public health approaches such as home visits.
* They can also be delivered through communication channels and media such as the Internet, telephone calls, and mail, depending on feasibility.

### **Marketplace**

* Marketplaces are important settings because they offer consumers fresh produce and other foods directly from producers, as well as ready-to-eat foods prepared by vendors.
* The lecture highlights the marketplace as a place where food hygiene, contamination risk, vendor behavior, and food handling practices can all become targets for health behavior change.

## **Audiences for Health Behavior Change**

### **Understanding the Target Audience**

* For interventions to be effective, strategies must be designed with an understanding of the recipients or target audiences.
* Important audience characteristics include health status, cultural context, social characteristics, beliefs, attitudes, values, skills, and past behaviors.
* Audiences may be reached as individuals, groups, through organizations, as communities, or through combinations of these approaches.
* Audiences may include health professionals, people at risk for disease, or patients.

### **Sociodemographic Characteristics and Ethnic/Racial Background**

* Audience characteristics such as gender, age, race, marital status, place of residence, and employment shape health behavior change efforts.
* Recognition of differences in disease and mortality across socioeconomic and ethnic or racial groups has increased efforts to reduce or eliminate health disparities.

### **Lifecycle Stage**

* Health education should be provided across the life cycle, from childbirth education to self-care education and rehabilitation for the very old.
* The lecture emphasizes a life-cycle approach, meaning the audience and intervention must fit the person’s stage of life.

### **Disease and At-Risk Status**

* People diagnosed with life-threatening diseases often experience symptoms as well as distress related to prognosis and medical decision-making.
* Timing, channels, and audiences for patient education should therefore be carefully considered.
* Successful patient education depends on a sound understanding of the patient’s viewpoint.
* Models and theories of health behavior can help suggest strategies to prevent relapse and improve maintenance of recommended practices for high-risk individuals.

## **Lecture Summary**

* Health behavior includes actions that prevent disease, detect disease early, protect health, improve health and well-being, and support quality of life.
* Health behavior change has shifted from an individual-only focus toward multilevel influence, including interpersonal, institutional, community, and policy levels.
* Effective health behavior change requires suitable settings such as schools, communities, workplaces, health care systems, homes, and marketplaces.
* Interventions must be designed for the right audiences, with attention to culture, social context, demographics, life stage, disease status, and risk status.
* The lecture closes by reinforcing that the goal is not only to start health behavior change, but to maintain it and prevent relapse over time.
