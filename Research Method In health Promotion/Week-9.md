# WEEK 9 — BCC AND SBCC

## Lecture 1 — BCC and SBCC (Part I)

### Behavior Change Communication (BCC)

* **Behavior Change Communication (BCC)** is a process of working with:

  * Individuals
  * Communities
  * Societies
* BCC aims to:

  * Develop communication strategies that promote **positive behaviors** appropriate to the setting.
  * Provide a **supportive environment** that enables people to initiate and sustain positive behaviors. 

### Difference Between BCC and IEC

* Simply providing information and telling people how they should behave is **not sufficient** to produce behavior change.
* Information is necessary because it helps people make personal decisions.
* However, behavior is not determined only by:

  * Information
  * Personal choice
* Behavior change also requires a **supportive environment**.
* Therefore:

  * **IEC is a component of BCC.**
  * **BCC builds upon IEC** by recognizing the environmental and social factors influencing behavior. 

### Emphasis Behaviors — Example: Maternal and Child Health (MCH)

#### Reproductive Health Practices

* Women of reproductive age should:

  * Practice family planning.
  * Seek antenatal care when pregnant.
  * Delay the first pregnancy.
  * Practice birth spacing.
  * Limit family size.
* Pregnant women should:

  * Seek antenatal care at least **two times** during pregnancy.
  * Take iron tablets.

#### Infant and Child Feeding Practices

* Mothers should provide:

  * Age-appropriate foods.
  * Age-appropriate fluids.
* **Exclusive breastfeeding** should be practiced for about **6 months**.
* From around 6 months:

  * Introduce appropriate complementary feeding.
  * Continue breastfeeding until **24 months**.

#### Immunization Practices

* Infants should receive the **full course of vaccinations**.
* Women of childbearing age should receive an appropriate course of **tetanus vaccination**.
* Infants should receive measles immunization as soon as possible after **9 months**.
* Infants should be immunized even when sick.
* A sick infant can receive immunization during a visit for curative care.
* Pregnant women and women of childbearing age should seek **tetanus toxoid vaccination at every opportunity**. 

### Planning Implementation of BCC Interventions

#### Example — Sanitation in Lahanga Village, Odisha

* Situational analysis identified several behavioral and educational gaps:

  * **87%** of the community practiced open defecation.
  * The community also had low literacy levels.
  * Many people lacked knowledge about the harmful effects of open defecation.
  * Only **13% of households** had access to toilets.
  * Only **10% of families** living in households with toilets had all family members using them.
* Social norms contributed to the problem:

  * Males and children were socially accepted as defecating in the open.
  * Some families feared that toilet pits would fill quickly.
* Children's feces were often disposed of:

  * On paths
  * In open areas outside the household
* This was partly because people felt it was easier to make small children defecate in the open.
* **93%** felt that open defecation was shameful.
* However, many believed poor people could not afford sanitary latrines.
* **89%** cited financial problems as a reason for not constructing toilets.
* **55%** were unaware of procedures for obtaining government financial assistance for sanitary latrine construction.
* **57%** knew about the Swachh Bharat Abhiyan. 

#### Identified Gaps

* Lack of knowledge about how open defecation contributes to disease transmission.
* Lack of knowledge about how these diseases affect children's:

  * Health
  * Well-being
  * Nutritional status
* Lack of knowledge about government incentives for constructing sanitary latrines.

#### Example Objectives

* Within the next **3 months**, at least **90%** of villagers should know how open defecation contributes to disease transmission.
* Within the next **3 months**, at least **90%** should know how open defecation affects children's health and well-being and contributes to undernutrition.
* Within the next **2 months**, at least **90%** should know about government incentives for constructing sanitary latrines. 

### Evaluation of BCC Interventions

#### Formative / Process Evaluation

* Conducted **during implementation**.
* Monitors the process.
* It asks whether what was planned was actually implemented.
* It is **not primarily about results**.

#### Summative Evaluation

* Conducted **after the intervention**.
* Evaluates:

  * Outcomes
  * Impact

#### Impact Evaluation

* Determines whether the intervention produced the desired effect on the **behavioral or environmental factors** targeted for change.
* Example:

  * After 12 months, 50% of villagers have stopped open defecation.

#### Outcome Evaluation

* Determines whether the intervention achieved the outcomes specified in its objectives.

* Example:

  * After 3 months, 90% of villagers know how open defecation contributes to disease transmission.

* Some outcomes and impacts may take a long time to become visible.

* Example:

  * Lifestyle changes among young people intended to prevent noncommunicable diseases may not show health benefits until middle age. 

### Case Example — Mobilizing Demand for Tetanus Toxoid in Pakistan

* In Pakistan:

  * About **80% of births** occurred at home without skilled attendance.
  * In 1999, only about half of women of childbearing age were immunized against tetanus.
  * About **22,000 newborns** died from tetanus that year.
* Initially, the government provided TT vaccination through antenatal services.
* This did not adequately meet the needs of women in the largely traditional culture.
* A campaign was developed by the Ministry of Health, UNICEF, WHO, JICA and SNL.
* **Lady Health Workers (LHWs)** conducted home visits because they were more acceptable to women than male vaccinators.
* A major barrier was suspicion that vaccination was connected with birth control.
* A multi-level campaign targeted:

  * Fathers and husbands
  * Mothers-in-law
  * Community leaders
  * Religious leaders
  * Teachers
* Religious and community leaders received information leaflets.
* Materials were distributed in girls' schools.
* Local mosques promoted acceptance of vaccinators before campaigns.
* Vaccinators were motivated using a short docudrama, which was also broadcast nationally.
* Result:

  * More than **80% of the five million women** in the target group received three doses of vaccine. 

### Other BCC Case Examples

#### Birth Preparation

* Birth preparation includes planning for:

  * Delivery
  * Skilled attendant
  * Referral facility in case of emergency
  * Transportation
  * Saving money
  * Community support for identifying transport.
* Specific behaviors differ between programs and settings.

#### Nepal

* **11%** of Nepali women delivered entirely by themselves.
* About **89%** of deliveries occurred at home.
* About **78%** occurred without skilled attendance.
* A major program focus was hygienic delivery.
* Safe delivery kits were produced in Nepal.
* Yet:

  * **80%** of women were unfamiliar with them.
  * Only **8%** had used them.
* A social marketing campaign was planned to promote their use.

#### Palestine

* In some settings, planning for an emergency is considered bad luck.
* In the West Bank, mothers were encouraged to view emergencies as something that should be prepared for.
* Roadblocks and border delays could cause women to deliver while travelling to facilities.
* Positive deviance research showed that women who planned in advance were better able to protect their infants.
* Women were encouraged to stay with friends or relatives located conveniently near health facilities as birth approached.
* Materials were developed to help women discuss emergency birth plans with their families. 

### Case Example — Feeding Messages in The Gambia

* A USAID program in The Gambia addressed wasting following chronic diarrhea.
* Feeding messages were more difficult to communicate than rehydration messages.
* Non-literate mothers without ORS access could learn and recite a home-mixed water-sugar-salt solution.
* However, they did not readily adopt nutrition changes during illness.
* After the first year:

  * **66%** could recite the SSS formula.
  * **47%** reported using it.
  * Only **21%** had adopted the message to give solid foods during and after diarrhea.
* Messages were revised in the second year:

  * Made more specific.
  * Distinguished feeding during illness from feeding during recovery.
  * Recognized that children often lacked appetite.
  * Recommended **small, frequent feeds**.
  * Highlighted local energy-rich foods.
  * Suggested adding simple ingredients to increase calories and protein.
  * Promoted solid foods as a source of "power" and weight gain.
* The revised campaign used a slogan and graphic materials.
* Print ORS materials were also revised to include feeding messages.
* After the revised messages:

  * Children receiving no solids or liquids decreased from **31% to around 10–16.7%**.
  * In the following year, **92.4–97.8%** received solid foods or supplemental liquids. 

### Case Example — Malaria in Zambia

* Research on care-seeking practices in Zambia informed recommendations at several program levels.

#### Simple Malaria

* Mothers recognized fever as a sign of malaria.
* They sought:

  * Free drugs from health centers
  * Chloroquine from drug vendors when they had money.
* Problems identified:

  * Incorrect antimalarial doses were commonly given.
  * Health workers sometimes provided partial doses.
  * **40% of mothers** did not understand medication instructions.
  * Mothers returning with treatment failure were sometimes given the same drug again because health workers did not ask what had already been given.
* Recommendations included:

  * Policy change so mothers received the full drug course.
  * Dosage charts at health centers.
  * Graphic dosage cards for non-literate caregivers.
  * Communication-skills training for health workers.
  * Allowing community health workers to provide first-line drugs because mothers recognized signs of simple malaria.

#### Severe Malaria

* Mothers recognized convulsions and even twitching as a warning sign.
* They often went to traditional healers.
* Recommendation:

  * Work with traditional healers to encourage referral to health centers.

#### Messages for Mothers

* Messages emphasized:

  * Correct amount of medication.
  * Completing the treatment course.
  * Signs of treatment failure.
  * Urgent health-center care when a child begins twitching or convulsing.

# Lecture 2 — BCC and SBCC (Part II)

## Using Theory for BCC Program Planning and Research

* Theory provides a **road map** for:

  * Studying problems
  * Developing appropriate interventions
  * Evaluating interventions
* Theory informs planners throughout the program.
* It helps explain:

  * Health behavior dynamics
  * Processes through which behavior changes
  * Effects of social and physical environments
* It helps planners identify:

  * Suitable target audiences
  * Methods for producing change
  * Outcomes to evaluate
  * Indicators to monitor and measure
* Theory guides the search for why people do or do not engage in particular health behaviors.
* It helps identify what planners need to understand before designing behavior-change programs.
* Theory helps develop strategies that reach target audiences and create impact.
* BCC planning, implementation and monitoring based on theory are presented as more likely to succeed than approaches developed without a theoretical perspective. 

## Explanatory Theory and Change Theory

* Both **Explanatory Theory** and **Change Theory** can be used to:

  * Understand health behavior.
  * Plan interventions.
  * Conduct research.
  * Implement BCC interventions effectively. 

## Transtheoretical Model — Stages of Change

* The lecture uses smoking cessation to demonstrate application of the model.
* Example:

  * A company has 200 employees who smoke.
  * Group smoking-cessation clinics are offered.
  * Only 50 smokers attend.
  * The question becomes: **How can the remaining 150 smokers be reached?**
* The Stages of Change Model helps:

  * Understand why people are not participating.
  * Develop a comprehensive program.
  * Tailor interventions to the person's stage of change.
  * Support maintenance of behavior change.

### Stages

* **Pre-contemplation**

  * Not interested in trying to quit.

* **Contemplation**

  * Thinking about quitting soon.

* **Preparation**

  * Ready to plan how to quit.

* **Action**

  * Currently trying to quit.

* **Maintenance**

  * Trying to remain smoke-free.

* Simple questions can determine where a person is on the continuum.

* The person's stage helps tailor:

  * Messages
  * Strategies
  * Programs

* Example:

  * Someone who enjoys smoking and does not want to quit may need an educational intervention to move from **pre-contemplation → contemplation**.
  * Carbon monoxide testing can demonstrate the health effects of smoking. 

## Theory of Reasoned Action and Planned Behavior

### Theory of Planned Behaviour — Example: Pap Test

* Surveillance data showed that younger women were more likely to receive Pap tests than older women.
* A health department planned a cervical cancer screening program targeting older women.
* A survey was designed to identify beliefs, attitudes and intentions associated with seeking a Pap test.

### Variables Studied

* **Behavior**

  * When the woman last received a Pap test.

* **Intention**

  * Likelihood of seeking a Pap test.

* **Attitude**

  * Attitude toward receiving a Pap test.

* **Subjective norm**

  * Whether important people would want her to receive a Pap test.

* **Perceived behavioral control**

  * Whether getting a Pap test is perceived to be under her control.

* Survey findings would be compared with actual Pap-test data to identify beliefs, attitudes and intentions that predict screening behavior. 

## Precaution Adoption Process Model (PAPM)

* PAPM describes **seven distinct stages** from:

  * Lack of awareness
  * Through adoption
  * To maintenance of a behavior.
* It has been applied to health behaviors such as:

  * Osteoporosis prevention
  * Colorectal cancer screening
  * Mammography
  * Hepatitis B vaccination
* Early stages include:

  * **Stage 1:** Completely unaware of the hazard.
  * **Stage 2:** Aware of the issue but unengaged.
  * **Stage 3:** Facing a decision about whether to act.
  * **Stage 4:** Decides not to act. 

## Diffusion of Innovations

### Key Attributes Affecting Speed and Extent of Diffusion

* **Relative advantage**

  * Is the innovation better than what it replaces?
* **Compatibility**

  * Does it fit the intended audience?
* **Complexity**

  * Is it easy to use?
* **Trialability**

  * Can it be tried before deciding to adopt?
* **Observability**

  * Are its results visible and easily measurable? 

### Example — Healthy Lifestyle Program in Schools

* A school program aimed to establish healthy lifestyle habits and prevent chronic disease.
* Components included:

  * Modifying fat and sugar content of school lunches.
  * Classroom-based health education.
  * Physical education to encourage physical activity.
* Follow-up showed that participating children maintained healthier habits during adolescence.
* For wider diffusion, planners needed to demonstrate:

  * **Relative advantage:** emphasize positive outcomes.
  * **Compatibility:** demonstrate approval of materials by state education authorities.
  * **Low complexity:** develop user-friendly materials for teachers and cafeteria workers.
  * **Trialability:** make materials available through a website.
  * **Observability:** demonstrate program components. 

### Key Takeaway

* Theory helps identify reasons behind behavior.
* It guides:

  * Program planning
  * Target-audience identification
  * Intervention selection
  * Evaluation indicators
* Both explanatory and change theories can support BCC research and implementation.

# Lecture 3 — BCC and SBCC (Part III)

## Paradigm Shift

* Behavior-change approaches have evolved through three broad stages:

**IEC → BCC → SBCC**

* **IEC**

  * Focused primarily on increasing individual awareness about health problems.
  * Used traditional health education strategies.
* **BCC**

  * Moved beyond awareness toward behavior change.
  * Focused on creating supportive/enabling environments.
* **SBCC**

  * Further expanded toward **social change**.
  * Aims to facilitate sustainable behavior change.

### Why the Shift Happened

* Individual behavior is influenced by:

  * Social norms
  * Cultural norms
  * Gender norms
  * Community influences
  * Environmental conditions
* Therefore:

  * Communities need to be mobilized to support recommended behaviors.
  * Advocacy is needed to influence policy and structural issues. 

## Example — Iron-Rich Food Among Pregnant Women

* The overall goal may be:

  * Within 12 months, at least **80% of pregnant women** consume iron-rich food daily.
* Separate objectives are required for different component behaviors.

### Component Objectives

* Within 4 months:

  * **100%** of pregnant women have access to affordable local iron-rich food.

* Within 6 months:

  * At least **90%** know and have skills to prepare/cook food in ways that prevent iron loss or improve iron absorption.

* Within 6 months:

  * **100% of household members** know the importance of appropriate food distribution for pregnant women, including:

    * Amount of food
    * Type of food
    * Iron-rich food
    * Combining meals with vitamin-C-rich foods to improve iron absorption.

* If major barriers are:

  * Social factors/social norms
  * Environmental factors such as price and marketing of iron-rich foods

* Then an **SBCC approach** is appropriate, using the Social Ecological Model. 

## SBCC Through the Social Ecological Model

* Behavior is influenced by factors operating at four major levels:

  * **Individual**
  * **Interpersonal**
  * **Community**
  * **Environment**
* The model's levels represent different domains of influence and people involved at each level.

### Four Cross-Cutting Factors — Triangle of Influence

**Information + Motivation + Ability to Act + Norms**

#### Information

* People need:

  * Timely
  * Accessible
  * Relevant information.
* Example:

  * Information about modern contraceptives and their side effects.

#### Motivation

* Determined by:

  * Attitudes
  * Beliefs
  * Perceptions of benefits
  * Perceptions of risks
  * Perceptions of seriousness
* Example:

  * Attitudes toward modern hormonal contraceptives.
  * Beliefs about family-planning benefits.

#### Ability to Act

* Motivation alone may not be enough.
* People may need:

  * Self-efficacy
  * Collective efficacy
  * Access to services
  * Ability/power to negotiate conditions.
* Example:

  * Women and girls may need the ability to negotiate conditions for contraceptive use.

#### Norms

* Norms strongly influence behavior.
* Types include:

  * **Perceived norms:** what an individual believes others expect.
  * **Sociocultural norms:** norms followed by communities/social or cultural groups.
  * **Gender norms:** socially constructed expectations for male and female behavior. 

## Advocacy, Social Mobilization and BCC

* SBCC uses an appropriate combination of strategies at different levels.

### Advocacy

* Raises:

  * Resources
  * Political commitment
  * Social leadership commitment
* Supports actions and goals.

### Social Mobilization

* Promotes:

  * Wider participation
  * Community mobilization
* Brings stakeholders together and empowers communities.

### Behavior Change Communication

* Focuses on changing:

  * Knowledge
  * Attitudes
  * Practices

* Targets specific program audiences.

* These strategies have separate but **mutually reinforcing objectives**.

* Social mobilization:

  * Raises awareness of a health problem.
  * Engages and motivates:

    * Community leaders
    * Public/private partners
    * Community organizations
  * Uses the **Community Organization Model** to empower communities through:

    * Active participation
    * Identification of health issues
    * Strategy development
    * Participatory decision-making. 

## Community Mobilization

* To mobilize means to **move something or someone**.
* Community mobilization is a **capacity-building process** in which community:

  * Individuals
  * Groups
  * Organizations
* Plan, implement and evaluate activities:

  * Participatively
  * Continuously
  * To improve health and other needs
  * Either on their own initiative or stimulated by others. 

### Community Participation

* Community mobilization addresses selected problems through:

  * Community engagement
  * Grassroots-led action.
* The first and essential component is **community participation**.
* People should actively participate in:

  * Planning
  * Implementation
  * Monitoring
* Participation is **not merely passive use of services**.
* Communities should participate from:

  * Defining the problem
  * Generating solutions
* Degree of engagement depends on:

  * Interest
  * Capabilities

### Community Organization

* After participation is ensured, community organization involves:

  * Organizing people so they can identify needs.
  * Prioritizing needs/objectives.
  * Developing confidence and willingness to act.
  * Finding resources.
  * Cooperative and collaborative action.
* Successful community organization leads to **community mobilization**. 

## Participatory Tools and Techniques

* Direct observation
* Group interviews
* Community maps
* Role-plays
* Stories
* Fables
* Proverbs
* Workshops. 

## Degrees of Community Participation

* **Co-option**

  * Local representatives are selected.
  * They have no real input or power.
* **Compliance**

  * Tasks are assigned with incentives.
  * Outsiders determine the agenda and direct the process.
* **Consultation**

  * Local opinions are obtained.
  * Outsiders analyze and decide the action.
* **Cooperation**

  * Local people and outsiders work together to determine realities.
  * Outsiders retain responsibility for directing the process.
* **Collective action**

  * Local people set their own agenda.
  * They mobilize and implement it without outside initiators/facilitators.
* **Co-learning**

  * Local people and outsiders share knowledge.
  * They develop new understanding together.
  * They jointly develop action plans.
  * Outsiders facilitate. 

## Social Marketing

* Social marketing uses **marketing techniques** to influence voluntary behavior.
* It goes beyond:

  * Informing
  * Persuading
* It reinforces behavior through:

  * Incentives
  * Other benefits
* It is an approach for promoting health behavior.

### Definition

* Alan Andreasen defines social marketing as applying commercial marketing technologies to:

  * Analysis
  * Planning
  * Execution
  * Evaluation
* The purpose is to influence voluntary behavior of target audiences to improve:

  * Personal welfare
  * Social welfare.

### Important Characteristics

* Social marketing programs are:

  * **Consumer-driven**
  * Targeted toward a defined group.
* **Market segmentation** means dividing a target audience into homogeneous subgroups with distinct, unifying characteristics and needs.
* Segmentation can use:

  * Gender
  * Exercise habits
  * Readiness for change
  * Media habits
* Example:

  * The broad group of "smokers" can be divided into meaningful subgroups. 

## Four Ps of Social Marketing

### Product

* The desired behavioral change.
* Includes:

  * Behavior being promoted
  * Benefits associated with it.

### Price

* Benefits and costs of adopting the behavior.
* Costs may involve:

  * Money
  * Time
  * Effort
  * Other barriers.

### Place

* Makes the new behavior:

  * Accessible
  * Convenient
  * Easy to perform.

### Promotion

* Communicates the product/behavior to the target audience.
* Explains:

  * Product/behavior
  * Benefits
  * Reasonable cost
  * Convenience. 

## Research and Evaluation in Social Marketing

* Social marketing begins with **formative research** to understand:

  * Target market perceptions
  * Needs
  * Wants
  * Health behavior
* Research examines:

  * Current behavior
  * What enables behavior
  * What reinforces behavior
* **Competitive/environmental analysis** examines:

  * The environment in which decisions are made.
  * Competing behaviors promoted to the target audience.
* Example:

  * Convenient inexpensive fast foods compete with messages encouraging consumption of five fruits and vegetables daily.
* Research also examines effects of:

  * Social surroundings
  * Physical surroundings
  * Economic situation. 

### Evaluation

* Formative research helps refine:

  * Concepts
  * Messages
  * Products
  * Services
  * Pricing
  * Distribution channels
* Qualitative methods such as:

  * Focus groups
  * Key informant interviews
* can pretest concepts, messages and materials.
* Pilot testing helps:

  * Verify effectiveness.
  * Identify diverse communication channels.
  * Measure outcomes.
* Process evaluation tracks:

  * Program outputs
  * Processes during implementation.
* Summative research monitors outcomes.
* Social marketing is most successful when implemented through a **research-driven process**, allowing consumer research to adjust messages and outputs. 

## Using Theories for Behaviour Change — Key Points

* Planning systems such as:

  * Social marketing
  * PRECEDE-PROCEED
* help practitioners develop programs through step-by-step examination of health and behavior at multiple levels.
* An ecological perspective provides two broad ways of addressing health problems:

  * Change people's behavior.
  * Change the environment.
* Powerful health promotion interventions integrate both.
* Individual-level theories can support strategies aimed at changing behavior.
* Community-level theories can support strategies aimed at changing environments.
* Interpersonal theories such as **Social Cognitive Theory** examine reciprocal interactions between individuals and their environments.
* Individual behavior influences the environment, and the environment also influences individual behavior.
* Individual-level strategies can indirectly change environments.

  * Example: improved eating habits can lead a cafeteria to provide healthier choices.
* Community-level strategies can produce individual behavior changes.

  * Example: community efforts to improve fruit and vegetable access can inspire healthier family eating.

# Lecture 4 — BCC and SBCC (Part IV)

## Designing a Social Behavior Change Communication Strategy

* SBCC uses data and creative ideas to:

  * Change or positively influence social norms for long-term, sustainable population-level behavior change.
  * Increase healthy behaviors.
  * Improve health-service provider/client interactions.
  * Strengthen community responses.
  * Influence decision-makers, family and peer networks.
  * Increase demand for health services and products.
  * Increase correct use of services and products.
  * Influence policy.
  * Increase local capacity for health-program planning and implementation. 

## Step 1 — Analyze the Situation

* Existing data should be examined first.
* If existing data are:

  * Insufficient
  * Outdated
  * Not sufficiently informative
* additional **formative research** should be conducted.
* Possible methods include:

  * Surveys
  * Focus group discussions
  * Key informant interviews
  * Observation checklists
* Data should be available **before strategy development**.

### Tasks in Situation Analysis

* **Task 1:** Share Vision
* **Task 2:** Identify Challenges and Root Causes
* **Task 3:** Understand Context of Core Challenge
* **Task 4:** Summarize Core Challenge
* **Task 5:** Determine Program Theory
* **Task 6:** Identify Potential Priority Groups
* **Task 7:** Assess Communication Needs and Resources
* **Task 8:** Assess Capabilities of Services and Products to Meet Demand
* **Task 9:** Conduct a Participation Analysis. 

## Task 2 — Identify Challenges and Root Causes

* Process:

  * Facilitated discussion
  * Critical thinking
  * Root-cause analysis tools
* Output:

  * Core challenge(s) guiding strategy design.
* **Core challenge** = key constraint preventing achievement of the shared vision.
* SBCC should focus on **one specific challenge at a time**.
* Most challenges have:

  * Multiple underlying causes
  * Multiple possible solutions
* Therefore, identifying the **primary/root cause** is important for determining the main reason behind the current situation. 

## Task 3 — Understand Context of the Core Challenge

* Uses the **Social Ecological Model**.
* Process:

  * Facilitated discussion.
* Output:

  * Tailored Social Ecological Model.
* Examine the challenge at every level.
* Similar questions are asked at each level, but answers differ depending on how the challenge affects that level. 

### Individual Level

Ask:

* What is the incidence/prevalence?
* How severe is the problem?
* Who is affected?
* What knowledge and skills could improve the situation?
* What beliefs and values could improve the situation?
* What beliefs and values could hinder improvement?
* What are the perceived:

  * Norms
  * Risks
  * Fears?
* What role does gender play?
* What are possible solutions?

  * Individual behavior change
  * Changes in beliefs
  * Changes in values
  * Changes in norms. 

### Family and Peer Network

* What are the effects on:

  * Family?
  * Peers?
* Who/what in the family influences behavior or social norms?
* Who/what in peer networks influences behavior or social norms?
* How does gender influence family and peer networks?
* Possible solutions include:

  * Behavior change
  * Changes in gender norms
  * Changes in family dynamics. 

### Community Level

* What are the effects on the community?
* Who/what influences behavior or social norms?
* Who/what can:

  * Facilitate change?
  * Hinder change?
* What services/products could help?
* Are they:

  * Available?
  * Accessible?
  * Affordable?
  * Acceptable?
* What communication resources exist?
* How does gender influence the community?
* Possible solutions:

  * Communication changes
  * Service-delivery changes
  * Product changes
  * Gender-norm changes
  * Community leadership changes. 

### Society Level

* Who/what influences behavior or social norms?
* What are the:

  * Social challenges?
  * Cultural challenges?
  * Economic challenges?
* Which social norms facilitate or hinder change?
* Which policies facilitate or hinder change?
* What can create an enabling environment for social/behavior change?
* Possible solutions:

  * Policy changes
  * More equitable gender norms
  * Equal service access
  * Structural changes in service provision. 

## Task 4 — Summarize the Core Challenge

* Process:

  * Discussion
  * Writing
* Output:

  * Core challenge statement.
* The statement should:

  * Summarize the challenge.
  * Identify key behaviors.
  * Identify relevant social norms.
  * Identify policies needed to make the shared vision a reality.
* Compare:

  * **Shared Vision**
  * **Current Situation**
* The **challenge** is the difference between the desired shared vision and the current situation. 

## Task 5 — Determine Program Theory

* A **theory** explains or predicts an event/situation through systematic observation and inference.
* A **program theory** explains:

  * Why the intended audience behaves as it does.
  * Assumptions about how the audience will behave or change through exposure to the SBCC program.
* Theory is important because SBCC programs based on social/behavioral theories are presented as more effective than programs without a theoretical base.
* The choice of theory depends on:

  * Type of change required.
  * Factors driving audience behavior.

### Behavior May Be Driven by Different Factors

* **Individual factors**

  * Preferences
  * Needs
  * Desires
* **Social factors**

  * Social pressure
  * Social cues
* **Structural/contextual factors**

  * Access to resources
  * Availability of products/services.

### Examples

* Brushing teeth before bed:

  * Individual need.

* Not smoking at a friend's house:

  * Social cues.

* Newlywed couple becoming pregnant soon after marriage:

  * Social influences.

* Availability of insecticide-treated bed nets:

  * Structural/contextual conditions.

* Because behavior has multiple influences:

  * Different theories may be required to address different barriers.

* If behavior is primarily individual:

  * Consider an individual-level theory.

* If behavior is primarily social or structural:

  * Select a theory addressing those levels. 

## Task 6 — Identify Potential Priority Groups

* Process:

  * Facilitated discussion.
* Output:

  * Thorough understanding of who is affected.
* Situation analysis should identify:

  * Who is affected.
  * Who communication should reach.
* The people most affected are **not always the communication audience**.
* Example:

  * Children under five may be most affected by diarrhea.
  * Parents/caregivers may be the communication audience.
* Choice of program theory affects how groups are analyzed.
* Priority groups can be identified by examining:

  * Common characteristics
  * Gender
  * Ideational factors
  * Family influences
  * Peer influences
  * Community influences
  * Society-level influences. 

### Demographic Characteristics

Consider:

* Age
* Sex
* Occupation
* Residence:

  * Rural
  * Urban
* Number of children
* Access to media
* Wealth
* Education.

### Gender Lens

Ask:

* How do gender roles/relationships affect the challenge?
* How does gender inequity contribute?
* What social norms/expectations define male/female behavior?
* How are men/boys affected?
* How are women/girls affected?
* Who has decision-making power?
* Are there differences in knowledge, attitudes and practices between:

  * Men and women
  * Boys and girls? 

## Ideational Factors

* **Ideation** refers to:

  * New ways of thinking.
  * Diffusion of these ways of thinking through social interaction in local communities.
* New ways of thinking are influenced by:

  * Knowledge
  * Attitudes
  * Discussion with others.

### Positive Ideational Factors

A person is more likely to adopt a new behavior when they:

* Have sufficient knowledge.

* Have developed a positive attitude.

* Have discussed it with others.

* Feel comfortable/right about doing it.

* These elements are:

  * Interrelated.
  * Cumulative.

* The more positive ideational elements a person possesses, the greater the likelihood of:

  * Adopting the new behavior.
  * Intending to adopt it in the future. 

## Social Ecological Factors Affecting Priority Groups

* The Social Ecological Model helps identify barriers to change at multiple levels.
* Positive influences on behavior can also exist at multiple levels.
* Influences can come from:

  * Family
  * Peer networks
  * Community
  * Social norms
  * Policies
  * Religious values
  * Cultural values
  * Physical environment
* Individuals do not behave independently.
* They exist within:

  * Families
  * Social networks
  * Communities
  * Nations
  * Physical environments.
* These contexts influence:

  * Behavior
  * Values
  * Ability to change.

Ask what factors:

* Affect behavior at:

  * Interpersonal level
  * Community level
  * Physical-environment level
  * Social-network level
  * Society level
* Could encourage behavior change at those same levels.

# Lecture 5 — BCC and SBCC (Part V)

## Step 2 — Audience

* Step 1 identifies potential priority groups.
* Step 2 divides and organizes them into audiences with similar:

  * Needs
  * Preferences
  * Characteristics
* This is called **audience segmentation**.
* Segmentation allows:

  * More effective interventions.
  * More appropriate interventions.
  * Customized messages.
  * Customized materials.

### Primary Audience

* Group of people who should receive the messages.
* May be:

  * Directly affected by the challenge.
  * Most at risk.
  * Best able to address the challenge.
  * Able to make decisions on behalf of affected people.
* Example:

  * Caregivers of young children.

### Influencing Audiences

* People who influence the primary audience directly or indirectly.
* Can include:

  * Family members
  * Service providers
  * Community leaders
  * Teachers
  * People who shape social norms
  * People who influence policy
  * People who influence how people think about the challenge. 

## Task 1 — Determine Audience Segments

* Process:

  * Facilitated discussion.
* Output:

  * Potential Audience Segmentation Table.

### Identify Potential Primary Audiences

Ask:

* Who is directly affected?
* Who is most at risk?
* Who makes decisions or has influence over affected people?

### Identify Potential Influencing Audiences

Ask:

* Who directly influences the primary audience?
* Who indirectly influences the primary audience?

### Characteristics Used for Segmentation

#### Demographic

* Age
* Gender
* Marital status
* Education
* Number of children.

#### Geographic

* Place of residence
* Region
* Urban/rural
* Conflict or peaceful area.

#### Socio-cultural

* Language
* Culture
* Religion
* Ethnicity
* Position/place in society.

#### Behavioral

* Behaviors that affect or influence the challenge.

#### Psychographic

* Personality
* Values
* Attitudes
* Interests
* Lifestyle.

#### Ideational

Factors that hinder or facilitate social and behavior change, including:

* Knowledge
* Beliefs and values
* Perceived risk
* Self-efficacy
* Social influence
* Social support
* Environmental support
* Environmental constraints. 

## Task 2 — Prioritize Audience Segments

* Process:

  * Facilitated discussion.
  * Ranking potential audience segments.
* Output:

  * Priority Audience Segments Table.

### Factors for Prioritization

* Revisit:

  * Ideational factors.
  * Details from Step 1.
* A **phased approach** can be used:

  * Start with audiences easiest to reach and most receptive to change.
  * Add other audiences in later phases.
* Consider efficient use of resources.
* When resources are limited:

  * Focus on fewer segments.
  * Focus on segments with potentially greater impact.
* The table uses a **1–5 scale**:

  * 5 = most important
  * 1 = least important. 

## Task 3 — Develop Audience Profiles

* Process:

  * Facilitated discussion and writing.
* Output:

  * Audience profiles.
* Create a profile for each audience segment to:

  * Personalize the audience.
  * Make the audience easier to understand.
* A profile describes characteristics of a specific audience using:

  * Qualitative data
  * Quantitative data.
* The profile should tell the story of an **individual representing the intended audience**.

### Information in the Profile

* Current behaviors

* Motivation

* Emotions

* Values

* Attitudes

* Age

* Income

* Religion

* Sex

* Place of residence

* Other relevant socio-demographic characteristics.

* During message/material development:

  * Focus on the representative individual.
  * Do not think only in terms of statistics or a large anonymous population. 

## Step 3 — Communication Objectives

* Communication objectives reflect the needs of the intended audience.
* They should clearly and concisely state:

  * Desired change in:

    * Behavior
    * Social norms
    * Policies
  * Intended effect of the change.
  * Timeframe required for change.

### SMART Communication Objectives

#### Specific

* States:

  * Who/what is the focus?
  * What type of change is intended?

#### Measurable

* Can the objective be measured?

#### Appropriate

* Is it sensitive to:

  * Audience needs?
  * Audience preferences?
  * Social norms?
  * Social expectations?

#### Realistic

* Can it actually be achieved with available:

  * Time?
  * Resources?

#### Time-bound

* Specifies the period within which the change should occur. 

## Task 2 — State How Much Change

Consider three major areas:

### Context of the Problem

* Identify:

  * Barriers
  * Facilitators
  * Incentives
* These can be used when developing messages to promote behavior.

### Prior Experiences

* Examine existing research/reports on previous communication programs.
* Ask:

  * What changes were achieved?
  * What changes are realistic?
  * What changes are feasible?

### Resources and Timeframe

* Consider available resources.
* Consider what is manageable within the strategy timeframe.
* Ask:

  * Can objectives be achieved with available resources?
  * Are communication approaches sufficient to reach the intended audience?
  * Can services meet increased demand?

### Determine Amount of Change

* Specify:

  * Existing baseline measure.
  * Expected measure.
* Ask:

  * What numerical/percentage change is expected?
  * Is it measurable?
  * Is it realistic? 

## Task 3 — Determine Timeframe for Expected Change

* Process:

  * Facilitated discussion.
* Output:

  * Timeframe for objectives.
* Determine when the desired change should occur.
* This makes objectives **time-bound**.
* Timeframe may be expressed in:

  * Months
  * Years.
* Ask:

  * Is enough time available for effective change?
  * Is the timeframe realistic? 

# Strategic Approaches

## Advocacy

* Strategic approach used to influence:

  * Leadership
  * Policy
  * Enabling environments.

## Community-Based Media

* Uses locally established outlets to reach communities.
* Examples:

  * Local radio
  * Community newsletters/newspapers
  * Rallies
  * Public meetings
  * Folk dramas
  * Sporting events.

## Community Mobilization

* Capacity-building process through which community:

  * Individuals
  * Groups
  * Organizations
* Plan, implement and evaluate activities participatively and sustainably to improve their lives.
* Can occur:

  * Through community initiative.
  * Or through stimulation by others.

## Counseling

* One-to-one communication with a trusted/influential communicator such as:

  * Counselor
  * Health provider
* Counseling tools/job aids can support clients.

## Distance Learning

* Learning platform that does not require attendance at a particular location.
* Access can occur through:

  * Radio
  * Internet
* Interaction can occur through:

  * Letters
  * Telephone calls
  * SMS
  * Chat rooms
  * Internet sites.

## Information and Communication Technology (ICT)

* Extends communication reach through digital media.
* Examples:

  * Websites
  * Email
  * Internet news feeds
  * Chat rooms
  * Virtual learning
  * E-learning
  * E-toolkits
  * Message boards
* Digital media can:

  * Deliver highly tailored messages.
  * Receive feedback.
  * Encourage real-time conversations.
* Social media is a subset of digital media.
* Examples:

  * Facebook
  * Twitter
  * LinkedIn
  * Blogs. 

## Interpersonal Communication / Peer Communication

* Can include:

  * Parent-child communication
  * Peer-to-peer communication
  * Communication with community leaders
  * Communication with religious leaders.

## Mass Media

* Reaches large audiences cost-effectively.
* Examples:

  * Radio
  * Television
  * Newspapers
* Because mass media can reach thousands of people:

  * Even a small-to-moderate effect across a large population can have substantial public-health impact.

## Social Mobilization

* Brings together:

  * Organizations
  * Policymakers
  * Networks
  * Communities
* Aims to:

  * Raise awareness.
  * Empower individuals/groups.
  * Promote action.
  * Create an enabling environment.
  * Produce positive behavior/social change.

## Support Media / Mid-Media

* Has less reach than mass media.
* Examples:

  * Posters
  * Brochures
  * Billboards. 

# Step 5 — Positioning and Strategy Outline

## Positioning

* **Positioning** means presenting a:

  * Challenge
  * Service
  * Product
* so that it:

  * Stands out from comparable/competing alternatives.
  * Is appealing.
  * Is persuasive.
* Positioning creates a:

  * Distinctive image.
  * Attractive image.
  * Lasting place in the minds of the intended audience.

## Key Promise

* The **Key Promise** is the main benefit associated with the proposed change.
* People make changes in:

  * Behavior
  * Policies
  * Social norms
* because they perceive a benefit.
* The perceived benefit should outweigh the personal cost of making the change.

## Key Message Points

* Key message points identify the **core information** that will be communicated across:

  * Messages
  * Activities
* Message design applies across all strategic approaches.
