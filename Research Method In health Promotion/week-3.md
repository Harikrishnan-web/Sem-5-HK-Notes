### PRECEDE-PROCEED Model: Overview

* The **PRECEDE-PROCEED Model** has been a cornerstone of health promotion practice for more than three decades.
* It helps guide the process of:

  * Designing health behavior change programs
  * Implementing health behavior change programs
  * Evaluating health behavior change programs
* It is considered one of the **most popular and well-respected models** in the field of health promotion program planning.
* **PRECEDE** stands for:

  * **Predisposing**
  * **Reinforcing**
  * **Enabling**
  * **Causes**
  * **In**
  * **Educational**
  * **Diagnosis**
  * **Evaluation**
* **PROCEED** stands for:

  * **Policy**
  * **Regulatory**
  * **Organizational**
  * **Constructs**
  * **In**
  * **Educational**
  * **Environmental**
  * **Development** 

### Development and purpose of the model

* When PRECEDE was developed in the **1970s**, it influenced the health education field toward an **outcome-focused approach to planning**.
* Instead of immediately applying solutions to health problems, the model promotes an **in-depth understanding** of:

  * The community
  * The population
  * The target audience
  * Their needs
  * The proximal determinants of health and quality-of-life problems
  * More distant contextual causes
* The model therefore encourages planners to understand the problem and its causes before deciding on solutions.
* Its use leads to interventions that are specifically targeted toward:

  * Desired outcomes
  * Identified causes
* It also provides a structure for systematically applying:

  * Theories
  * Concepts 

### Social assessment

* In the first phase, **social assessment, participatory planning, and situation analysis**, planners try to understand the community in which they are working.
* Intervention planners often use **qualitative methods** during this phase.
* These methods are used to collect:

  * Information
  * Opinions
  * Views from community members
* This phase is also important for ensuring **community participation**.
* Community members can include:

  * Potential intervention implementers
  * Potential participants
* Community participation helps ensure that:

  * The project addresses issues that are important to community members.
  * Project findings are locally relevant.
  * Participating communities develop capacity in intervention development and research.
* Assessment is carried out in both:

  * **Objective terms**
  * **Subjective terms**
* The assessment focuses on high-priority problems for the common good.
* These problems can be defined for a population using:

  * Economic indicators
  * Social indicators
* Individuals can also define problems in terms of their **quality of life**.
* Measurable social factors that can be obtained include:

  * Unemployment rates
  * Poverty
  * Crime
  * Population density 

### Epidemiological assessment

* In the second phase, **epidemiological assessment**, the planner begins building a **logic model of the health problem**.
* The logic model is usually developed from **right to left**.
* Planning generally begins with descriptions of:

  * Health problems
  * Related quality-of-life issues in the community
* Once completed, the model is read from **left to right** as a causal model explaining health and quality-of-life problems.
* Epidemiological analysis includes:

  * Health problems
  * Their related quality-of-life impact
  * Behavioral causes of health problems
  * Environmental causes of health problems or risk behaviors
* The purpose is to identify the:

  * Extent of the health problem
  * Distribution of the health problem
  * Causes of the health problem
* This assessment is conducted within a **defined population**. 

### Example of epidemiological assessment

* Suppose **premature mortality and morbidity from health care-associated infections** are identified as the health problem.
* The related quality-of-life issues can include:

  * Loss of productive years
  * Increased health care costs
* These consequences affect:

  * Individuals
  * Society
* The behavioral analysis examines what the **at-risk group does** that increases the risk of experiencing the health problem. 

### Secondary and tertiary prevention

* In the case of **secondary and tertiary prevention**, the analysis investigates what individuals do that increases their risk of:

  * Disability
  * Death
* This applies to individuals who already have a health problem.
* Example: **Diabetes**

  * Lack of adherence to prescribed dietary guidelines
  * Lack of adherence to physical activity guidelines
  * Lack of adherence to medication guidelines
* These behaviors can act as major barriers to proper diabetes treatment.
* They can contribute to increased disease burden, including:

  * Morbidity
  * Mortality 

### Environmental analysis

* Environmental analysis examines conditions in the:

  * Social environment
  * Physical environment
  * Biological environment
* These environmental conditions can influence health problems:

  * Directly
  * Indirectly through behavioral causes
* The environment often has a significant and **modifiable role** in causing health problems.
* Example of a **direct environmental influence**:

  * Exposure to lead-based paint
  * Exposure to lead-contaminated dust
  * These can contribute to lead poisoning.
* Example of an **indirect environmental influence through behavior**:

  * Lack of smoking bans in the workplace
* Behavioral indicators include:

  * Consumption patterns
  * Preventive actions
  * Self-care
  * Use
* Environmental indicators include:

  * Medical services
  * Economic gauges
  * Community gauges 

### Ecological and educational assessment

* In the third phase, **ecological and educational assessment**, health professionals explore the factors that produce the behavioral and environmental conditions identified in the previous phase.
* The important question is:

  * **What antecedent and reinforcing factors encourage the risk behaviors or environmental conditions that cause or contribute to the health problem?**
* Green and Kreuter describe these factors as:

  * Predisposing factors
  * Reinforcing factors
  * Enabling factors 

### Predisposing factors

* Predisposing factors are related to a person or population's:

  * Knowledge
  * Attitudes
  * Beliefs
  * Values
  * Perceptions
* They can facilitate or hinder **motivation for change**.
* Predisposing factors occur at the **cognitive level**.
* Examples include:

  * Knowledge
  * Self-efficacy
  * Attitudes
  * Skills
  * Beliefs
* These factors occur **before the behavior** and provide motivation for the behavior. 

### Enabling factors

* Enabling factors help individuals **act on their motivation** to change behavior.
* They include:

  * Skills
  * Resources
  * Barriers
* They can help or hinder:

  * Desired behavioral changes
  * Environmental changes
* Examples include:

  * Availability of walking paths
  * Skills required to cook healthy foods
  * Community resources
  * Laws 

### Reinforcing factors

* Reinforcing factors are the **rewards or incentives** that encourage a person to repeat a behavior.
* They include:

  * Rewards received after adopting a behavior
  * Feedback received from others following adoption of a behavior
* They function as continuing rewards or incentives that support repetition and maintenance of the behavior. 

### Administrative and policy assessment and intervention alignment

* In the fourth phase, **administrative and policy assessment and intervention alignment**, the main question is:

  * What program components and interventions are needed to produce the changes identified in the previous phases?
* Another important question is:

  * Does the program have the necessary organizational, policy, administrative capability, and resources to develop and implement the program?
* Organizational and administrative opportunities may include:

  * Local community sentiment
  * Organizational support
  * Financial support
  * Available staff to support the program
* Policy assessment involves identifying existing:

  * Policies
  * Regulations
* These policies and regulations may:

  * Support the proposed behavior changes
  * Discourage the proposed behavior changes 

### Implementation phase

* Moving into the final **PROCEED phases**, the planner prepares for implementation.
* The implementation preparation includes developing:

  * Necessary training
  * Materials
  * Resources
* These are required to support program delivery.
* Data collected during the first four phases are used to guide:

  * Specific in-service content
  * Skills development
* This ensures that program facilitators have:

  * The capacity required to implement the program
  * The necessary resources to implement the proposed program 

### Evaluation phases

* The later PROCEED phases involve developing data collection plans for:

  * Process evaluation
  * Impact evaluation
  * Outcome evaluation
* **Process evaluation** determines the extent to which the program was implemented according to the planned protocol.
* **Impact evaluation** assesses changes in:

  * Predisposing factors
  * Reinforcing factors
  * Enabling factors
  * Behavioral factors
  * Environmental factors
* **Outcome evaluation** determines the effect of the program on:

  * Health indicators
  * Quality-of-life indicators
* The measurable objectives written during each phase serve as **milestones** against which accomplishments can be evaluated. 

### Process evaluation

* Process evaluation is the assessment of the **program implementation**.
* It can include:

  * Number of individuals reached by the program
  * Feedback from participants about the program
* It essentially examines how well the program was delivered and whether implementation followed the intended approach. 

### Impact evaluation

* Impact evaluation assesses the **effects of the program**.
* It examines changes in:

  * Predisposing factors
  * Enabling factors
  * Reinforcing factors
  * Behavioral factors
  * Environmental factors 

### Outcome evaluation

* Outcome evaluation assesses the **effect of the program** on broader outcomes.
* It includes changes in:

  * Health
  * Social benefits
  * Quality of life 

### Importance of the PRECEDE-PROCEED Model

* The PRECEDE-PROCEED Model is considered:

  * One of the most popular models in health promotion program planning.
  * One of the most well-respected models in the field.
* It uses an **ecological approach** to program planning.
* Many consider it the **gold standard in health promotion planning**.
* This is largely because of the extensive assessments required **before program development begins**.
* The model:

  * Takes an ecological approach to planning.
  * Provides guidance for using theory to understand health problems.
  * Helps plan interventions that influence the determinants of health problems. 

### Core flow to remember

* **Social assessment**

  * Understand the community, its needs, quality of life, and important social problems.
* **Epidemiological assessment**

  * Identify the health problem, its extent, distribution, quality-of-life impact, behavioral causes, and environmental causes.
* **Ecological and educational assessment**

  * Identify predisposing, enabling, and reinforcing factors behind behaviors and environmental conditions.
* **Administrative and policy assessment**

  * Determine whether the required organizational, administrative, financial, staff, policy, and regulatory support exists.
* **Implementation**

  * Prepare training, materials, resources, and facilitator capacity.
* **Process evaluation**

  * Check whether the program was implemented as planned.
* **Impact evaluation**

  * Check changes in predisposing, enabling, reinforcing, behavioral, and environmental factors.
* **Outcome evaluation**

  * Check the final effects on health, social benefits, and quality of life.

## Theory

* A **theory** is a set of:

  * Interrelated concepts
  * Definitions
  * Propositions
* These collectively present a **systematic view of events or situations**.
* A theory specifies the **relationships among variables**.
* The purpose is to:

  * Explain events or situations.
  * Understand why events or situations occur.
  * Predict events or situations.
* A theory can contain several ideas and concepts, particularly when working in health research or public health research.
* The important feature is that these ideas and concepts are organized into a **systematic framework** rather than existing as unrelated ideas.
* The relationships between different variables help researchers understand and predict particular events or situations. 

## Usefulness of Theories in Health Promotion Research

* Theories are useful during different stages of an intervention:

  * **Planning**
  * **Implementation**
  * **Evaluation**
* Once a particular theory is selected for an intervention, that theory should remain in consideration during:

  * Planning the intervention
  * Implementing the intervention
  * Evaluating the intervention
* Program planners and health promotion experts can use theories to guide the search for answers to:

  * **Why?**
  * **What?**
  * **How?**

### Why?

* Theories can help identify **why people are not following public health and medical advice**.
* They can help explain why people may not care for themselves in healthy ways.
* For example, during the COVID-19 pandemic, some people:

  * Did not use masks.
  * Did not sanitize their hands.
  * Did not practice proper hand washing.
  * Did not follow recommended preventive measures.
* Theories can help researchers understand why certain people or categories of people do not follow preventive measures or public health advice.
* They can also help understand why people fail to maintain positive health behaviors.
* Performing a healthy behavior once is not enough; the behavior needs to be **maintained consistently**. 

### What?

* Theories help identify **what one needs to know before developing and organizing an intervention program**.
* A health promotion planner or expert needs to understand what information is necessary before:

  * Developing an intervention
  * Designing an intervention
  * Implementing an intervention
  * Organizing an intervention program
* Theory provides guidance regarding what needs to be understood before beginning the intervention.

### How?

* Theories provide insight into **how to shape program strategies**.
* They help determine how strategies can be developed to:

  * Reach people
  * Reach communities
  * Reach organizations
  * Make a positive impact on them
* The purpose of an intervention is not simply to implement an activity.
* The intervention should ultimately produce a **positive impact and positive outcome**.

### Role of Theory in Evaluation

* Theories can help identify:

  * What should be monitored.
  * What should be measured.
  * What should be compared.
* These aspects become particularly important during **program evaluation**.
* The different constructs within a theory can guide researchers in deciding what needs to be assessed during evaluation. 

## Explanatory Theories

* **Explanatory theories** are often called the **theory of the problem**.
* They help:

  * Describe why a problem exists.
  * Identify why a problem exists.
  * Explain why a particular health or social issue exists in a community.
* They also help **predict behaviors under defined conditions**.
* In other words, under a particular situation or set of circumstances, explanatory theories can help predict whether people are likely to follow a particular behavior.

### Modifiable Factors

* Explanatory theories guide the search for **modifiable factors**.
* Modifiable factors are factors that can potentially be changed or influenced.
* Examples include:

  * Knowledge
  * Awareness
  * Attitudes
  * Self-efficacy
  * Social support
  * Resources or lack of resources
* Identifying these factors is important because interventions can then be designed to modify or address them. 

## Change Theories / Theories of Action

* **Change theories**, also called **theories of action**, guide the development of interventions.
* They help researchers determine how an intervention can be developed to produce a desired change.
* Change theories also form the **basis for evaluation**.
* They encourage the evaluator to make explicit their assumptions about:

  * How a program should work.
  * Why the program should produce its expected outcome.
  * How the different components of the program are expected to lead to change.
* Therefore, theories of action are useful both for:

  * Developing interventions.
  * Evaluating interventions. 

## Implementation Theories

* **Implementation theories** are change theories that specifically connect theory to:

  * A given problem
  * A particular audience
  * A particular context
* Therefore, implementation theories provide a more specific connection between theoretical concepts and the practical implementation of an intervention. 

## Concepts, Constructs and Variables

### Concepts

* **Concepts are the major components of a theory.**
* They are the:

  * Building blocks of a theory.
  * Primary elements of a theory.
* Concepts represent basic ideas from which a theory is developed.
* Different ideas and concepts can be generated while working or discussing health research and public health research.
* Concepts can differ in the extent to which:

  * They have meaning outside a particular theory.
  * They can be understood independently of the context of a specific theory.
* Therefore, the meaning of a concept may depend partly on the theoretical context in which it is being used. 

### Constructs

* When concepts are **developed or adapted for use in a particular theory**, they are called **constructs**.
* A construct is therefore a concept that has been incorporated into a particular theoretical framework.
* Constructs are important components of specific theories or models.

### Example: Perceived Susceptibility

* The **Health Belief Model** contains the construct **perceived susceptibility**.
* The underlying concept is a person's belief regarding whether they are:

  * Vulnerable to a particular disease.
  * Susceptible to a particular disease.
* The general idea is whether a person believes that a particular disease can affect them.
* When this concept is developed and used within the Health Belief Model, it becomes the construct **perceived susceptibility**.

### Example: Subjective Norms

* The **Theory of Planned Behavior** contains the construct **subjective norms**.
* This demonstrates how concepts can become constructs when they are adopted and developed within a particular theory. 

### Variables

* **Variables are the empirical counterparts or operational forms of constructs.**
* Once a construct has been identified within a theory or model, it needs to be converted into something that can actually be **measured**.
* Variables specify **how a construct is to be measured in a specific situation**.
* Variables therefore provide the measurable or operational form of a theoretical construct.
* Variables should be properly **matched to constructs** when deciding what needs to be assessed during the evaluation of a theory-driven program.
* A single construct can have **more than one variable**.
* The ultimate purpose is to ensure that the theoretical constructs can be assessed and measured during evaluation.
* For example, a specific health problem such as **cervical cancer screening** can be studied using constructs from the Health Belief Model, with appropriate measurable variables used to assess those constructs. 

### Relationship Between Concepts, Constructs and Variables

* **Concept → Construct → Variable**
* A concept is a basic idea.
* When that concept is developed or adopted for use within a particular theory, it becomes a construct.
* The construct is then operationalized into measurable variables.
* The variable allows the researcher to actually assess the construct in a specific situation.

## Models

* Health behavior is highly complex.
* The concepts that guide and influence health behavior are also very complex.
* Therefore, health behavior is generally **too complex to be explained by a single, unified theory**.
* More than one theory may be required to understand a particular health behavior or problem.
* **Models draw on a number of theories** to help understand:

  * A specific problem
  * A particular setting
  * A particular context
* Models are often informed by:

  * More than one theory.
  * Empirical findings.
* Therefore, models provide a broader framework for understanding a specific problem by bringing together relevant theoretical explanations and empirical evidence. 

## Behavior Change Techniques

* **Behavior Change Techniques (BCTs)** are methods used by:

  * Behavioral scientists
  * Psychologists
  * Medical personnel
  * Health professionals
  * Other professionals
* These methods are used to try to:

  * Change the behavior of individuals.
  * Change the behavior of groups.
  * Increase motivation.
  * Address other factors or constructs that influence behavior.
* Behavior change techniques therefore focus not only on the behavior itself but also on the **motivations, determinants, factors and constructs that influence that behavior**.
* According to **Abraham and Michie (2008)**, there are **26 common methods** that can be used to try to change the behavior of other people. 

## Providing Information

### Provide Information on the Behavior–Health Link

* The first step in behavior change can be providing the individual with appropriate information.
* The person needs to understand the relationship between a particular behavior and health.
* Information can explain:

  * The health problem.
  * The health risk.
  * How a particular behavior affects health.

### Provide Information on Consequences

* Individuals should understand the consequences of performing or not performing a particular behavior.
* Explain:

  * What happens if the person performs the desired behavior.
  * What happens if the person does not perform the desired behavior.
* Consequences can be:

  * Positive.
  * Negative.
  * Beneficial.
  * Harmful.
* Understanding consequences can help individuals recognize why behavior change is important.

### Provide Information About Others' Approval

* Individuals can be influenced by whether people around them approve or disapprove of a particular behavior.
* Information can therefore be provided about:

  * What other people think.
  * Whether the surrounding people approve of the behavior.
  * Whether the surrounding people disapprove of the behavior.
* This relates to **social influence** and the influence of people around the individual. 

## Prompt Intention Formation

* Individuals should be encouraged to form an **intention to perform the desired behavior**.
* A health promotion expert or planner should encourage the person in such a way that they develop a clear intention to perform the behavior.
* Having the intention to perform a behavior is an important part of encouraging behavior change. 

## Prompt Barrier Identification

* Individuals or groups may face different **barriers** to behavior change.
* Providing information and motivation alone may not be sufficient if significant barriers remain.
* Therefore, identifying barriers is an important behavior change technique.
* Barriers can make it difficult or impossible for a person to perform the desired action.
* The health promotion expert should identify these barriers and consider how they can be addressed.

## Provide General Encouragement

* General encouragement involves continuously encouraging the individual.
* Encouraging words can include:

  * “Well done.”
  * “You have done this.”
  * “You can do this.”
* Encouragement can help individuals remain motivated.
* Health promotion experts should provide appropriate encouragement to their target audience. 

## Set Graded Tasks

* A desired behavior can be divided into a **series of tasks**.
* Instead of expecting the person to perform everything at once:

  * Break the behavior into manageable steps.
  * Establish a sequence of tasks.
  * Gradually progress from one task to the next.
* The individual should understand what needs to be done at each stage. 

## Provide Instruction

* Individuals need to know:

  * **Why** they should perform the behavior.
  * **How** they should perform the behavior.
* Instruction is therefore essential when the individual does not know how to perform the desired behavior.
* **Hand washing example:**

  * If the purpose is to promote hand washing, simply explaining that hand washing is important may not be enough.
  * The individual should also be taught the correct hand-washing technique.
  * The steps need to be explained clearly.
* Instruction therefore provides the practical knowledge required to perform the behavior. 

## Model or Demonstrate Behavior

* The health promotion expert should demonstrate the desired behavior.
* Demonstration makes it easier for individuals or groups to understand what they are expected to do.
* **Hand washing example:**

  * Demonstrate the correct hand-washing steps.
  * Individuals can observe the behavior and then practice it themselves.
* Demonstration is particularly useful when the behavior involves a specific technique or sequence of actions. 

## Specific Goal Setting

* Goals should be **specific** rather than broad or vague.
* Vague goals can make individuals lose interest or become uncertain about what they are supposed to do.
* Specific goals should clearly identify:

  * What needs to be done.
  * What behavior is expected.
  * What the individual is trying to achieve.
* Clear and specific goals make behavior change easier to understand and maintain. 

## Review of Behavioral Goals

* Previously established behavioral goals should be reviewed.
* The individual should look at:

  * What the original goals were.
  * What has actually been accomplished.
  * Which behaviors have been performed.
  * Which goals have not yet been achieved.
* Reviewing goals helps the individual assess progress and continue working toward behavior change. 

## Self-Monitoring

* **Self-monitoring** is an important behavior change technique.
* It involves the individual monitoring and recording their own behavior.
* It can be difficult, but it can be implemented effectively.
* **Diet example:**

  * An individual trying to manage obesity can maintain a diary.
  * They can record what food they eat throughout the day.
  * They can record the quantity or calorie intake.
* **Physical activity example:**

  * Record physical activity performed in the morning.
  * Record physical activity performed in the evening.
  * Record activities such as brisk walking.
* Self-monitoring allows individuals to become more aware of their own behaviors and patterns. 

## Feedback

* Feedback is provided by comparing what was planned with what was actually done.
* For example:

  * A person planned to perform a particular behavior in the coming days.
  * The health promotion expert follows up to determine whether the person actually performed it.
* If the behavior was not performed, the individual can be asked why it was not done.
* Feedback helps the individual understand their progress and make adjustments.
* It can encourage the person to continue moving forward with the desired behavior. 

## Contingent Rewards

* Sometimes rewards or incentives can be provided when a desired behavior is performed.
* Rewards may be:

  * Monetary.
  * Non-monetary.
* The reward is **contingent on performing the desired behavior**.
* The basic principle is:

  * If you perform the desired behavior → you receive the specified reward.
* Rewards should be used appropriately depending on the intervention and target population. 

## Teach to Use Prompts and Cues

* People may forget to perform a desired behavior.
* **Prompts and cues** can remind individuals to perform the behavior.
* Examples include:

  * Charts.
  * Posters.
  * Written reminders.
  * Time-based reminders.
* **Jogging example:**

  * If the goal is to wake up at 6 AM and go jogging, a reminder can be placed somewhere visible.
* Prompts and cues help prevent individuals from forgetting the desired behavior and encourage repeated performance. 

## Agree Behavioral Contract

* A **behavioral contract** involves an agreement regarding the behaviors that the individual intends to perform.
* It may be:

  * Verbal.
  * Written.
* Written behavioral contracts are commonly described in the literature.
* The individual or group agrees to perform specified behaviors.
* A health promotion expert may establish such an agreement with the target audience.
* The purpose is to create commitment toward the desired behavior. 

## Prompt Practice

* Practice should be actively encouraged.
* The individual should be encouraged to intentionally practice the desired behavior.
* Practice helps transform instructions and knowledge into actual behavior.
* For behavior change, simply knowing what to do is not enough; the person should also be encouraged to practice it.

## Use of Follow-Up Prompt

* Follow-up is important after the initial intervention.
* Health promotion experts can:

  * Call individuals.
  * Send emails.
  * Contact them digitally.
  * Meet them physically.
  * Discuss their progress.
* Follow-up helps determine whether the individual is continuing to perform the behavior.
* It also provides an opportunity to identify problems if the desired behavior is not being maintained. 

## Provide Opportunity for Social Comparison

* Social comparison involves comparing one's behavior or progress with that of other people.
* It is particularly relevant in **groups**.
* For example, in a group of students:

  * One student may have achieved a particular goal.
  * Another student may not have achieved the same goal.
* Comparing progress can make individuals aware of their own performance.
* Social comparison can therefore provide motivation and encourage improvement. 

## Plan Social Support / Social Change

* Social support is important for maintaining behavior change.
* Support can come from:

  * Family.
  * Friends.
  * Other members of the community.
  * Other relevant social groups.
* The health promotion expert should consider how appropriate social support can be planned and provided.
* Social support can make it easier for an individual to adopt and maintain a desired behavior. 

## Prompt Identification as a Role Model / Position Advocate

* An individual can be identified as a **role model** for others.
* This can be particularly useful among school children or groups.
* **Example:**

  * One student can be selected as a role model for other students for a particular day.
  * The student can demonstrate correct hand-washing techniques.
  * The student can talk about the importance of hand washing.
  * The student can motivate others through persuasion and demonstration.
* The role-model approach allows an individual to demonstrate the desired behavior to others. 

## Prompt Self-Talk

* Self-talk is a form of **self-encouragement**.
* It does not necessarily mean speaking loudly.
* It involves mentally or verbally encouraging oneself before performing a desired behavior.
* **Example:**

  * Before starting the day, a person can tell themselves that they will go jogging for an hour.
* Self-talk can help strengthen motivation and commitment toward the desired behavior.
* Individuals can be encouraged to talk positively to themselves before starting a behavior activity. 

## Relapse Prevention

* **Relapse prevention** focuses on preventing an individual from returning to an undesired behavior or abandoning a newly adopted healthy behavior.
* A common problem is that people may initially start a behavior but fail to maintain it.
* **Example:**

  * A person begins physical activity.
  * After a few days, they stop doing it.
* The health promotion expert should identify:

  * Problems that interfere with maintenance.
  * Factors that cause the individual to stop.
  * Barriers to continuing the behavior.
  * Facilitating conditions that can support continued behavior.
* The aim is to prevent relapse and support **maintenance of behavior change**. 

## Stress Management

* Stress can interfere with behavior change.
* If individuals become stressed while trying to perform a desired behavior, they may find it difficult to continue.
* Stress management therefore needs to be considered during behavior change interventions.
* Individuals should be provided with appropriate ways to:

  * Relax.
  * Reduce stress.
  * Reduce anxiety.
* Stress-reduction strategies can help individuals continue with the desired behavior. 

## Motivational Interviewing

* **Motivational interviewing** is an important behavior change technique.
* Its main purpose is to provide motivation to the individual.
* Motivational interviewing can be considered a form of **counselling**.
* Important principles include:

  * Empathy.
  * Respect.
  * Support.
  * Understanding the person's views.
  * Understanding the person's problems.
* The health promotion expert should **not simply force the individual** to perform a behavior.
* Instead, the expert should understand the individual's perspective and support the person in moving toward behavior change.
* The approach involves helping the individual resolve uncertainty and move away from **ambivalence**.
* Ambivalence refers to a situation where the individual may have conflicting or inconsistent feelings about changing the behavior.
* The aim is to encourage a more definite commitment toward the desired behavior.
* Empathy is particularly important.
* Motivational interviewing has an important role in behavior change. 

## Factors to Consider When Selecting Behavior Change Techniques

* The choice of behavior change technique depends on:

  * **Target audience**
  * **Specific problem**
  * **Target behavior**
  * **Available resources**
  * **Feasibility of implementation**
* It is not enough to consider only the needs of the audience.
* The resources available to the researcher or health promotion expert are also important.
* The intervention should be practically feasible for the people who are responsible for implementing it.
* Different audiences and different problems may therefore require different combinations of behavior change techniques.

## Time Management

* Time management is another important behavior change consideration.
* Individuals already have their own:

  * Work schedules.
  * Daily routines.
  * Responsibilities.
  * Time commitments.
* Adding several new behaviors to an individual's daily routine may create difficulty in managing time.
* **Examples of desired behaviors:**

  * Getting up early and going jogging.
  * Brushing teeth before going to sleep.
  * Washing hands with soap and water before cooking.
* If too many activities are added without considering the individual's schedule, the person may:

  * Fail to manage their time.
  * Miss some activities.
  * Become stressed.
  * Experience anxiety.
* Health promotion experts should therefore consider how the desired behavior can be incorporated into the individual's existing daily schedule.
* Behavior change should be realistic and compatible with the individual's everyday routine.

## Theory

* A **theory** is a set of:

  * Interrelated concepts
  * Definitions
  * Propositions
* These present a **systematic view of events or situations**.
* A theory specifies relationships among different variables.
* The purpose of specifying these relationships is to:

  * Explain events or situations
  * Understand events or situations
  * Predict events or situations 

### Importance and Uses of Theories

* Theories are useful during different stages of an intervention:

  * **Planning**
  * **Implementation**
  * **Evaluation**
* Program planners and health promotion experts can use theories to guide the search for answers to:

  * **Why?**
  * **What?**
  * **How?** 

### Theory and the Question “Why?”

* Theories can help identify **why people are not following public health or medical advice**.
* They can also help explain why people may not care for themselves in healthy ways.
* Example:

  * During the COVID-19 pandemic, some people did not use masks.
  * Some did not sanitize their hands.
  * Some did not practice appropriate handwashing.
* Theories can help understand why certain groups or individuals fail to follow preventive measures or public health advice.
* They can help identify the reasons behind failure to maintain positive health behaviors. 

### Theory and the Question “What?”

* Theories help identify **what needs to be known before developing and organizing an intervention program**.
* Before planning an intervention, a health promotion planner needs to understand:

  * What information is required
  * What factors are influencing the problem
  * What needs to be considered before designing the intervention
  * What needs to be considered before implementing the intervention
  * What needs to be considered when organizing the intervention 

### Theory and the Question “How?”

* Theories provide insight into **how program strategies should be shaped**.
* They help health promotion experts develop strategies to:

  * Reach people
  * Reach communities
  * Reach organizations
  * Create an impact on the target population
* The goal is not simply to implement an intervention.
* The intervention should produce a **positive impact or positive outcome**. 

### Theory and Program Evaluation

* Theories can help identify:

  * What should be monitored
  * What should be measured
  * What should be compared
* These are important during **program evaluation**.
* The constructs within a theory can help determine what should ultimately be assessed during evaluation. 

## Types of Theories

### Explanatory Theories — Theory of the Problem

* Explanatory theories are often called the **“theory of the problem.”**
* They help:

  * Describe a problem
  * Identify why a problem exists
  * Explain why a particular health or social issue exists in a community
* They also help **predict behavior under defined conditions**.
* They guide the search for **modifiable factors**.

### Modifiable Factors

Examples of modifiable factors include:

* Knowledge
* Attitudes
* Self-efficacy
* Social support
* Lack of resources
* Other factors that can potentially be changed through an intervention 

### Change Theories — Theories of Action

* Change theories are also called **theories of action**.
* They guide the **development of interventions**.
* They help determine how an intervention can be designed to produce a desired behavioral change.
* They also form the **basis for evaluation**.
* They encourage the evaluator to make their assumptions explicit regarding:

  * How the program is expected to work
  * Why the program should produce a particular outcome
  * What processes are expected to lead to change 

### Implementation Theories

* Implementation theories are **change theories that link theory specifically to**:

  * A given problem
  * A particular audience
  * A specific context
* Therefore, implementation theory connects theoretical concepts with the practical situation in which an intervention is being implemented. 

## Concepts, Constructs and Variables

### Concepts

* Concepts are the **major components of a theory**.
* They are the:

  * Building blocks of a theory
  * Primary elements of a theory
* Different ideas generated during health research or public health research can form concepts.
* Concepts may differ in how much meaning they have outside the context of a particular theory.
* A concept may have a particular meaning within one theoretical context and may have a different level of meaning or understanding outside that context. 

### Constructs

* When concepts are **developed or adopted for use within a particular theory**, they are called **constructs**.
* Constructs are therefore theory-specific forms of concepts.

### Example — Perceived Susceptibility

* Consider the concept that a person believes they may be vulnerable to a particular disease.
* The person may believe:

  * “I am vulnerable to this disease.”
  * “I may be susceptible to this disease.”
* When this concept is incorporated into a particular theory such as the **Health Belief Model**, it becomes the construct **perceived susceptibility**.
* Thus:

  * **Concept:** Belief regarding vulnerability to a disease
  * **Construct:** Perceived susceptibility

### Other Examples of Constructs

* In the **Health Belief Model**:

  * Perceived susceptibility is a construct.
* In the **Theory of Planned Behavior**:

  * Subjective norms are a construct.
* Different concepts are adapted or developed according to the specific theory in which they are being used. 

### Variables

* Variables are the **empirical counterparts or operational forms of constructs**.
* A construct is theoretical, but ultimately it needs to be measured.
* Variables specify **how a construct will be measured in a specific situation**.
* Variables make the theoretical construct measurable in research.
* Variables should be appropriately matched to constructs when deciding what should be assessed in the evaluation of a **theory-driven program**. 

### Relationship Between Concepts, Constructs and Variables

* **Concept → Construct → Variable**
* A concept is a basic idea.
* When the concept is developed or adopted for a specific theory, it becomes a construct.
* When the construct is converted into an empirical and measurable form, it becomes a variable.
* A **single construct can have more than one variable**.
* Variables are therefore important when the researcher needs to actually measure the theoretical constructs during evaluation.

## Models

* Health behavior is highly complex.
* The concepts that influence health behavior are also complex.
* Therefore, health behavior is generally **too complex to be explained by a single, unified theory**.
* Models draw upon **multiple theories** to understand a specific problem.
* A model may be used for:

  * A specific problem
  * A particular setting
  * A particular context
* Models are often informed by:

  * More than one theory
  * Empirical findings 

### Key Difference to Remember

* **Theory:** Provides a systematic explanation of relationships among concepts/variables.
* **Model:** May draw upon several theories and empirical findings to understand a specific problem in a particular context.

## Behavior Change Techniques

* **Behavior Change Techniques (BCTs)** are methods used by:

  * Behavioral scientists
  * Psychologists
  * Medical personnel
  * Health professionals
  * Other professionals
* Their purpose is to try to:

  * Change the behavior of individuals
  * Change the behavior of groups
  * Change motivation
  * Influence other factors or constructs that affect behavior 
* According to **Abraham and Michie (2008)**, there are **26 common methods** that can be used to attempt to change the behavior of other people. 

## Providing Information

### Provide Information on the Behavior–Health Link

* First, the individual needs to understand the relationship between:

  * A particular behavior
  * Its effect on health
* Information should explain the health problems or health risks associated with the behavior.
* The target person should understand why the behavior is important from a health perspective.

### Provide Information on Consequences

* Explain what may happen if the person:

  * Performs the desired behavior
  * Does not perform the desired behavior
* Explain both:

  * Positive consequences
  * Negative consequences
* The person should understand the possible benefits and harms associated with the behavior.

### Provide Information About Others’ Approval

* Explain whether people around the individual:

  * Approve of the behavior
  * Disapprove of the behavior
* This relates to **social influence**.
* People may be influenced by:

  * Family
  * Friends
  * Community members
  * Other important people in their surroundings

## Prompt Intention Formation

* Encourage individuals to develop an **intention to perform the desired behavior**.
* The health promotion expert should encourage the person in a way that creates a clear intention to carry out the behavior.
* Intention is important because the person needs to decide and be willing to perform the behavior.

## Prompt Barrier Identification

* Individuals or groups may face barriers that prevent them from performing a desired behavior.
* Giving information and motivation alone may not be sufficient if significant barriers remain.
* Therefore, the health promotion expert should identify:

  * What barriers exist
  * Why the person cannot perform the behavior
  * What can be done to overcome those barriers
* Barrier identification is important because barriers can prevent behavioral action even when motivation is present.

## Provide General Encouragement

* Individuals should receive regular encouragement.
* Encouragement may include statements such as:

  * “Well done.”
  * “You have done this.”
  * “You can do this.”
* Encouraging words can help maintain motivation.
* Health promotion experts should continue to provide positive encouragement to the target audience.

## Set Graded Tasks

* A large behavioral goal can be divided into a **series of smaller tasks**.
* Tasks can be arranged progressively.
* Individuals can then complete:

  * One task
  * Followed by the next task
  * Followed by subsequent tasks
* Graded tasks make a behavior easier to approach and perform.

## Provide Instruction

* Individuals need to know:

  * **Why** they should perform a behavior
  * **How** they should perform it
* Instructions should explain the practical steps involved.

### Example — Handwashing

* If the target behavior is proper handwashing:

  * Explain why handwashing is important.
  * Explain how handwashing should be performed.
  * Provide the correct handwashing steps.
* Simply telling someone to wash their hands is not enough if they do not know the correct technique.

## Model or Demonstrate Behavior

* Demonstration helps individuals understand how a behavior should actually be performed.
* Example:

  * Demonstrate the steps of proper handwashing.
* Demonstration can make the behavior easier for individuals or groups to understand and reproduce.

## Specific Goal Setting

* Goals should be **specific** rather than broad or vague.
* Vague goals can reduce interest and make it difficult for people to understand exactly what they are expected to do.
* Specific goals clearly identify the actions that should be performed.
* The individual should understand exactly what needs to be achieved.

## Review of Behavioral Goals

* Previously established behavioral goals should be reviewed.
* The individual should examine:

  * What the original goals were
  * What has already been accomplished
  * What has not yet been accomplished
* Reviewing goals helps determine progress and identify areas that require further attention.

## Self-Monitoring

* Self-monitoring involves the individual **observing and recording their own behavior**.
* It can be difficult, but it is an important behavior change technique.
* Individuals can maintain records or diaries of their behavior.

### Example — Food Intake

* A person trying to manage obesity can record:

  * What food they consume
  * The amount of food
  * Calories consumed

### Example — Physical Activity

* A person can record:

  * Morning physical activity
  * Duration of exercise
  * Evening physical activity
  * Walking or brisk walking
* Self-monitoring helps individuals become more aware of their own behavior.

## Feedback

* Feedback involves reviewing what was planned versus what was actually done.
* Example:

  * A person planned to perform a particular behavior during the coming days.
  * The person then needs to review whether the behavior was actually performed.
* If the planned behavior was not completed, the reason should be explored.
* Feedback helps the individual understand progress and continue working toward behavioral change.

## Contingent Rewards

* Rewards can sometimes be used to encourage a desired behavior.
* Rewards may be:

  * Monetary
  * Non-monetary
* The reward is provided when the desired behavior is performed.
* Basic principle:

  * **If you perform the desired behavior → you receive the specified reward/incentive.**
* Rewards are not necessarily appropriate in every situation.

## Teach the Use of Prompts and Cues

* People may forget to perform a desired behavior.
* Prompts and cues can serve as reminders.
* Examples:

  * Posters
  * Charts
  * Written reminders
  * Notes
  * Time-based reminders
* Example:

  * A person wants to wake up at 6 AM and go jogging.
  * A reminder or written cue can help them remember the planned behavior.
* Prompts and cues help individuals maintain the behavior by reducing forgetfulness.

## Agree on a Behavioral Contract

* A behavioral contract involves an agreement regarding the behaviors that an individual intends to perform.
* It may be:

  * Verbal
  * Written
* Written behavioral contracts are commonly used in research and intervention settings.
* The target individual or group can agree to perform specified behaviors regularly.
* The contract creates a clear commitment toward the desired behavior.

## Prompt Practice

* Practice should be actively encouraged.
* The individual should be prompted to repeatedly practice the desired behavior.
* Intentional practice can help establish and maintain behavioral change.
* Encouraging actual practice is important rather than merely providing information about the behavior.

## Use of Follow-Up Prompts

* Follow-up is important after an intervention has begun.
* Health promotion experts can:

  * Contact participants
  * Call them
  * Send emails
  * Follow up physically
  * Discuss their progress
* Follow-up helps determine:

  * Whether the behavior is being performed
  * Whether problems have occurred
  * Whether additional support is needed
* Digital technology can be used for follow-up, but physical interaction can also be used.

## Provide Opportunity for Social Comparison

* Individuals can compare their behavior or progress with other people.
* This is particularly useful in groups.
* Example:

  * A group may contain several students.
  * Each student has a behavioral goal.
  * Participants can compare what they planned to achieve with what they actually achieved.
* Social comparison can help individuals recognize:

  * Their progress
  * Areas where they are falling behind
  * Areas where improvement is required

## Plan Social Support and Social Change

* Social support is important for maintaining behavior change.
* Support can come from:

  * Family
  * Friends
  * Other members of the community
* Appropriate social support can help an individual maintain the desired behavior.
* Social support should therefore be considered while planning behavior change interventions.

## Prompt Identification as a Role Model or Position Advocate

* An individual can be assigned the role of a **role model** for others.
* This can be particularly useful among school children or groups.
* Example:

  * One student may be selected as the role model for the day.
  * The student can demonstrate proper handwashing.
  * The student can explain the importance of handwashing.
  * The student can motivate other students.
* The role model can influence others through:

  * Persuasion
  * Demonstration
  * Communication
  * Personal example

## Prompt Self-Talk

* Self-talk involves encouraging oneself internally.
* It does not necessarily mean speaking loudly.
* It involves mentally preparing and encouraging oneself before performing a behavior.
* Example:

  * Before exercising, a person may tell themselves that they will go jogging that morning.
* Self-talk can provide:

  * Self-encouragement
  * Motivation
  * Mental preparation for the desired behavior

## Relapse Prevention

* Relapse occurs when a person begins a desired behavior but later stops maintaining it.
* Example:

  * A person starts physical activity.
  * After a few days, the person stops exercising.
* Relapse prevention focuses on identifying:

  * Problems that interfere with maintaining behavior
  * Factors that cause the person to stop
  * Conditions that make maintenance difficult
* The health promotion expert should identify **facilitating conditions** that can help prevent relapse.
* The objective is to help the individual maintain behavioral change over time.

## Stress Management

* Stress can interfere with behavior change.
* Individuals may experience:

  * Stress
  * Anxiety
  * Pressure associated with performing the desired behavior
* Stress management techniques should help individuals relax.
* Reduction of anxiety is important for successful behavioral maintenance.
* Appropriate stress-management or “stress-buster” strategies can therefore support behavior change.

## Motivational Interviewing

* Motivational interviewing is an important technique for behavior change.
* Its main purpose is to **provide motivation to the target individual or population**.
* Motivational interviewing can be considered a form of **counseling**.

### Important Principles of Motivational Interviewing

* **Empathy** is important.
* **Support** should be provided.
* **Respect** for the individual is important.
* The health professional should understand:

  * The person's views
  * The person's problems
  * The person's concerns
* The person should not simply be forced to perform a particular behavior.
* Instead of saying:

  * “You have to do these things from tomorrow.”
* The professional should understand the person's perspective and motivate them toward change.

### Avoiding Ambivalence

* Ambivalence refers to having conflicting feelings or uncertainty about changing a behavior.
* Motivational interviewing aims to reduce or avoid this ambivalence.
* The individual should be encouraged toward a clearer commitment to the desired behavior.
* Motivation should be developed through:

  * Understanding
  * Empathy
  * Support
  * Respect
  * Appropriate counseling

### Importance of Motivational Interviewing

* Motivational interviewing has an important role in behavior change.
* The principles of counseling should be remembered when using motivational interviewing.
* It should be selected according to:

  * Target audience
  * Specific problem
  * Target behavior
  * Available resources
  * Feasibility of implementation

## Time Management

* Time management can affect the ability of an individual to maintain a new behavior.
* Individuals already have their own:

  * Work schedules
  * Daily routines
  * Responsibilities
  * Time commitments
* Adding new behaviors to an existing routine can create difficulties.
* Example:

  * Exercising every morning
  * Brushing teeth every night
  * Washing hands before cooking
* Individuals may feel that adding these behaviors makes their schedules difficult to manage.
* Poor time management can lead to:

  * Missed activities
  * Stress
  * Anxiety
  * Failure to maintain the desired behavior
* Health promotion experts should therefore consider the individual's daily schedule.
* Behavior change interventions should help individuals incorporate the desired behavior realistically into their daily routine.
