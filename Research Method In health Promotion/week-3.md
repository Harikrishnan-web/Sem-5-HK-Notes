## Overview of the PRECEDE-PROCEED Model

* The **PRECEDE-PROCEED Model** has been a cornerstone of health promotion practice for more than three decades.
* It helps guide the process of:

  * Designing health behavior change programs
  * Implementing health behavior change programs
  * Evaluating health behavior change programs
* It is considered one of the **most popular and well-respected models** in the field of health promotion program planning.
* **PRECEDE** stands for:

  * **P**redisposing
  * **R**einforcing
  * **E**nabling
  * **C**auses
  * **E**ducational
  * **D**iagnosis
  * **E**valuation
* **PROCEED** stands for:

  * **P**olicy
  * **R**egulatory
  * **A**nd
  * **O**rganizational
  * **C**onstructs
  * **I**n
  * **E**ducational
  * **A**nd
  * **E**nvironmental
  * **D**evelopment 

## Development and basic approach of the model

* When PRECEDE was developed in the **1970s**, it influenced the field of health education toward an **outcome-focused approach to planning**.
* The model does not encourage planners to immediately jump into applying solutions to health problems.
* Instead, it promotes an **in-depth understanding** of:

  * The community
  * The population
  * The target audience
  * Their needs
  * The immediate or proximal determinants of health problems
  * The determinants of quality-of-life problems
  * More distant contextual causes
* The model therefore encourages planners to understand **why a health problem exists before deciding what intervention should be applied**.
* Its use leads to interventions that are:

  * Specifically targeted toward desired outcomes
  * Specifically targeted toward identified causes
  * Systematically planned
* It also provides a structure for applying **theories and concepts systematically** during health promotion planning. 

## Phases of the PRECEDE-PROCEED Model

* The model follows a sequence of assessments and planning activities.
* The major areas covered include:

  * Social assessment
  * Epidemiological assessment
  * Ecological and educational assessment
  * Administrative and policy assessment
  * Intervention alignment and implementation
  * Process evaluation
  * Impact evaluation
  * Outcome evaluation 

## Social assessment, participatory planning and situation analysis

* The first phase focuses on understanding the **community in which the intervention will be conducted**.
* Planners try to understand the community's:

  * Needs
  * Problems
  * Priorities
  * Opinions
  * Quality-of-life concerns
* **Qualitative methods** are often used to collect information and opinions from community members.
* Community participation is an important component of this phase.
* Community members may include:

  * Potential intervention implementers
  * Potential intervention participants
  * Other relevant members of the community
* Community participation helps ensure that:

  * The project addresses issues that are actually important to community members.
  * The findings of the project are locally relevant.
  * The participating community develops capacity in intervention development.
  * The participating community develops capacity in research.
* Assessment is performed using both:

  * **Objective measures**
  * **Subjective measures**
* High-priority problems are assessed in terms of the **common good**.
* The common good can be defined for a population using:

  * Economic indicators
  * Social indicators
* Individuals may define problems in terms of their **quality of life**.
* Measurable social factors that can be assessed include:

  * Unemployment rates
  * Poverty
  * Crime
  * Population density 

## Epidemiological assessment

* The epidemiological assessment begins building a **logic model of the health problem**.
* The planner generally builds the model from **right to left**.
* The process usually begins with:

  * Health problems
  * Related quality-of-life problems in the community
* Once completed, the model can be read from **left to right as a causal model**.
* The epidemiological analysis includes:

  * Health problems
  * Quality-of-life impacts associated with those health problems
  * Behavioral causes of health problems
  * Environmental causes of health problems or risk behaviors
* The purpose is to identify:

  * The extent of a health problem
  * The distribution of the health problem
  * The causes of the health problem
* These factors are studied within a **defined population**.

### Example of epidemiological assessment

* Suppose the health problem is **premature mortality and morbidity caused by health care–associated infections**.
* The associated quality-of-life issues may include:

  * Loss of productive years
  * Increased health care costs
* The behavioral analysis examines what the at-risk group does that increases its risk of experiencing the health problem.
* Therefore, the planner does not only identify the disease or health problem but also examines the behaviors and environmental conditions contributing to it. 

## Behavioral and environmental analysis

* In **secondary and tertiary prevention**, the analysis examines what individuals do that increases their risk of:

  * Disability
  * Death
* This is particularly important when individuals already have a health problem.
* An example is **diabetes**:

  * Lack of adherence to prescribed dietary guidelines
  * Lack of adherence to physical activity recommendations
  * Lack of adherence to medication guidelines
* Poor adherence can act as a major barrier to proper diabetes treatment.
* It can contribute to:

  * Increased disease burden
  * Morbidity
  * Mortality

### Environmental analysis

* Environmental analysis examines conditions in:

  * Social environments
  * Physical environments
  * Biological environments
* These environmental conditions can influence health problems:

  * Directly
  * Indirectly through behavioral causes
* The environment often plays a significant and **modifiable role** in causing health problems.

### Examples

* **Direct environmental influence:**

  * Exposure to lead-based paint
  * Exposure to lead-contaminated dust
  * These can contribute directly to lead poisoning.
* **Indirect environmental influence through behavior:**

  * Lack of smoking bans in workplaces
  * This can influence smoking-related behavior and exposure.

### Indicators

* **Behavioral indicators** include:

  * Consumption patterns
  * Preventive actions
  * Self-care
  * Use
* **Environmental indicators** include:

  * Medical services
  * Economic measures
  * Community measures/gauges 

## Ecological and educational assessment

* This phase explores the factors responsible for producing the:

  * Behavioral conditions
  * Environmental conditions
    identified during the epidemiological assessment.

* The important question is:

  * **What antecedent and reinforcing factors encourage the risk behaviors or environmental conditions that cause or contribute to the health problem?**

* Green and Kreuter describe three important categories of factors:

  * Predisposing factors
  * Reinforcing factors
  * Enabling factors

### Predisposing factors

* Predisposing factors are related to a person's or population's:

  * Knowledge
  * Attitudes
  * Beliefs
  * Values
  * Perceptions
* They can either:

  * Facilitate motivation for change
  * Hinder motivation for change
* Predisposing factors occur mainly at the **cognitive level**.
* Examples include:

  * Knowledge
  * Self-efficacy
  * Attitudes
  * Skills
  * Beliefs
* These factors occur **before the behavior** and provide motivation for the behavior.

### Enabling factors

* Enabling factors help individuals **act on their motivation to change behavior**.
* They include:

  * Skills
  * Resources
  * Barriers
* They can either:

  * Help desired behavioral changes
  * Hinder desired behavioral changes
  * Help environmental changes
  * Hinder environmental changes
* Examples include:

  * Availability of walking paths
  * Skills to cook healthy foods
  * Community resources
  * Laws

### Reinforcing factors

* Reinforcing factors are the **continuing rewards or incentives** that encourage a person to repeat a behavior.
* They may include:

  * Rewards received after adopting a behavior
  * Feedback received from other people
* Reinforcement therefore helps maintain a desired behavior after it has been adopted. 

## Administrative and policy assessment and intervention alignment

* This phase asks:

  * **What program components and interventions are needed to produce the changes identified in the previous phases?**
  * **Does the program have the organizational, policy, administrative capability and resources required to develop and implement the program?**
* The planner must determine whether the organization and administrative system can actually support the proposed intervention.

### Organizational and administrative opportunities

* Important considerations include:

  * Local community sentiment
  * Organizational support
  * Financial support
  * Availability of staff
* These factors determine whether the program can realistically be developed and implemented.

### Policy assessment

* Policy assessment identifies existing:

  * Policies
  * Regulations
* These policies and regulations may either:

  * Support the intended behavior changes
  * Discourage the intended behavior changes
* Therefore, policies and regulations must be considered before implementing an intervention. 

## Implementation and preparation for program delivery

* In the final PROCEED phases, the planner prepares for implementation.
* Preparation includes developing:

  * Training
  * Materials
  * Resources
* These are required to support effective program delivery.
* Information collected during the earlier assessment phases is used to determine:

  * Specific in-service training content
  * Skills that need to be developed
* The goal is to ensure that program facilitators have:

  * The necessary capacity
  * The necessary skills
  * The necessary resources
* This allows them to implement the proposed program effectively. 

## Process evaluation

* Process evaluation determines the extent to which the program was implemented **according to the planned protocol**.
* It assesses the actual delivery of the program.
* It can include:

  * The number of individuals reached by the program
  * Feedback from participants
* It helps determine whether the intervention was implemented as intended. 

## Impact evaluation

* Impact evaluation assesses the **effects of the program**.
* It examines changes in:

  * Predisposing factors
  * Reinforcing factors
  * Enabling factors
  * Behavioral factors
  * Environmental factors
* It therefore determines whether the intervention has produced changes in the factors that influence behavior and health. 

## Outcome evaluation

* Outcome evaluation determines the effect of the program on:

  * Health indicators
  * Quality-of-life indicators
* It assesses the final effects or outcomes produced by the intervention.
* It can include changes in:

  * Health
  * Social benefits
  * Quality of life 

## Relationship between the evaluation types

* **Process evaluation**

  * Focuses on whether the program was implemented as planned.
  * Includes program reach and participant feedback.

* **Impact evaluation**

  * Focuses on changes produced by the program in:

    * Predisposing factors
    * Enabling factors
    * Reinforcing factors
    * Behavioral factors
    * Environmental factors

* **Outcome evaluation**

  * Focuses on the final effect of the program on:

    * Health
    * Social benefits
    * Quality of life 

## Measurable objectives and evaluation milestones

* Measurable objectives are written during the different stages of PRECEDE-PROCEED planning.
* These measurable objectives serve as **milestones**.
* The accomplishments of the program can be evaluated against these milestones.
* Therefore, objectives developed during planning also provide a basis for evaluating program performance. 

## Importance of the PRECEDE-PROCEED Model

* It is considered one of the **most popular and well-respected models** for health promotion program planning.
* It uses an **ecological approach** to program planning.
* It is considered by many to be the **gold standard in health promotion planning**.
* One reason for this is the extensive assessment required **before program development begins**.
* The model provides guidance for:

  * Understanding health problems
  * Understanding determinants of health problems
  * Applying theory to health promotion planning
  * Planning interventions that influence the determinants of health problems
* It therefore connects:

  * Community needs
  * Health problems
  * Behavioral determinants
  * Environmental determinants
  * Organizational factors
  * Policy factors
  * Intervention planning
  * Program implementation
  * Evaluation

## Models of Individual Health Behavior

### Theory of Reasoned Action and Theory of Planned Behavior

* The **Theory of Reasoned Action (TRA)** and **Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB)** focus on theoretical constructs concerned with **individual motivational factors** that determine the likelihood of performing specific behaviors.
* Both theories are based on the assumption that the **best predictor of a behavior is behavioral intention**.
* Behavioral intention is determined by:

  * Attitudes toward the behavior
  * Social normative perceptions regarding the behavior
* The **Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB)** is an extension of the **Theory of Reasoned Action (TRA)**.
* TPB adds an additional construct:

  * **Perceived behavioral control** over performance of the behavior. 

### Theory of Reasoned Action — Core Concepts

* The best predictor of a behavior is **behavioral intention**.
* **Attitude** and **subjective norms** form the core of the Theory of Reasoned Action.
* The Theory of Planned Behavior adds **perceived behavioral control** to the Theory of Reasoned Action. 

### Behavioral Intention

* Behavioral intention refers to the **perceived likelihood of performing a behavior**.
* It represents the **motivational factors** that influence a particular behavior.
* The stronger the intention to perform a behavior, the more likely the behavior is to be performed. 

### Attitude

* Attitude refers to the **degree to which a person has a favorable or unfavorable evaluation** of the behavior of interest.
* It involves considering the **outcomes of performing the behavior**. 

### Subjective Norm

* Subjective norm refers to a person's **belief about whether most people approve or disapprove of the behavior**.
* It relates to beliefs about whether:

  * Peers
  * Important people
  * Other relevant individuals
    think that the person should engage in the behavior. 

### Perceived Behavioral Control

* Perceived behavioral control refers to a person's **perception of how easy or difficult it is to perform the behavior of interest**. 

### Beliefs Underlying TRA and TPB

* **Behavioral belief**

  * Belief that performing a behavior is associated with certain attributes or outcomes.

* **Evaluation of behavioral outcomes**

  * The value attached to a particular behavioral outcome or attribute.

* **Normative belief**

  * Belief about whether each relevant referent approves or disapproves of the behavior.

* **Motivation to comply**

  * Motivation to do what each relevant referent thinks the person should do.

* **Control belief**

  * Perceived likelihood that a particular facilitating or constraining condition will occur.

* **Perceived power**

  * Perceived effect of each condition in making behavioral performance difficult or easy. 

## An Integrated Behavior Model

* The **Integrated Behavior Model (IBM)**, like TRA and TPB, considers **intention to perform the behavior** as the most important determinant of behavior.
* Four other components directly affect behavior.
* Three of these components are particularly important in determining whether behavioral intentions can result in actual behavioral performance. 

### Knowledge and Skill

* Even when a person has a **strong behavioral intention**, the person needs:

  * Knowledge
  * Skills
* These are necessary to actually carry out the behavior. 

### Environmental Constraints

* There should be **no or few environmental constraints** that make behavioral performance:

  * Difficult
  * Impossible
* A person may have a strong intention but still be unable to perform the behavior when environmental conditions prevent it. 

### Behavioral Salience

* The behavior should be **salient to the person**.
* The behavior needs to be sufficiently noticeable or important to the individual for intention to translate into performance. 

### Experience and Habit

* Experience in performing a behavior may make that behavior **habitual**.
* When a behavior becomes habitual:

  * Intention becomes less important in determining behavioral performance.
  * Previous experience can influence whether the behavior continues to be performed. 

### Experiential and Instrumental Attitude

* **Experiential attitude**, or affect:

  * Refers to an individual's **emotional response** to the idea of performing a recommended behavior.
  * A strong negative emotional response makes a person less likely to perform the behavior.
  * A strong positive emotional response makes the person more likely to engage in the behavior.

* **Instrumental attitude**:

  * Is cognitively based.
  * Is determined by beliefs about the outcomes of performing the behavior.
  * This is similar to the attitude concept in TRA/TPB. 

### Normative Influence

* In TRA/TPB, **subjective norm** is defined as an **injunctive norm**.
* Injunctive norm refers to:

  * Normative beliefs about what others think a person should do.
  * The person's motivation to comply with those expectations.
* However, subjective norm may not completely capture normative influence.
* Another important component is the **descriptive norm**.
* Descriptive norm refers to perceptions about **what other people in a person's social or personal networks are actually doing**.
* Therefore, both:

  * What others think one should do
  * What others are actually doing
    can influence behavior. 

### Personal Agency

* **Personal agency** is described by Bandura as bringing one's influence to bear on:

  * One's own functioning
  * Environmental events
* In the Integrated Behavior Model, personal agency is a major factor influencing **behavioral intention**.
* Personal agency consists of two constructs:

  * **Self-efficacy**
  * **Perceived control** 

### Perceived Control

* Perceived control refers to a person's perception of the degree to which different factors make it:

  * Easy to perform a behavior
  * Difficult to perform a behavior
* It therefore concerns the perceived influence of different conditions on behavioral performance. 

### Self-Efficacy

* Self-efficacy refers to a person's **degree of confidence in performing a behavior**.
* This confidence applies even when the person faces:

  * Obstacles
  * Challenges
* Self-efficacy is therefore different from perceived control:

  * **Perceived control** → perception of factors making the behavior easy or difficult.
  * **Self-efficacy** → confidence in one's ability to perform the behavior despite obstacles or challenges. 

## Precaution Adoption Process Model

* The **Precaution Adoption Process Model (PAPM)** attempts to explain:

  * How a person comes to a decision to take action.
  * How the person translates that decision into actual action.
* PAPM mainly applies to **deliberate action** involving:

  * Adoption of a new precautionary behavior.
  * Cessation of a risky behavior.
* Example of adopting a new precautionary behavior:

  * Consistent use of masks during the COVID-19 pandemic.
* Example of cessation of a risky behavior:

  * Avoiding crowding in public places during the COVID-19 pandemic.
* PAPM is more appropriate for deliberate precautionary actions than for the gradual development of habitual behaviors.
* Examples of gradual habitual behaviors include:

  * Exercise
  * Diet 

## Key Points

* **TRA and TPB**

  * Focus on individual motivational factors that determine the likelihood of performing specific behaviors.
  * Behavioral intention is the best predictor of behavior.
  * TRA focuses on attitude and subjective norms.
  * TPB extends TRA by adding perceived behavioral control. 

* **Integrated Behavior Model**

  * Intention is the most important determinant of behavior.
  * Actual behavioral performance also depends on knowledge and skills, environmental constraints, behavioral salience, and experience/habit.
  * Personal agency consists of self-efficacy and perceived control.

* **Precaution Adoption Process Model**

  * Explains how people make decisions to take precautionary action and translate those decisions into action.
  * Mainly applies to deliberate adoption of precautionary behaviors or cessation of risky behaviors.

## Health Belief Model

* The **Health Belief Model (HBM)** was initially developed in the **1950s** by a group of social psychologists working in the **U.S. Public Health Service**.
* Its development was related to research into the failure of many eligible adults to participate in **tuberculosis screening programs**.
* These tuberculosis screening services were:

  * Provided free of charge.
  * Conducted using mobile X-ray units.
  * Conveniently located in different neighborhoods.
* Researchers were concerned with identifying the factors that:

  * Facilitated participation.
  * Inhibited participation.
* The HBM contains several primary concepts that help predict **why people take action to:**

  * Prevent illness.
  * Screen for illness.
  * Control an illness condition. 

### Perceived Susceptibility

* **Perceived susceptibility** refers to a person's **subjective perception of the risk of contracting an illness**.
* It is concerned with how susceptible or vulnerable an individual believes they are to a particular illness. 

### Perceived Severity

* **Perceived severity** refers to beliefs concerning the **seriousness of the consequences of contracting an illness**.
* Possible consequences mentioned include:

  * Death
  * Disability
  * Pain 

### Perceived Benefits

* **Perceived benefits** refer to beliefs regarding the **effectiveness of available actions in reducing a disease threat**.
* Benefits are not limited to health-related benefits.
* They can also include **non-health-related benefits**, such as:

  * Saving money. 

### Perceived Barriers

* **Perceived barriers** involve a spontaneous **cost analysis** performed by an individual when evaluating preventive actions.
* Preventive actions may be perceived as:

  * Expensive
  * Dangerous
  * Unpleasant
  * Inconvenient
  * Time-consuming
  * Or associated with other difficulties.
* These perceived costs can influence whether an individual takes the recommended preventive action. 

### Cues to Action

* **Cues to action** are the **stimuli needed to trigger the decision-making process** to accept a recommended health action.
* They act as triggers that encourage an individual to move toward taking the recommended health action. 

### Self-Efficacy

* **Self-efficacy** was introduced as a concept by **Bandura in 1977**.
* It refers to the conviction that a person **can successfully execute the behavior required to produce the desired outcomes**.
* It represents the level of a person's **confidence in their ability to successfully perform a behavior**. 

## Protection Motivation Theory

* **Protection Motivation Theory (PMT)** was first introduced by **Rogers**.
* It was developed to understand the mechanisms through which people adopt **protective behaviors to reduce perceived threat**.
* The model has been **revised several times**.
* These revisions have improved its capacity to predict **health-related behaviors**. 

### Two Pathways in Protection Motivation Theory

* PMT consists of constructs organized into **two pathways** that link perceptions to behavior.

### Threat Appraisal Pathway

* The **threat appraisal pathway** involves evaluating a person's perception of the **threat associated with certain behaviors or diseases**.
* It focuses on how an individual evaluates the seriousness and vulnerability associated with a threat. 

### Coping Appraisal Pathway

* The **coping appraisal pathway** involves evaluating a person's **ability to cope with the threat**.
* It focuses on whether the person believes that they can respond effectively to the threat. 

### Conditions Required for Protection Motivation and Coping Behavior

* There are **six sufficient conditions** that are prerequisite to eliciting protection motivation and coping behavior.
* An individual must believe that:

  * **The threat is severe**

    * The person must believe that the threat has serious consequences.

  * **He or she is vulnerable**

    * The person must believe that they are susceptible to the threat.

  * **He or she can perform the coping response**

    * The individual must believe that they are capable of carrying out the recommended coping response.

  * **The coping response is effective**

    * The individual must believe that the recommended response can effectively deal with or reduce the threat.

  * **Rewards associated with the maladaptive response are outweighed by factors decreasing the probability of making the maladaptive response**

    * The perceived rewards or benefits of continuing the maladaptive behavior should not outweigh the reasons for avoiding it.

  * **Costs of the adaptive response are outweighed by factors increasing the probability of making the adaptive response**

    * The perceived costs of adopting the protective/adaptive behavior should be outweighed by factors encouraging the person to adopt it. 

## Transtheoretical Model of Change

* The **Transtheoretical Model (TTM)** uses **stages of change** to integrate:

  * Processes of change.
  * Principles of change.
  * Concepts from major theories of intervention.
* It is called **transtheoretical** because its concepts come from different theories of:

  * Human behavior.
  * How people can be changed.
* The model views behavioral change as a **progression through a series of stages**. 

### Stages of Change

### Precontemplation

* In the **precontemplation** stage:

  * The person has **no intention to take action within the next 6 months**.
* The individual is therefore not currently intending to change the behavior within this time period. 

### Contemplation

* In the **contemplation** stage:

  * The person **intends to take action within the next 6 months**.
* The person has moved from having no intention of action toward considering future behavioral change. 

### Preparation

* In the **preparation** stage:

  * The person intends to take action **within the next 30 days**.
  * The person has already taken **some behavioral steps** toward the intended change. 

### Action

* In the **action** stage:

  * The person has changed their **overt behavior**.
  * The behavioral change has been maintained for **less than 6 months**. 

### Maintenance

* In the **maintenance** stage:

  * The person has changed their **overt behavior**.
  * The behavioral change has been maintained for **more than 6 months**. 

### Termination

* In the **termination** stage:

  * There is **no temptation to relapse**.
  * The individual has **100% confidence** in maintaining the changed behavior. 

## Key Points

* **Health Belief Model**

  * Contains several primary concepts that predict why people take action to prevent, screen for, or control illness.
  * Important concepts include:

    * Perceived susceptibility
    * Perceived severity
    * Perceived benefits
    * Perceived barriers
    * Cues to action
    * Self-efficacy. 

* **Protection Motivation Theory**

  * Was introduced by Rogers.
  * Explains mechanisms through which people adopt protective behaviors to reduce perceived threats.
  * Uses threat appraisal and coping appraisal pathways. 

* **Transtheoretical Model**

  * Uses stages of change.
  * Integrates processes and principles of change from different major theories of intervention.
  * Views behavioral change as progression through different stages.
