## The Chain of Research in Health Promotion

* Exploratory investigations **generate rather than test** specific research questions.
* Refined studies initially **test a given set of research questions**.
* Priority populations are defined through:

  * Surveillance studies
  * Epidemiological studies
* Advanced investigations are built on the findings obtained from previous tests of research questions.
* Once the research questions have been adequately addressed in a priority population:

  * The investigations should be **replicated with a second priority population**.
* The chain therefore progresses from generating research questions to testing them, building on the findings, and eventually replicating the research in another priority population. 

## Research Design in Health Promotion

* **Research design** is the strategy that an investigator chooses for answering the **research question**.
* The research question is an important factor in deciding the research design.
* However, research design is not determined by the research question alone.
* Other important factors that influence the choice of an appropriate research design include:

  * **Ethical issues**
  * **Cost**
  * **Feasibility**
  * **Access to the study population**
* Therefore, an appropriate research design should consider both the research question and the practical and ethical circumstances surrounding the study. 

## Exploratory Research

* Exploratory research is **preliminary research**.
* It serves as the **basis for future conclusive research efforts**.
* The research question in exploratory research involves investigations that will **describe the issue**.
* Exploratory investigations are particularly important at the beginning of the research process.
* Their major role is to generate specific research questions rather than primarily testing already-established questions.
* Exploratory research is associated with understanding and investigating an issue before moving toward more refined or conclusive research. 

## Conclusive Research

* Conclusive research provides information that is useful for:

  * **Reaching conclusions**
  * **Making decisions**
* The conclusive phase generally has **five overarching purposes**:

  * **To document the scope of the issue**

    * Determines and describes how extensive or widespread an issue is.
  * **To test causal or etiological theories**

    * Examines theories concerning the causes or origins of a health problem.
  * **To identify the sequelae of disease or health conditions**

    * Examines the consequences or outcomes that follow a disease or health condition.
  * **To evaluate measurement instruments**

    * Determines whether instruments used for measurement are appropriate.
  * **To evaluate treatments or interventions**

    * Examines the effectiveness of treatments or interventions. 

## Exploratory Research vs Conclusive Research

| Exploratory Research                               | Conclusive Research                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Preliminary in nature                              | Provides information useful for reaching conclusions or making decisions                   |
| Serves as the basis for future conclusive research | Used when research needs to provide evidence for conclusions and decisions                 |
| Research questions involve describing the issue    | Can be used to examine specific research questions and evaluate interventions              |
| Generates specific research questions              | Tests or evaluates research questions, theories, instruments, treatments and interventions |
| Associated with the exploratory stage of research  | Associated with more refined and advanced investigation                                    |



## Health Promotion Research: Stages, Purpose, Types and Methods

* Health promotion research can be understood through different:

  * **Stages of research**
  * **Purposes of research**
  * **Types of research**
  * **Research methods**
* Exploratory and conclusive research represent important stages/types within the overall research process.
* The selection of research methods depends on the research question and the purpose of the investigation. 

## Qualitative Research

* Qualitative methods involve making observations or collecting data that typically **cannot be expressed numerically**.
* Observational research that uses qualitative methods aligns with the **exploratory stage** of research.
* Qualitative research typically does **not entail the use of specific research designs** in the same way as quantitative research.
* Instead, depending on the research question, **different strategies are used**.
* Examples of qualitative strategies include:

  * **Naturalistic observation**
  * **Phenomenology**
  * **Ethnography**
  * **Grounded theory**
* Qualitative research is particularly useful when the researcher needs to explore or understand an issue in depth rather than primarily represent observations through numerical values. 

## Quantitative Research

* Quantitative methods involve observations or data that can take on **numerical values**.
* Data may be directly numerical, or participants' verbal or written responses may be **quantified by transforming them into numerical values**.
* Analysis of quantitative methods requires the use of **statistical procedures**.
* Quantitative research can be:

  * **Observational**
  * **Experimental**
* Quantitative research therefore allows researchers to represent observations numerically and apply statistical analysis to the resulting data. 

## Qualitative Research vs Quantitative Research

| Qualitative Research                                                                      | Quantitative Research                                          |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Data typically cannot be expressed numerically                                            | Data can take numerical values                                 |
| Often aligned with exploratory research                                                   | Can be observational or experimental                           |
| Uses different strategies depending on the research question                              | Requires statistical procedures for analysis                   |
| Does not typically rely on specific research designs                                      | Uses quantitative research designs                             |
| Examples include naturalistic observation, phenomenology, ethnography and grounded theory | Participant responses can be transformed into numerical values |



## Key Issues in Research Technique

Important issues that need to be considered in research technique include:

* **Selection of participants**

  * Sampling is important for selecting the participants who will take part in the research.
* **Measurement of variables**

  * The researcher needs to select an **appropriate tool** for measuring the variables.
* **Analysis of findings**

  * The collected data need to undergo appropriate **data analysis**.
* Therefore, research technique involves consideration of:

  * Who will participate
  * How participants will be selected
  * How variables will be measured
  * Which measurement tools will be appropriate
  * How the collected data will be analyzed
  * How the findings will ultimately be interpreted. 

## Sampling

* **Sampling** is taking any portion of a population or universe as representative of that population or universe.
* Sampling allows researchers to study a selected portion of a population rather than necessarily studying the entire population.
* **Target population**

  * The collection of all:

    * Individuals
    * Families
    * Groups
    * Organizations
    * Events
  * that the researcher is interested in finding out about.
* **Sampling unit**

  * The unit about which information is collected.
* **Unit of analysis**

  * The unit that provides the basis of analysis.
* **Sampling frame**

  * The actual list of sampling units from which the sample is selected. 

## Important Sampling Terms

### Target Population

* Refers to the complete collection of individuals, families, groups, organizations or events that the researcher is interested in studying.
* It defines the population to which the research interest is directed. 

### Sampling Unit

* The unit about which information is collected.
* It represents the basic unit selected for obtaining information during sampling. 

### Unit of Analysis

* The unit that provides the basis for analysis.
* It is the unit at which the collected information is analyzed. 

### Sampling Frame

* The actual list of sampling units from which the sample is selected.
* It provides the practical source or list from which researchers draw their sample. 

## Sampling Techniques

Sampling techniques can broadly be divided into **probability** and **non-probability** sampling.

### Probability Sampling

* **Simple random sampling**
* **Systematic random sampling**
* **Stratified random sampling**
* **Cluster random sampling**
* **Multi-stage sampling**

### Non-Probability Sampling

* **Convenience sampling**
* **Venue-based-time sampling**
* **Purposive sampling**
* **Quota sampling**
* **Chain-referral or snowball sampling**
* **Respondent-driven sampling**

## Venue-Based-Time Sampling

* Venue-based-time sampling is used for **hard-to-reach populations** where convenience sampling may not be possible.
* It can be viewed as a **quasi-probability sampling method**.
* It is a **two-stage sampling design**.
* The method allows the researcher to generate a sample with **known properties from hard-to-reach populations**.
* It improves on convenience sampling because it employs **systematic procedures**.
* Because of these systematic procedures, inferences relating to the **venue-visiting population** can be made.
* It is particularly useful in studies involving hard-to-reach populations.
* Examples given include studies involving:

  * **Commercial sex workers**
  * **Injection drug users**

## Important Points to Remember

* Research design is the **strategy chosen by the investigator to answer the research question**.
* Ethical issues, cost, feasibility and access to the study population also influence research design.
* Research in health promotion may be:

  * **Exploratory**
  * **Conclusive**
* Exploratory investigations **generate research questions**.
* Refined studies **test research questions**.
* Advanced investigations are built on findings from previous research.
* Research should be replicated with a **second priority population** after research questions have been adequately addressed in the first priority population.
* Exploratory research is preliminary and provides a basis for future conclusive research.
* Conclusive research provides information useful for conclusions and decisions.
* Conclusive research can document the scope of an issue, test causal or etiological theories, identify disease sequelae, evaluate measurement instruments and evaluate treatments or interventions.
* Qualitative research generally involves data that cannot be expressed numerically and is commonly aligned with the exploratory stage.
* Important qualitative strategies include:

  * Naturalistic observation
  * Phenomenology
  * Ethnography
  * Grounded theory
* Quantitative research involves numerical data or data transformed into numerical values.
* Quantitative analysis requires statistical procedures.
* Quantitative research may be observational or experimental.
* Participants in research are selected through **sampling**.
* The target population is the complete population of interest.
* The sampling unit is the unit about which information is collected.
* The unit of analysis provides the basis of analysis.
* The sampling frame is the actual list of sampling units from which the sample is selected.
* Sampling methods are broadly classified into:

  * **Probability sampling**
  * **Non-probability sampling**
* Probability methods include:

  * Simple random sampling
  * Systematic random sampling
  * Stratified random sampling
  * Cluster random sampling
  * Multi-stage sampling
* Non-probability methods include:

  * Convenience sampling
  * Venue-based-time sampling
  * Purposive sampling
  * Quota sampling
  * Chain-referral or snowball sampling
  * Respondent-driven sampling
* Venue-based-time sampling is particularly useful for **hard-to-reach populations** and uses systematic procedures to improve upon convenience sampling.
* The choice of research design and sampling technique must be appropriate to the research question, population and practical circumstances.
