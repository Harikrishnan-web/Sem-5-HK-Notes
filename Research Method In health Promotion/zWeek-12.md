## Lecture 1 — Developing a Research Proposal in Health Promotion

* **Lecture #56**
* **Lecturer:** Dr. Arista Lahiri, MD, MBA

* **Institution:** Dr. B. C. Roy Multi-Speciality Medical Research Centre, IIT Kharagpur

### Components of a Research Proposal

A health-promotion research proposal generally contains:

* **Title**

  * Brief and self-explanatory.
* **Summary/Abstract**
* **Background/Introduction**

  * Background of proposed work.
  * May include review of literature.
  * Should demonstrate the research question.
* **Rationale/Justification**

  * May be included within the background.
* **Aims and Objectives**
* **Methodology**

  * Research design.
  * Timeline.
  * Methods.
  * Tools and techniques.
  * Analysis plan.
* **Ethical issues**
* **References**
* **Appendices/Additional materials**

### Research Proposal Development

* Write the proposal assuming the reader has **limited knowledge of the topic**.
* The proposal should be:

  * Comprehensive.
  * Logically organized.
  * Clearly written.
* Use:

  * Clear language.
  * Minimal jargon.
* Clearly describe:

  * Research questions.
  * Methodology.
  * Data collection.
  * Data analysis.
* Reviewers from funding agencies usually have extensive knowledge of the field.
* Therefore, the proposal should demonstrate:

  * Knowledge of the current state of research.
  * Why the proposed investigation is worthwhile.

### Title of the Research Proposal

* The title should be:

  * Brief.
  * Clear.
  * Informative.
* It should:

  * State or clearly indicate the research question.
  * Preferably mention the research design.
* Example:

  * *Evaluation of a community health promotion program in a socioeconomically deprived city district in the Netherlands using mixed methods and guided by action research.*

### Abstract / Summary

* Summarizes:

  * Objectives of the proposed research.
  * Methods to be used.
  * Target group/population.
  * Relevance of the research.
  * Expected benefits.
* Reviewers should understand:

  * Why the research is being proposed.
  * What the research will focus on.
* **Write the abstract after the proposal has been fully developed.**

### Background / Introduction

* Provides the **context** of the proposed research.
* Should include concise descriptions of:

  * Previous research.
  * Problems encountered previously in collecting and analyzing data.
  * Interpretation of previous findings.
* May include:

  * Theoretical framework.
  * Premises underlying the study.
* The **research question should be proposed at the end** of the background/introduction.

### Statement of Need / Rationale / Justification

* Can include:

  * Data on prevalence of the health problem.
  * Findings from a previous needs assessment.
* Give a brief description of:

  * Extent/nature of researchers' involvement with the target group.
  * Relevant agencies.
* This is particularly important in health promotion research because it contributes to **feasibility**.
* Mention the **innovation** being proposed.

### Hypothesis

* A hypothesis is a testable prediction.
* Example:

  * Children participating in a 15-minute exercise program at the beginning of each school day will be more attentive in class.

### Aims and Objectives

* May be:

  * A separate section.
  * Included at the end of the introduction.
* A separate section makes them easier for readers to identify.
* Objectives should be:

  * **Measurable**
  * **Attainable**
  * Achievable within the study timeframe.
* Objectives may follow **SMART** principles:

  * **S — Specific**
  * **M — Measurable**
  * **A — Achievable**
  * **R — Relevant**
  * **T — Time-bound**

### Methodology — Study Design

* Describe methods used to achieve objectives:

  * What will be done?
  * How will it be done?
  * With whom?
  * When?
* The **objectives guide the choice of study design**.
* Qualitative and quantitative methodologies are appropriate for different types of research.
* Clearly justify:

  * Design selected.
  * Methods selected.
* Keep the design:

  * Simple.
  * Clear.
* Possible designs include:

  * Surveys.
  * Interviews.
  * Focus groups.
  * Observational studies.
  * Interventional studies.

### Methodology — Sample Selection

Describe:

* How participants will be selected.
* Study population/target group.
* Characteristics of participants.
* Representativeness.
* Required number of:

  * Individuals.
  * Groups.
  * Communities.
* **Inclusion criteria**
* **Exclusion criteria**

### Methodology — Data Measurement

* Explain:

  * Whether quantitative, qualitative, or both types of data will be collected.
  * How the data fit the research question.
  * Tools/measures used.
* Measurement can include:

  * **Scales and indexes** — structured measurements.
  * **Personal accounts and observations** — less structured measurements.
* If evaluating an intervention/program:

  * Clearly specify criteria for:

    * Success.
    * Failure.
  * Justify the criteria.

### Methodology — Data Collection

* Explain:

  * How data will be collected.
  * Procedures used to ensure data are:

    * Complete.
    * Good quality.
* Discuss **SOPs — Standard Operating Procedures**.
* Data collection should:

  * Be clearly described.
  * Have a rationale.
  * Be consistent with study objectives.

#### Common Data Collection Pitfalls

* **Collecting too much data**

  * Collecting information merely because it may be interesting, even though it does not relate to objectives.
* **Collecting too little/incorrect data**

  * Failing to collect information required to meet the study objectives.

### Methodology — Data Analysis

#### Quantitative Data

* Specify:

  * Statistical procedures.
  * Analysis methods.
* Include validation of key measures.

#### Qualitative Data

* Describe how:

  * Themes.
  * Categories
    emerging from interviews, observations, documents or other sources will be:
  * Analyzed.
  * Interpreted.

#### Software

Possible software should be mentioned, e.g.:

* SPSS
* STATA
* R
* NVIVO

#### Common Pitfall

* Conducting analyses that were **not specified in the objectives**.
* Analysis should remain focused on the **specific study objectives**.

### Timeline

* Present timelines for different study activities.
* A **Gantt chart** is preferable.
* Timelines should be aligned with:

  * Objectives.
  * Milestones.

### Ethics in Research

The proposal should state:

* Ethical principles being followed.
* How those principles will be observed.
* Ethics committee approval.
* Informed consent.

### References

* List cited resources in an appropriate format, such as:

  * Vancouver.
  * APA.
  * AMA.
* Formatting depends on:

  * Journal guidelines.
  * Funding agency guidelines.

### Appendices

May include:

* Questionnaires.
* Intervention materials.
* Checklists.
* IDI guides.
* Participant information sheet.
* Consent form.
* Letters of support.
* Approval letters.
* Other study materials.

### Research Grant Proposal

* A **research grant** generally means funding support obtained from outside the research team.
* A grant proposal contains the standard technical research proposal plus additional requirements.

#### Budget

* Include a **budget with justification**.
* Budget should be:

  * Itemized.
  * Detailed.
  * Appropriately justified.
* Expenditures should be organized under relevant heads.

#### Funding Agency Guidelines

* Topic and proposal structure should align with:

  * Funding agency priorities.
  * Funding agency guidelines.

#### Work Packages

* Represent the overall research as different **work packages**.
* Each work package should have:

  * Milestones.
  * Appropriate timelines.
* This helps:

  * Plan timelines.
  * Align the budget.

### Research Team

* Health promotion research requires a **multidisciplinary team** with complementary expertise.
* Useful expertise may include:

  * Epidemiology.
  * Sociology.
  * Psychology.
  * Health promotion practice.
* An expert in:

  * Biostatistics.
  * Necessary analytical skills
    is recommended.
* Grant proposals should describe:

  * Roles.
  * Responsibilities
    of different team members.

### Guidelines for Preparing a Research Proposal

* **SPIRIT 2013**

  * Provides standard elements for clinical trial protocols.
  * Can also be useful in designing health promotion intervention research.
* Related modifications/extensions mentioned:

  * **SPIRIT-PRO**

    * Patient-reported outcomes in clinical trial protocols.
  * **SPENT 2019**

    * Extension/elaboration for n-of-1 trials.
  * **SPIRIT-AI**

    * Clinical trial protocols involving artificial intelligence.
  * **CONSERVE 2021**

    * Reporting trial protocols and completed trials during COVID-19 and other extenuating circumstances.
* EQUATOR Network provides reporting guidelines.

### Final Proposal-Writing Principles

* Connect the proposal with:

  * Interests.
  * Priorities
    of the funding agency.
* Be:

  * Concise.
  * Comprehensive.
* Follow the relevant guideline.
* Make organization:

  * Clear.
  * Structured.
* Make the proposal easy to skim:

  * Highlight important points.
* Ensure smooth transitions.
* Use:

  * Direct language.
  * Simple sentences.
  * Minimal jargon.
* Clearly outline specific objectives.
* State research questions carefully and concisely.
* Demonstrate contribution to **new knowledge**.
* Demonstrate competence of the research team.
* Always check relevant standard guidelines such as **SPIRIT**.

# Lecture 2 — Report Writing in Health Promotion: An Overview

* **Lecture #57**
* **Lecturer:** Dr. Arista Lahiri, MD, MBA
* **Designation:** Assistant Professor, Community Medicine
* **Institution:** Dr. B. C. Roy Multi-Speciality Medical Research Centre, IIT Kharagpur

## Selecting an Appropriate Journal

* Select a journal that aligns with:

  * Research topic.
  * Methodology.
  * Perspective.
* Aim for a journal that provides maximum impact for:

  * Topic.
  * Practical significance of findings.
* Consider:

  * Open-access journal.
  * Subscription-only journal.
* Select a journal whose:

  * Readership is interested in the study outcomes.
  * Objectives match the research.
* Health promotion research is multidisciplinary.
* A particular health promotion study may not appeal to a general audience.
* Study nature and analysis should be understandable to the **intended readership**.
* Collaboration across disciplines is a strength of health promotion research.

## Structure of a Research Manuscript

The exact structure depends on journal guidelines.

Common sections:

* **Abstract**
* **Introduction**
* **Methods**
* **Results**
* **Discussion**
* **References**

## Abstract

* Must concisely describe within the allowed word limit:

  * Objective(s).
  * Methods.
  * Results.
  * Conclusion(s).

## Introduction

* Focuses on:

  * Identifying gaps in existing literature.
  * Addressing those gaps.
* Describe the **chain of research** that led to the current study.
* This prepares readers for the conclusions.
* Effective introduction generally:

  * Begins broadly.
  * Gradually narrows to the specific research question.

## Methods

### Study Sample

Describe:

* Population from which sample was drawn.
* Inclusion criteria.
* Exclusion criteria.
* Number of otherwise eligible participants excluded.
* Sampling technique.
* Rationale for selecting that sampling technique.

### Data Collection

* Explain **how data were collected**, not simply what data were collected.

### Measures

* Justify constructs being measured.
* Example:

  * Self-efficacy.
* Describe measures used to assess those constructs.
* Essentially explains **what data were collected**.

### Data Analysis

* Should be:

  * Brief.
  * Informative.
* Describe all specific statistical techniques used.

## Results

### Characteristics of the Sample

* Purely descriptive.
* Purpose:

  * Inform readers about composition of the study sample.

### Findings

* Use:

  * Technical writing.
  * Terse language.
  * Precise language.
* Use appropriate subheadings.
* Subheadings often correspond to:

  * Study objectives.

### Tables and Figures

* Must **not duplicate** information already presented in the text.
* Should have:

  * Appropriate title.
  * Description.
  * Footnotes.
  * Legends.
* They should be **self-explanatory**.

## Discussion

### General Guidelines

* Opening paragraph:

  * Summarize major findings.
  * Avoid excessive statistics.
  * Avoid jargon.
  * Use language understandable to a lay audience.
* Compare findings with:

  * Previous studies.
* Discuss possible reasons findings:

  * Agree with previous research.
  * Differ from previous research.
* Highlight:

  * Findings supporting hypotheses.
  * Mechanisms involved.
* Explain why hypotheses were not supported.
* Write paragraphs so findings are useful in the field.
* Discuss implications using:

  * Humble.
  * Engaging language.

### Limitations

* Every research study has limitations.
* Limitations represent recognized weaknesses that may reduce overall rigor.
* They **must still be stated**.
* Limitations help readers:

  * Understand the transition from methods to results.
  * Place findings into proper context.

## Conclusions

* Conclusions represent the **crux of the manuscript and research**.
* Preferably written as:

  * One short paragraph.
  * Sometimes 2–3 sentences are sufficient.
* Should be:

  * Direct.
  * Clear.
* Should specify a **call to action** through its description.

## References

* Mandatory section of a manuscript.
* Include all materials cited in the text.
* Possible styles:

  * Vancouver.
  * APA.
  * AMA.
* Use the style required by the journal.

## Critiquing Journal Articles

### General Criteria

Check whether the article is:

* Concise.
* Parsimonious.
* Grammatically correct.
* Well organized.
* Logical.
* Free of unnecessary jargon.
* Free of biased language.

Also assess whether the topic is:

* Timely.
* Relevant to health promotion.
* Relevant to practice.
* Relevant to research.
* Providing new insights into the problem.

### Specific Criteria — Introduction

Check whether the introduction provides:

* Overview of the problem.
* Extent of the problem.
* Who is affected.
* Theoretical framework.
* Review of relevant literature.
* Identification of gaps.
* Exact research question(s).
* Hypotheses, when applicable.

### Specific Criteria — Methods

Check whether methods provide:

* Study sample.
* Sampling methodology.
* Recruitment methodology.
* Research design.
* Measures administered.
* Data collection process.
* Analytical procedures.

### Specific Criteria — Results

Check for:

* Linkage to research question(s).
* Necessary statistical information.
* Clear description of findings.
* Relevant tables.
* Relevant figures.

### Specific Criteria — Discussion

Check whether discussion includes:

* Clear and concise overview of findings.
* Findings discussed in context of reviewed literature.
* Potential/plausible explanations.
* Implications for practice.
* Implications for future research.
* Limitations.
* Significance and relevance of findings to the field.

### Key Points

* Journal choice should consider:

  * Readership.
  * Journal objectives.
* Manuscript structure usually depends on:

  * Journal guidelines.
* Critiquing journal articles helps:

  * Improve presentation.
  * Improve robustness of the research.
  * Enhance scientific reliability.

# Lecture 3 — Report Writing: Quantitative Research in Health Promotion (Part I)

* **Lecture #58**
* **Lecturer:** Dr. Arista Lahiri, MD, MBA
* **Designation:** Assistant Professor, Community Medicine
* **Institution:** Dr. B. C. Roy Multi-Speciality Medical Research Centre, IIT Kharagpur

## Reporting Guidelines for Observational Studies

### STROBE

* **STROBE = Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology**
* STROBE provides reporting guidance for:

  * Observational studies.
  * Epidemiological studies.
* Main checklist is a **combined checklist** covering:

  * Cross-sectional studies.
  * Case-control studies.
  * Cohort studies.

### STROBE Extensions

* **STROBE-ME**

  * Molecular epidemiology.
* **STROME-ID**

  * Infectious diseases.
* **STROBE-RDS**

  * Respondent-driven sampling.
* Extensions also exist for:

  * Case-cohort studies.

## STROBE Combined Checklist

### Title and Abstract

#### Item 1 — Title and Abstract

* Indicate the study design using a commonly used term in:

  * Title.
  * Abstract.
* Abstract should provide an:

  * Informative.
  * Balanced
    summary of:
  * What was done.
  * What was found.

### Introduction

#### Item 2 — Background/Rationale

* Explain:

  * Scientific background.
  * Rationale for the investigation.

#### Item 3 — Objectives

* State:

  * Specific objectives.
  * Prespecified hypotheses, where applicable.

## Methods

### Item 4 — Study Design

* Present key elements of the study design early in the paper.

### Item 5 — Setting

Describe:

* Setting.
* Locations.
* Relevant dates.
* Recruitment period.
* Exposure period.
* Follow-up period.
* Data collection period.

### Item 6 — Participants

#### Cohort Study

* Eligibility criteria.
* Sources and methods of participant selection.
* Methods of follow-up.

#### Case-Control Study

* Eligibility criteria.
* Case ascertainment.
* Control selection.
* Rationale for selecting cases and controls.

#### Cross-Sectional Study

* Eligibility criteria.
* Sources and methods of participant selection.

#### Matched Studies

* Matching criteria.
* Number of:

  * Exposed/unexposed participants in cohort studies.
  * Controls per case in case-control studies.

### Item 7 — Variables

Clearly define:

* Outcomes.
* Exposures.
* Predictors.
* Potential confounders.
* Effect modifiers.
* Diagnostic criteria, where applicable.

### Item 8 — Data Sources and Measurement

For each important variable:

* Give source of data.
* Describe measurement/assessment methods.
* If more than one group exists:

  * Describe comparability of measurement methods.

### Item 9 — Bias

* Describe efforts used to address:

  * Potential sources of bias.

### Item 10 — Study Size

* Explain how the study size was determined.

### Item 11 — Quantitative Variables

* Explain how quantitative variables were handled during analysis.
* If grouped:

  * Describe grouping.
  * Explain why those groupings were selected.

### Item 12 — Statistical Methods

Describe:

* All statistical methods.
* Methods used to control confounding.
* Methods used to examine:

  * Subgroups.
  * Interactions.
* Methods used to handle:

  * Missing data.
* Cohort studies:

  * Methods addressing loss to follow-up.
* Case-control studies:

  * Methods addressing matching.
* Cross-sectional studies:

  * Methods accounting for sampling strategy.
* Sensitivity analyses, where applicable.

## Results

### Item 13 — Participants

Report numbers at each stage:

* Potentially eligible.
* Assessed for eligibility.
* Confirmed eligible.
* Included.
* Completed follow-up.
* Analyzed.

Also:

* Give reasons for non-participation.
* A participant flow diagram may be used.

### Item 14 — Descriptive Data

Report:

* Characteristics of participants:

  * Demographic.
  * Clinical.
  * Social.
* Exposure information.
* Potential confounders.
* Number of participants with missing data for each important variable.
* Cohort studies:

  * Follow-up time.

### Item 15 — Outcome Data

* **Cohort studies**

  * Number of outcome events or summary measures over time.
* **Case-control studies**

  * Number in each exposure category or summary measures of exposure.
* **Cross-sectional studies**

  * Number of outcome events or summary measures.

### Item 16 — Main Results

* Give:

  * Unadjusted estimates.
  * Confounder-adjusted estimates where applicable.
  * Precision, e.g. **95% confidence interval**.
* Clearly state:

  * Which confounders were adjusted for.
  * Why they were included.
* If continuous variables are categorized:

  * Report category boundaries.
* Where relevant:

  * Translate relative risk estimates into absolute risk over a meaningful period.

### Item 17 — Other Analyses

Report:

* Subgroup analyses.
* Interaction analyses.
* Sensitivity analyses.

## Discussion

### Item 18 — Key Results

* Summarize key results in relation to:

  * Study objectives.

### Item 19 — Limitations

Discuss:

* Study limitations.
* Potential sources of:

  * Bias.
  * Imprecision.
* Consider:

  * Direction of potential bias.
  * Magnitude of potential bias.

### Item 20 — Interpretation

* Give a **cautious overall interpretation**.
* Consider:

  * Objectives.
  * Limitations.
  * Multiplicity of analyses.
  * Similar studies.
  * Other relevant evidence.

### Item 21 — Generalisability

* Discuss:

  * Generalisability.
  * External validity
    of findings.

### Item 22 — Funding

* Give:

  * Funding source.
  * Role of funders.
* If based on an original study:

  * Also mention funding/role related to that original study.

### STROBE — Exam Takeaway

* **STROBE → observational studies**
* Covers:

  * Cross-sectional.
  * Case-control.
  * Cohort.
* Reporting should cover:

  * Title/abstract.
  * Background/objectives.
  * Design/setting/participants.
  * Variables/measurement/bias.
  * Study size/statistics.
  * Participants/results.
  * Main results/other analyses.
  * Limitations/interpretation/generalizability.
  * Funding.
* STROBE can also be applied to **health promotion research** because health promotion research uses epidemiological building blocks.

# Lecture 4 — Report Writing: Quantitative Research in Health Promotion (Part II)

* **Lecture #59**
* **Lecturer:** Dr. Arista Lahiri, MD, MBA
* **Designation:** Assistant Professor, Community Medicine
* **Institution:** Dr. B. C. Roy Multi-Speciality Medical Research Centre, IIT Kharagpur

## Reporting Guidelines for Interventional Studies

### CONSORT

* **CONSORT = Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials**
* Mainly applies to:

  * Randomized trials.
* Has several extensions.
* Can also be used for:

  * Health promotion interventions.

### CONSORT Extensions

Important extensions include:

* CONSORT Harms.
* CONSORT Non-Inferiority.
* CONSORT Cluster.
* CONSORT Herbal.
* CONSORT Non-Pharmacological Treatment Interventions.
* CONSORT Pragmatic Trials.
* CONSORT-PRO:

  * Patient-reported outcomes.
* **TIDieR**

  * Template for Intervention Description and Replication.

### Diagnostic/Prognostic Accuracy Reporting

* **STARD**
* **TRIPOD**

## CONSORT Guidelines

### Title and Abstract

#### Item 1a — Title

* Identify the study as a **randomized trial** in the title.

#### Item 1b — Abstract

* Structured summary of:

  * Trial design.
  * Methods.
  * Results.
  * Conclusions.

### Introduction

#### Item 2a — Background and Objectives

* Scientific background.
* Explanation of rationale.

#### Item 2b

* Specific:

  * Objectives.
  * Hypothesis.

### Methods

#### Item 3 — Trial Design

* Describe trial design, e.g.:

  * Parallel.
  * Factorial.
* Include:

  * Allocation ratio.
* Report important changes to methods after trial commencement.
* Give reasons for changes.

#### Item 4 — Participants

* Eligibility criteria.
* Settings.
* Locations where data were collected.

#### Item 5 — Interventions

* Describe experimental and control interventions.
* Provide sufficient detail to allow **replication**.
* State:

  * How interventions were administered.
  * When they were administered.

### Item 6 — Outcomes

* Completely define:

  * Prespecified primary outcomes.
  * Prespecified secondary outcomes.
* State:

  * How outcomes were assessed.
  * When outcomes were assessed.
* Report changes to outcomes after trial commencement and reasons for those changes.

### Item 7 — Sample Size

* Explain:

  * How sample size was determined.
* Where applicable:

  * Interim analyses.
  * Stopping guidelines.

### Item 8 — Randomization: Sequence Generation

* Describe:

  * Method used to generate random allocation sequence.
  * Type of randomization.
  * Restrictions such as:

    * Blocking.
    * Block size.

### Item 9 — Allocation Concealment

* Describe mechanism used to implement the random allocation sequence.
* Explain steps used to conceal the sequence until intervention assignment.

### Item 10 — Randomization: Implementation

State who:

* Generated the allocation sequence.
* Enrolled participants.
* Assigned participants to interventions.

### Item 11 — Blinding

If performed:

* State who was blinded after assignment:

  * Participants.
  * Care providers.
  * Outcome assessors.
* Explain how blinding was done.
* If relevant, describe similarity of interventions.

### Item 12 — Statistical Methods

* Statistical methods for:

  * Primary outcomes.
  * Secondary outcomes.
* Additional analyses:

  * Subgroup analyses.
  * Adjusted analyses.

## Results

### Item 13 — Participant Flow

For each group report:

* Number randomized.

* Number receiving intended treatment.

* Number analyzed for primary outcome.

* Losses after randomization.

* Exclusions after randomization.

* Reasons for losses/exclusions.

* A **participant flow diagram is strongly recommended**.

### Item 14 — Recruitment

Report:

* Recruitment dates.
* Follow-up dates.
* Why trial ended or was stopped.

### Item 15 — Baseline Data

* Provide a table showing:

  * Baseline demographic characteristics.
  * Baseline clinical characteristics
    for each group.

### Item 16 — Numbers Analysed

* Give denominator for each analysis.
* State whether analysis followed the original assigned groups.

### Item 17 — Outcomes and Estimation

For each primary and secondary outcome:

* Report results for each group.
* Give:

  * Estimated effect size.
  * Precision, e.g. 95% CI.
* For binary outcomes:

  * Present both:

    * Absolute effect size.
    * Relative effect size.

### Item 18 — Ancillary Analyses

Report:

* Other analyses.
* Subgroup analyses.
* Adjusted analyses.
* Clearly distinguish:

  * Prespecified analyses.
  * Exploratory analyses.

### Item 19 — Harms

* Report all important:

  * Harms.
  * Unintended effects
    in each group.

## Discussion

### Item 20 — Limitations

Discuss:

* Trial limitations.
* Potential bias.
* Imprecision.
* Multiplicity of analyses, when relevant.

### Item 21 — Generalisability

* Discuss:

  * External validity.
  * Applicability
    of trial findings.

### Item 22 — Interpretation

* Interpretation should be:

  * Consistent with results.
* Balance:

  * Benefits.
  * Harms.
* Consider:

  * Other relevant evidence.

### Item 23 — Registration

* State:

  * Registration number.
  * Name of trial registry.

### Item 24 — Protocol

* State where the full trial protocol can be accessed, if available.

### Item 25 — Funding

* Report:

  * Funding sources.
  * Other support.
  * Role of funders.

### CONSORT — Exam Takeaway

* **CONSORT → randomized/interventional trials**
* Important areas:

  * Randomization.
  * Allocation concealment.
  * Blinding.
  * Participant flow.
  * Outcomes.
  * Sample size.
  * Statistical analysis.
  * Harms.
  * Limitations.
  * Generalisability.
  * Registration.
  * Funding.
* CONSORT and extensions can be used for **interventional health promotion research**.
* Participant recruitment and follow-up **flowchart is strongly recommended**.

## Report Writing in Qualitative Research

### Writing the Qualitative Report

* A qualitative report should develop:

  * **Descriptions**
  * **Themes**
    from the data.
* These descriptions and themes should:

  * Convey multiple participant perspectives.
  * Provide detailed descriptions of:

    * Setting.
    * Individuals.
* Depending on the qualitative strategy, findings may be presented as:

  * **Narrative research**

    * Chronological narrative of an individual's life.
  * **Phenomenology**

    * Detailed description of experiences.
  * **Grounded theory**

    * Theory generated from the data.
  * **Ethnography**

    * Detailed portrait of a culture-sharing group.
  * **Case study**

    * In-depth analysis of one or more cases.

### Forms of Qualitative Presentation

Findings and interpretation may be presented as:

* Objective accounts.
* Fieldwork experiences.
* Chronology.
* Process model.
* Extended story.
* Analysis by cases.
* Analysis across cases.
* Detailed descriptive portrait.

Specific writing strategies should be selected to effectively communicate the qualitative research.

# Standards for Reporting Qualitative Research — SRQR

## Title and Abstract

### S1 — Title

* Concise description of:

  * Nature of study.
  * Topic.
* Recommended to identify:

  * Study as qualitative.
  * Qualitative approach, e.g.:

    * Ethnography.
    * Grounded theory.
  * Data collection method, e.g.:

    * Interview.
    * Focus group.

### S2 — Abstract

* Summarize key elements.
* Usually includes:

  * Background.
  * Purpose.
  * Methods.
  * Results.
  * Conclusions.

## Introduction

### S3 — Problem Formulation

* Describe:

  * Problem/phenomenon.
  * Significance.
* Review:

  * Relevant theory.
  * Empirical work.
* Provide:

  * Problem statement.

### S4 — Purpose or Research Question

* State:

  * Purpose.
  * Specific objectives.
  * Research questions.

## Methods

### S5 — Qualitative Approach and Research Paradigm

State:

* Qualitative approach, such as:

  * Ethnography.
  * Grounded theory.
  * Case study.
  * Phenomenology.
  * Narrative research.
* Guiding theory, if appropriate.
* Research paradigm, e.g.:

  * Postpositivist.
  * Constructivist/interpretivist.
* Rationale for selecting the approach.

### S6 — Researcher Characteristics and Reflexivity

Describe researcher characteristics that may influence research, including:

* Personal attributes.
* Qualifications.
* Experience.
* Relationship with participants.
* Assumptions.
* Presuppositions.

Also consider interaction between researcher characteristics and:

* Research questions.
* Approach.
* Methods.
* Results.
* Transferability.

### S7 — Context

* Describe:

  * Setting/site.
  * Important contextual factors.
* Provide rationale.

### S8 — Sampling Strategy

Describe:

* How participants/documents/events were selected.
* Why they were selected.
* Criteria for deciding when no further sampling was needed.
* Example:

  * Sampling saturation.
* Provide rationale.

### S9 — Ethical Issues

Report:

* Ethics review board approval.
* Participant consent.
* Explanation if approval/consent was not obtained.
* Confidentiality.
* Data security.

### S10 — Data Collection Methods

Describe:

* Types of data collected.
* Data collection procedures.
* Start and stop dates, where appropriate.
* Iterative processes.
* Triangulation of:

  * Sources.
  * Methods.
* Modifications to procedures resulting from evolving findings.
* Rationale.

### S11 — Data Collection Instruments and Technologies

Describe:

* Instruments:

  * Interview guides.
  * Questionnaires.
* Devices:

  * Audio recorders.
  * Other recording technologies.
* Explain whether/how instruments changed during the study.

### S12 — Units of Study

Report:

* Number of:

  * Participants.
  * Documents.
  * Events.
* Relevant characteristics.
* Level of participation.

### S13 — Data Processing

Describe:

* Transcription.
* Data entry.
* Data management.
* Data security.
* Verification of data integrity.
* Data coding.
* Anonymization/de-identification of excerpts.

### S14 — Data Analysis

Describe:

* How:

  * Inferences.
  * Themes
    were identified and developed.
* Researchers involved in analysis.
* Relevant paradigm/approach.
* Rationale.

### S15 — Techniques to Enhance Trustworthiness

Describe methods used to improve:

* Trustworthiness.
* Credibility.

Examples:

* Member checking.
* Audit trail.
* Triangulation.
* Rationale for their use.

## Results / Findings

### S16 — Synthesis and Interpretation

Report:

* Main findings.
* Interpretations.
* Inferences.
* Themes.
* Theory/model development where applicable.
* Integration with previous research/theory.

### S17 — Links to Empirical Data

Support findings using evidence such as:

* Participant quotations.
* Field notes.
* Text excerpts.
* Photographs.

## Discussion

### S18 — Integration with Prior Work, Implications, Transferability and Contribution

Include:

* Short summary of main findings.
* How findings/conclusions:

  * Connect with earlier scholarship.
  * Support earlier findings.
  * Elaborate earlier findings.
  * Challenge earlier findings.
* Scope of application/generalizability.
* Unique contribution to the field.

### S19 — Limitations

* State:

  * Trustworthiness issues.
  * Limitations of findings.

### Other

### S20 — Conflicts of Interest

* Identify potential sources of influence/perceived influence.
* Explain how conflicts were managed.

### S21 — Funding

* State:

  * Funding sources.
  * Other support.
  * Role of funders in:

    * Data collection.
    * Interpretation.
    * Reporting.

# COREQ — Consolidated Criteria for Reporting Qualitative Research

* **COREQ** is a checklist for reporting qualitative research, particularly interviews and focus groups.
* It contains **32 items** organized into **3 domains**.

## Domain 1 — Research Team and Reflexivity

### Personal Characteristics

1. **Interviewer/facilitator**

   * Which author(s) conducted the interview or focus group?

2. **Credentials**

   * What were the researcher's credentials?
   * Example:

     * PhD.
     * MD.

3. **Occupation**

   * What was the researcher's occupation at the time of study?

4. **Gender**

   * Was the researcher male or female?

5. **Experience and training**

   * What experience/training did the researcher have?

### Relationship with Participants

6. **Relationship established**

   * Was a relationship established before study commencement?

7. **Participant knowledge of interviewer**

   * What did participants know about the researcher?
   * Examples:

     * Personal goals.
     * Reasons for conducting research.

8. **Interviewer characteristics**

   * What characteristics were reported?
   * Examples:

     * Bias.
     * Assumptions.
     * Reasons/interests in research topic.

## Domain 2 — Study Design

### Theoretical Framework

9. **Methodological orientation and theory**

   * What methodological orientation underpinned the study?
   * Examples:

     * Grounded theory.
     * Discourse analysis.
     * Ethnography.
     * Phenomenology.
     * Content analysis.

### Participant Selection

10. **Sampling**

* How were participants selected?
* Examples:

  * Purposive.
  * Convenience.
  * Consecutive.
  * Snowball.

11. **Method of approach**

* How were participants approached?
* Examples:

  * Face-to-face.
  * Telephone.
  * Mail.
  * Email.

12. **Sample size**

* How many participants were included?

13. **Non-participation**

* How many people:

  * Refused to participate.
  * Dropped out.
* What were the reasons?

### Setting

14. **Setting of data collection**

* Where was data collected?
* Examples:

  * Home.
  * Clinic.
  * Workplace.

15. **Presence of non-participants**

* Was anyone besides participants and researchers present?

16. **Description of sample**

* Important characteristics of sample.
* Examples:

  * Demographic data.
  * Date.

### Data Collection

17. **Interview guide**

* Were questions/prompts/guides provided?
* Was the guide pilot tested?

18. **Repeat interviews**

* Were interviews repeated?
* If yes, how many?

19. **Audio/visual recording**

* Were audio or visual recordings used?

20. **Field notes**

* Were field notes made during/after:

  * Interview.
  * Focus group?

21. **Duration**

* Duration of interviews/focus groups.

22. **Data saturation**

* Was data saturation discussed?

23. **Transcripts returned**

* Were transcripts returned to participants for:

  * Comment.
  * Correction?

## Domain 3 — Analysis and Findings

### Data Analysis

24. **Number of data coders**

* How many coders coded the data?

25. **Description of coding tree**

* Was the coding tree described?

26. **Derivation of themes**

* Were themes:

  * Identified in advance?
  * Derived from the data?

27. **Software**

* What software, if any, was used to manage data?

28. **Participant checking**

* Did participants provide feedback on findings?

### Reporting

29. **Quotations presented**

* Were participant quotations used to illustrate:

  * Themes.
  * Findings?
* Was each quotation identified, e.g. by participant number?

30. **Data and findings consistent**

* Was there consistency between:

  * Data presented.
  * Findings?

31. **Clarity of major themes**

* Were major themes clearly presented?

32. **Clarity of minor themes**

* Were diverse cases/minor themes described or discussed?

# Report Writing in Mixed Methods Research

## Mixed Methods Components in a Report

### Title

* Title should indicate that the study is:

  * A **mixed methods study**.
* Terms such as **mixed methods research** can be included.

### Introduction

May include:

* Deficiency/gap in previous studies.
* Reason for collecting both:

  * Quantitative data.
  * Qualitative data.
* Purpose statement.
* Quantitative research questions.
* Qualitative research questions.
* Mixed methods research question(s).

### Methods

Should begin with:

* Statement that the study uses mixed methods.
* Type of mixed methods design.
* Reasons for selecting the design.
* Examples of other studies using the design.

Should describe:

* Quantitative data collection.
* Qualitative data collection.
* Quantitative analysis.
* Qualitative analysis.
* **Integration procedures**.

## Results in Mixed Methods Research

* Structure depends on the mixed methods design.

### Convergent Design

Results may include:

* Separate quantitative results.
* Separate qualitative results.
* Merged mixed-methods analysis.

If merged analysis is presented:

* Use **joint displays**.
* Joint displays may relate:

  * Qualitative themes.
  * Quantitative variables.

### Sequential Design

Present:

1. Results of first phase.
2. Results of connection procedures.
3. Results of final phase.
4. Steps used to connect and interpret results.

* Joint displays may also be useful.

## Discussion in Mixed Methods Research

* Interpret the results.
* Relate interpretation to:

  * Existing literature.
* Discuss:

  * Limitations.
  * Implications for future research.

### Convergent Design

* Interpretation may focus on **merging data**.
* Compare:

  * Quantitative findings.
  * Qualitative findings.
* Use them together to answer the mixed methods research question.

### Explanatory and Exploratory Sequential Designs

* Interpretation generally follows the:

  * Sequence of data collection.
  * Sequence of analysis.
* Report conclusions answering the mixed methods question.

### Mixed Methods Intervention Designs

* Interpretation focuses primarily on the major findings from the **primary data set**.
* Also explain:

  * How the mixed methods question was answered.

---

# GRAMMS — Good Reporting of a Mixed Methods Study

A good mixed methods report should:

* Explain the **justification** for using mixed methods.
* Describe the design according to:

  * Purpose.
  * Priority.
  * Sequence of methods.
* Describe each method in terms of:

  * Sampling.
  * Data collection.
  * Data analysis.
* Explain:

  * Where integration occurred.
  * How integration occurred.
  * Who participated in integration.
* Describe limitations of one method associated with the presence of the other method.
* Describe insights gained through:

  * Mixing.
  * Integration.

## Research Proposal

* **Introduction:** broad → narrow → research gap/question.
* **Methods:** how study was conducted.

* **Discussion:** what findings mean and how they compare with previous research.
* **Conclusion:** short, direct, actionable.
* **References:** all cited material.
* Tables/figures:

  * Should complement, not duplicate, text.
