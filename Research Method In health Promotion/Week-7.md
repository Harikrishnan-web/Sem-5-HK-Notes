## **Questionnaire / Schedule**

* Contains questions with options, i.e. **forced-choice questions**.
* Each question is called an **item**.
* A questionnaire may have different **segments**.
* It can incorporate:

  * Scales
  * An index
* While preparing a questionnaire, the **method of scoring or analysis** should also be developed.
* Scoring/analysis should be based on the **study objective**.
* The questionnaire needs to be tested for:

  * **Reliability**
  * **Validity**
* The exact testing depends on the structure of the questionnaire. 

---

## **Checklist**

* Contains **predefined items**.
* Items are directly related to:

  * An event
  * An activity
  * A practice
  * A program
* The data collector usually **observes the items**.
* The collector marks whether an item is:

  * Present or not present
  * Yes / No
  * Checkbox
* The **scoring and analysis method** should be developed while designing the checklist. 

---

## **In-depth Interview Guide / Focus Group Discussion Guide**

* Used as a **qualitative assessment tool**.
* Important components include:

  * **Rapport build-up**

    * Introduction of the interviewer
    * Introduction to the topic of interview
  * **Open-ended questions**
  * **Probes**
* Probes are used to **elicit responses from participants**.

* Print materials include:

  * Pamphlets

  * Handouts
* They are generally considered useful for providing a **timely reminder of key communication messages**.
* They are commonly used for **health communication**. 

---

# **Intervention Tools — Online / Digital Materials**

* Online/digital materials for health promotion have become increasingly popular because more people use digital services.
* Common digital materials for health communication and behavior change include:

  * E-flyers
  * Flashcards
  * Flash messages
  * E-banners

# **Posters**

### Definition

* A **poster** is a large sheet of paper containing:

  * Words
  * Pictures
  * Symbols
* These are used to convey a message. 

### Purposes of Posters

* Give **information and advice**.
* Give **directions and instructions**.
* Announce **important events and programs**.
* Posters can be used during:

  * Group methods such as **group discussion**
  * Individual methods such as **counselling**

### Placement of Posters

* Posters should be placed where people are likely to see them.
* Suitable locations include places through which many people pass:

  * Market areas
  * Registration areas in health facilities
* Permission should be obtained before putting posters in:

  * Buildings
  * Houses
* Posters should be **changed regularly** to maintain people's interest. 

---

# **Flipcharts**

* Flipcharts consist of a **number of posters**.
* The posters are shown **one after another**.
* They are used to present several aspects of a topic.
* Flipcharts with **blank sheets** can also be used to:

  * Record ideas
  * Record ideas emerging from group meetings
  * Record ideas from discussions 

---

# **Pamphlets / Brochures / Leaflets / Flyers**

* These are generally a **single piece of paper**.
* The paper can be folded in different ways.
* Information about a health issue or problem is printed on either side.
* Common forms include:

  * **Tri-fold pamphlet/brochure**
  * **Bi-fold brochure**
  * **Leaflets/flyers** 

### Purpose

* Promote:

  * Healthy behavior
  * A health service
* Inform the reader about the **benefits of the item being promoted**.
* Usually contain:

  * Logo of the source
  * Summarized information about the health issue/problem
  * Information about services
* Useful when information needs to be released in a:

  * **Condensed**
  * **Easy-to-read**
  * **Interesting** form.
* Aim to:

  * Capture the reader's attention
  * Extend the reader's knowledge about the specific topic. 

---

## **Key Elements of a Pamphlet / Brochure / Leaflet / Flyer**

* Command attention with the **cover**.
* Use **compelling text**.
* Set the tone with **color**.
* Use the **right font and size**.
* Use **white space strategically**.
* Highlight important information using **boxes**.
* Use **appropriate folds**.
* Make it lively with **photographs**. 

---

# **Flannel Graph**

* A flannel graph consists of:

  * A wooden board
  * Rough flannel cloth or khadi pasted/fixed over the board
* It provides a suitable background for displaying:

  * Cut-out pictures
  * Other illustrations
* The pictures/illustrations have a rough surface at the back.
* Materials used at the back can include:

  * Sandpaper
  * Felt
  * Rough cloth
* These rough surfaces allow the pictures to **adhere to the flannel**. 

---

# **Flash Cards**

* Consist of a series of cards.
* Each card is approximately **10 × 12 inches**.
* Each card contains an illustration related to a:

  * Story
  * Talk
* Cards are arranged in a **sequence**.
* Each card is held in front of the chest and **“flashed” or displayed** before a group while the talk is in progress.
* The message on each card should be:

  * **Brief**
  * **To the point** 

---

# **E-Flyers and Flash Messages — Digital Materials**

* Can be used **digitally**.
* Contain information that directly appeals to and focuses on the topic.
* E-flyers and flash messages are often used on **websites dealing with health issues**.
* Intervention materials can also contain similar digital items. 

---

## **Lecture 1 — Key Points**

* Different types of materials are used for:

  * Health communication intervention

  * Posters
  * Leaflets
  * Handouts

  * Comprehension

# **Steps in Developing a Questionnaire / Interview Schedule**

## **Preliminary Decisions**

* Decide the form the schedule will take:

  * Structured
  * Unstructured
  * Semi-structured
* Decide the **type of information** that needs to be collected.
* List and **operationally define the variables**.
* Develop the pattern of:

  * Coding
  * Recording 

---

## **Drafting the Schedule**

* Wording of every question must be **carefully monitored**.
* Decide the **sequence of questions**. 

---

## **Revising and Pretesting**

* The draft instrument should be **critically discussed by experts**.
* A **pretest** is a trial run of the instrument.
* It is conducted to determine, as far as possible:

  * Clarity
  * Adequacy
  * Freedom from bias
* A pretest **does not provide information about the reliability of the responses** to the questions.
* Psychometric properties should be evaluated for establishing the **reliability of the instrument**. 

---

# **Translation of Tool**

* Translation is performed if required.
* The tool may be translated from **English into the concerned language**.
* One method used to establish reliability is **back-translation**:

  * Translate the tool into the required language.
  * Translate it back into English.
  * Examine the correctness of the first translation. 

---

# **Coding**

* Coding is an **essential part of questionnaire development**.
* Items should be stated so that answers can be classified.
* Example:

  * Yes = **1**
  * No = **2**
* Respondents may be asked to enter the appropriate code.
* Coding makes responses convenient to:

  * Enter into a computer
  * Tally by hand 

---

# **Planning for Scoring**

* Scoring technique should be planned in advance.
* It helps determine:

  * Type of data
  * Possible statistical computations
* Choice of question type depends largely on:

  * Subject matter of the study
  * Verbal ability of respondents
  * Amount of time available 

---

# **Steps in Developing Observation Schedule / Checklist**

* **Select the aspect of behavior to be observed.**
* List categories of behaviors that:

  * May be manifested
  * May not be manifested
* In structured observation, a common method is to construct a **category system**.
* Observed behaviors or characteristics are assigned to the relevant categories.
* **Quantifying observations:**

  * Score **1** = successful completion
  * Score **0** = non-completion
* Develop procedures to facilitate recording.
* Maintain an adequate account of what was observed.
* Record information:

  * During observation
  * And/or immediately after observation is completed. 

---

## **Broad Categories and Coding**

* Sometimes a long list of behaviors can be avoided by listing only **broad categories** on a one-page schedule.
* Each behavior can be coded based on combinations of observation categories.
* The observer needs to **learn the coding system**.
* During recording, the behavior code is entered against each category instead of using a tally. 

---

# **Requirements for Developing a Category System**

* Behaviors and characteristics to be observed should have **careful and explicit definitions**.
* Referent behaviors should be **mutually exclusive**.
* A **coding system** should be developed.
* The system should be applicable to a **wide variety of research situations**.
* A **pilot run** should be conducted to assess suitability for the intended study.
* A new category system should be:

  * Developed
  * Refined
  * Re-tested 

---

# **Characteristics of a Good Questionnaire / Interview Schedule**

* Deals with what is **significant to the topic under study**.
* Seeks information that **cannot be obtained from other sources**.

  * Example: school reports
* Should be **as short as possible**.
* Should be only long enough to obtain essential data.
* Questionnaire should ideally take **not more than 20–25 minutes**.
* Interview should ideally take **not more than 1 hour**.
* Fatigue in respondents should be avoided.
* Should have an:

  * Attractive appearance
  * Neat arrangement
  * Clear duplication/printing 

### Additional Characteristics

* Directions should be:

  * Clear
  * Complete
  * Understandable
* Each item should deal with **a single idea**.
* Questions should be worded:

  * Simply
  * Clearly
* Items should be in good **psychological order**.
* Questions should proceed from:

  * General → Specific
* This is called the **funnel approach**.
* Items should be objective.
* There should be **no leading suggestions** about responses.
* The tool should be easy to:

  * Tabulate
  * Interpret 

---

# **Parts of a Questionnaire**

### Introduction and Instructions

* Provides the necessary introductory information and instructions.

### Personal Data

* May include:

  * Age
  * Sex
  * Education
  * Etc.

### Background Data

* May include:

  * Family income
  * HOF's occupation
  * Environment
  * Sanitation
  * Etc.

### Content Areas

* May cover different constructs such as:

  * Knowledge
  * Opinion
  * Belief
  * Attitude
  * Practices

### Ending

* End with an acknowledgment of participation.
* Example:

  * **“Thank you.”** 

---

## **Lecture 2 — Key Points**

* Questionnaires and checklists are developed through **multiple sequential steps**.
* They should be properly **tested before field implementation**.
* Coding and scoring in quantitative tools are **essential**.
* Coding/scoring should preferably be:

  * Easy to compute
  * Easy to interpret
* A good questionnaire should be:

  * Clear
  * Comprehensive
  * Lucidly worded
  * Time-optimized

---

# **Reliability**

* Reliability concerns the extent to which measurement of a phenomenon provides a **stable and consistent result**.
* Reliability is also concerned with **repeatability**.
* A scale or test is reliable when repeated measurement under constant conditions gives the **same result**. 

### Easy way to remember

* **Reliability = Repeatability / Consistency**

---

# **Validity**

* Validity refers to the ability of an instrument to measure **what it is supposed to measure**. 

### Easy way to remember

* **Validity = Accuracy of measurement of the intended thing**

---

# **Relationship Between Reliability and Validity**

* A test may be **reliable but not valid**.
* A valid test is usually **reliable**.
* Reliability is important but is **not sufficient by itself**.
* Reliability needs to be combined with **validity**. 

### Important exam concept

* **Reliable ≠ necessarily valid**
* **Valid → usually reliable**

---

# **Degree / Measures of Reliability**

## **Coefficient of Stability**

* Refers to consistency of performance on a test **over a period of time**.
* Based on correlation between:

  * Initial test
  * Retest after a distinct interval
* Also called **Test-Retest Reliability**. 

### Remember

* **Stability → Time**

---

## **Coefficient of Equivalence**

* Based on correlation between performances on **different forms of a test**.
* The different forms are administered at nearly the same time.
* One is the original test version and the other is an **equivalent form**. 

### Remember

* **Equivalence → Different forms**

---

## **Coefficient of Internal Consistency**

* Refers to consistency of performance across:

  * Different parts
  * Different items
* Measured when the test is taken at a **single sitting**.
* Examples include:

  * **Cronbach's alpha**
  * **Split-half correlation** 

### Remember

* **Internal consistency → Items within the same test**

---

# **Internal Consistency Reliability**

* A scale has high internal consistency reliability when its items:

  * Appear to fit together
  * Collectively measure the **same construct**
* Most commonly used measure:

  * **Cronbach's Alpha coefficient**
* Cronbach's alpha is based on **inter-item correlation**.
* It is the most appropriate reliability measure for **Likert scales**.
* A minimum internal consistency coefficient of **0.70** is recommended. 

### ⭐ Important number

* **Cronbach's Alpha ≥ 0.70 → recommended minimum internal consistency**

---

# **Common Measures of Reliability**

### Test-Retest Reliability

* Scores are consistent over at least **two distinct points in time**.

### Internal Reliability

* Refers to the **average inter-item correlations** for a scale.

### Cronbach's Alpha

* A statistical measure of **internal reliability**.

### Split-Half Method

* Items from a scale are randomly assigned into sets.
* Scores from one set are correlated with scores from the other set. 

---

# **Types / Measures of Validity**

* **Face validity**
* **Content validity**
* **Construct validity**
* **Criterion validity** 

---

# **Face Validity**

* Face validity is a **subjective judgment** on the operationalization of a construct.
* It refers to the degree to which a measure **appears to be related** to a specific construct.
* Judgment can come from:

  * Experts
  * Non-experts / test takers
* It evaluates the appearance of a questionnaire in terms of:

  * Feasibility
  * Readability
  * Consistency of style
  * Formatting
  * Clarity of language 

### Remember

* **Face validity = Does the tool look appropriate on the surface?**
* It is **subjective**.

---

# **Content Validity**

* Refers to the degree to which items in an instrument reflect the **content universe** to which the instrument will be generalized.
* It evaluates whether a new survey instrument:

  * Includes all essential items
  * Eliminates undesirable items
* The evaluation is done in relation to a particular **construct**. 

### Remember

* **Content validity = Are all important parts of the construct covered?**

---

# **Steps to Apply Content Validity**

### Step 1 — Literature Review

* Conduct an **exhaustive literature review**.
* Extract relevant/related items.

### Step 2 — Content Validity Survey

* Generate a content validity survey.
* Each item is assessed using a **3-point scale**:

  * Not necessary
  * Useful but not essential
  * Essential

### Step 3 — Experts

* Send the survey to **experts in the same research field**.

### Step 4 — Calculate CVR

* Calculate the **Content Validity Ratio (CVR)** for each item.
* Use **Lawshe's method**.

### Step 5 — Eliminate Items

* Items that are not significant at the critical level are **eliminated**. 

---

## **Lecture 3 — Key Points**

* **Reliability** → repeatability of an instrument.
* **Validity** → how well the instrument measures what it is supposed to measure.
* **Cronbach's alpha** → most commonly used internal consistency reliability estimate.
* **Face validity** → subjective measure.
* **Content validity** → usually measured using **Content Validity Ratio (CVR)**.

# **Construct Validity**

* Construct validity refers to how well a:

  * Concept
  * Idea
  * Behavior
* that represents a construct is **translated/transformed into a functioning and operating reality**.
* This process is called **operationalization**.
* Construct validity is understood through:

  * **Convergent validity**
  * **Discriminant validity** 

---

# **Discriminant Validity**

* Refers to the extent to which one **latent variable** can discriminate from other latent variables.
* Example:

  * Latent variable A should discriminate from B, C, D.
* A latent variable should account for more variance in its associated observed variables than:

  * Measurement error
  * Similar external/unmeasured influences
  * Other constructs within the conceptual framework.
* Also called **divergent validity**.
* Tests whether constructs that **should have no relationship actually have no relationship**. 

### Remember

* **Discriminant = Different**
* If two constructs should be unrelated → they should actually show **no relationship**.

---

# **Convergent Validity**

* Very commonly used in **behavior studies**.
* Refers to the degree to which two measures of constructs that theoretically should be related are **actually related**.
* Tests whether constructs expected to be related are, in fact, **related**. 

### Remember

* **Convergent = Come together**
* Related constructs should show a relationship.

---

# **Measurement of Construct Validity**

* Factor analysis can be conducted using:

  * **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**
  * With an appropriate rotation method
* Items with loading **above 0.40** are considered for further analysis.
* **0.40** is the minimum recommended value mentioned in the lecture.
* Items with **cross-loading above 0.40** should be deleted. 

### Criteria for Discriminant Validity

* Loading should be at least **0.40**.
* There should be **no cross-loading above 0.40**.

### Criteria for Convergent Validity

* Eigenvalue of **1**
* Loading of at least **0.40**
* Items should load on the **posited constructs**. 

### ⭐ Important numbers

* **Factor loading ≥ 0.40**
* **Cross-loading > 0.40 → delete**
* **Eigenvalue = 1** for the stated convergent validity criterion.

---

# **Criterion Validity**

* Also called **concrete validity**.
* Refers to the extent to which a measure is related to an **outcome**.
* Measures how well one measure **predicts an outcome for another measure**.
* A tool has criterion validity when it is useful for predicting:

  * Performance
  * Behavior
* in another situation that may be:

  * Past
  * Present
  * Future 

### Types of Criterion Validity

* **Concurrent validity**
* **Predictive validity**
* **Postdictive validity**

---

# **Predictive Validity**

* A survey is predictively valid if the test **accurately predicts what it is supposed to predict**.
* Scores from the **predictor measure are taken first**.
* Criterion data are collected **later**.
* It assesses whether the operationalization of a construct can predict something it should theoretically be able to predict.
* It is the ability of one assessment tool to predict:

  * Future performance in an activity
  * Another assessment of the same construct
* It can take a **long time**. 

### Example from the lecture

* Administer a **behavioral intention test** to participants.
* Later observe their **future practice of the behavior**.
* Examine whether the test scores correlate with future behavior. 

### Remember

* **Predictive = Future**

---

# **Concurrent Validity**

* Refers to the extent to which results from a measurement correspond to those of an **established measurement for the same construct**.
* It assesses whether the operationalization can distinguish between groups that it should theoretically distinguish between.
* It provides evidence that can be used to defend a test for **predicting other outcomes**. 

### Remember

* **Concurrent = Same/current time**

---

# **Postdictive Validity**

* In this type of validity, the **criterion is in the past**.
* The criterion, such as another test, was administered in the past.
* It is a form of **criterion-referenced validity**.
* It is determined by the degree to which scores on a given test are related to scores on another:

  * Already established test
  * Criterion
* that was administered at a **previous point in time**. 

### Remember

* **Postdictive = Past**

---

# **Two-Step Psychometric Process**

* The lecture presents a **two-step psychometric process for reliability and validity of scales and indexes**.
* The key sequence stated in the lecture is:

  * **First test reliability**
  * **Then examine validity** 

### ⭐ Very Important

**Reliability → Validity**

---

## **Lecture 4 — Key Points**

* **Construct validity**

  * Measures operationalization.
  * Understood through convergent and discriminant validity.
* **Criterion validity**

  * Measures the relationship between a measure and an outcome.
  * Includes:

    * Concurrent
    * Predictive
    * Postdictive
* For establishing psychometric validity:

  * **First test reliability**
  * **Then examine validity**

# **Observations**

### Complete Participant

* Researcher **conceals the researcher's role**.
* Researcher participates completely as a **participant**.

* Researcher **only observes**.
* Researcher does **not participate**. 

### ⭐ Difference

* **Complete participant → participates**
* **Complete observer → only observes**

---

# **Interviews**

### Types

* **Face-to-face, one-on-one, in-person interview**
* **Telephone interview**

  * Researcher interviews participants by phone.
* **Focus groups**

  * Researcher interviews participants in a group.
* **E-mail internet interview** 

---

# **Documents**

### Public Documents

* Minutes of meetings
* Newspapers

### Private Documents

* Journals
* Diaries
* Letters

# **Audiovisual / Digital Materials**

* Photographs
* Videotapes
* Art objects
* Computer messages
* Sounds
* Films

# **Observation Protocol**

* The researcher should plan to **develop and use a protocol** for recording observations in a qualitative study.
* Researchers often conduct **multiple observations** during a qualitative study.
* An observational protocol is used to record information while observing. 

### Structure of Observation Protocol

* It may be a **single page**.
* A dividing line can separate two types of notes:

### Descriptive Notes

* Portraits of participants
* Reconstruction of dialogue
* Description of physical setting
* Accounts of particular events
* Accounts of activities

### Reflexive Notes

* Researcher's personal thoughts, such as:

  * Speculation
  * Feelings
  * Problems
  * Ideas
  * Hunches
  * Impressions
  * Prejudices

### Other Information

* Demographic/field-setting information may include:

  * Time
  * Place
  * Date
* These indicate when and where the observation takes place. 

---

# **Interview Protocol**

* Plan to develop and use an interview protocol for:

  * Asking questions
  * Recording answers
* Researchers can record information using:

  * Handwritten notes
  * Audio recording
  * Video recording
* Even if an interview is recorded, researchers should **take notes**.
* This is useful if the recording equipment fails.
* If audiotaping is used, the researcher should plan **transcription in advance**. 

---

# **Length and Structure of Interview Protocol**

* Interview protocol should be about **two pages** long.
* There should be spaces between questions.
* These spaces allow the interviewer to write:

  * Short notes
  * Quotes
* This is particularly useful if the audio-recording device does not work.
* Total number of questions should generally be **5–10**.
* However, there is **no precise number** of questions.
* Protocol should:

  * Be prepared in advance
  * Be used consistently across interviews 

---

# **Components of an Interview Protocol**

* **Basic information about the interview**
* **Introduction**
* **Interview content questions with probes**
* **Closing instructions** 

---

# **Basic Information of the Interview**

Should include:

* Time of interview
* Date of interview
* Location where interview took place
* Name of interviewer
* Name of interviewee
* Project length of the interview may also be noted.
* File name for:

  * Digital audio recording
  * Transcriptions 

---

# **Introduction**

* Provides instructions to the interviewer.
* Helps ensure that useful information is **not overlooked**.
* Interviewer should:

  * Introduce himself/herself.
  * Discuss the purpose of the study.
* The introduction should contain a prompt to collect the **signed informed consent form**.
* It should explain the general structure of the interview:

  * How it will begin
  * Number of questions
  * Expected time
* Interviewee should be asked whether they have **any questions before the interview begins**. 

---

# **Opening Question**

* The first step is to make the interviewee **feel at ease**.
* Usually begins with an **ice-breaker type question**.
* Participants may be asked to talk about themselves.
* The lecture specifically states that **personal questions are not asked** as the opening question. 

---

# **Content Questions**

* Content questions are the **research sub-questions** in the study.
* They ask about different facets of the **central phenomenon**.
* After the interviewee answers all the sub-questions, the qualitative researcher should have a good understanding of how the **central research question has been answered**. 

---

# **Using Probes**

* Content questions should include **probes**.
* Probes act as reminders for the researcher.
* Two purposes of probes:

  * Ask for **more information**
  * Ask for an **explanation of ideas**
* Probes can:

  * Extend the duration of an interview
  * Help obtain useful information 

---

# **Closing Instructions**

* Thank the interviewee for their **time**.
* Respond to any **final questions**.
* Assure the interviewee about the **confidentiality** of the interview.
* Ask whether another follow-up interview can be conducted if clarification of certain points is needed. 

---

## **Lecture 5 — Key Points**

* Researchers often conduct **multiple observations** in qualitative studies.
* An **observational protocol** is used to record information during observations.
* An **interview protocol** contains:

  * Basic interview information
  * Introduction
  * Content questions with probes
  * Closing instructions

## **Lecture 3 → Reliability + Face/Content Validity**

* Reliability = **repeatability**
* Validity = **measures what it should**
* Reliability measures:

  * Stability

  * Internal consistency

## **Lecture 4 → Construct + Criterion Validity**

* Construct validity → operationalization
* **Convergent** → constructs expected to relate actually relate
* **Discriminant** → constructs expected to differ/no relation actually differ/no relation
* Factor loading → **≥ 0.40**
* Cross-loading **> 0.40 → delete**
* Criterion validity:

  * Concurrent
  * Predictive
  * Postdictive
* Predictive → **future**
* Concurrent → **established/current comparison**
* Postdictive → **past**
* Psychometric sequence:

  * **Reliability → Validity**
