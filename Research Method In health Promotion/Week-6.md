## Types of Research Studies

* There are three broad types of research studies:

  * Quantitative research
  * Qualitative research
  * Mixed-method research
* Qualitative and quantitative studies should not be viewed as rigid, completely distinct categories or dichotomies.
* They can be understood as different ends of a continuum.
* Mixed-method studies lie in the middle of this continuum.
* Mixed-method research integrates elements of both qualitative and quantitative studies.
* One type of research is not necessarily better than another.
* One type is also not necessarily more scientific or more rigorous than another.
* Quantitative and qualitative approaches are compatible and complementary.
* Using both approaches can allow researchers to capture diverse forms of data.
* Combining diverse forms of data can provide a more contextually rich and complete understanding of a research problem. 

## Choosing the Type of Research Study

* The decision about which type of study should be used depends on:

  * The nature of the research problem
  * The research questions
  * The research design
* Simply using multiple methods for data collection does not automatically make a study a mixed-method study.
* Multi-informant studies are also not necessarily mixed-method designs.
* For a study to be considered a true mixed-method study, there must be genuine integration of qualitative and quantitative data.
* This integration must occur at one or more stages of the research process.
* The central assumption of mixed-method research is that integrating qualitative and quantitative data can produce additional insight.
* The resulting insight should go beyond what could be obtained from either quantitative or qualitative data alone. 

## Construction of Scientific Knowledge

* Scientific knowledge is constructed through a systematic research process.
* The researcher identifies a problem and views it within its broadest context.
* An objective approach relies specifically on scientific evidence.
* Scientific evidence can include:

  * Experiments
  * Statistics
* The research process may move from data to theory.
* It may also move from the specific to the general.
* The researcher studies what other researchers have already done.
* Existing theories related to the phenomenon being studied are examined.
* Hypotheses emerging from existing theories can then be tested.
* The research process may also begin with:

  * Surprising facts
  * Puzzles
* The research process is then devoted to explaining these surprising facts or puzzles. 

## Research Question

* A research question is closely connected to the aim and objectives of the research.
* Research questions are used to obtain the information needed to address the research aim.
* Mixed-method research answers research questions through a combination and **integration** of qualitative and quantitative data. 

## Examples of Health Promotion Problems

* Overweight among young people
* Malnutrition among children under five years
* Unmet needs for family planning
* Vaccine hesitancy 

## Research Questions and Aims/Objectives

* Research questions are based on the aims and objectives of the research.
* The answer to each research question should provide information that helps meet the aim of the research.
* Examples of research questions related to obesity among adolescents include:

  * How do adolescents view the health risks of obesity?
  * What are the relevant social or subjective norms regarding obesity in their social environment? 

## When to Choose a Mixed-Method Study

* Mixed-method research can be selected when results from one method need to be elaborated or clarified using the results from another method.
* It can be used to check the accuracy or validity of databases.
* It is useful for finding answers to:

  * Paradoxes
  * Contradictions
  * New perspectives
* Different methods can be used to answer different research questions.
* Quantitative and qualitative research may each be better suited to answering particular questions.
* Qualitative methods can be used to develop instruments for quantitative research.
* Examples include developing:

  * Questionnaire items
  * Scale items
* One method can facilitate the sampling of respondents or cases used in another method.
* Qualitative methods can provide contextual understanding of:

  * Findings obtained through quantitative research
  * Relationships among variables identified through a quantitative survey
* Mixed-method research can be used for **triangulation**.
* Triangulation involves:

  * **Convergence** — results from different methods coming together
  * **Corroboration** — confirmation of findings
  * **Correspondence** — similarity of results obtained through different methods
* Both quantitative and qualitative methods have their own strengths and weaknesses.
* Combining the two approaches can:

  * Offset the weaknesses of each method
  * Draw on the strengths of both methods
  * Produce a more complete understanding of the research problem 

## Key Points

* There are three broad types of research studies:

  * Quantitative
  * Qualitative
  * Mixed-method
* Mixed-method research lies between quantitative and qualitative approaches on the research continuum.
* It integrates elements of both approaches.
* The choice of research type depends on the research problem, research questions and research design.
* Merely combining several data-collection methods does not constitute mixed-method research.
* A true mixed-method study requires genuine integration of qualitative and quantitative data at one or more stages of the research process.
* Integration should generate additional insight beyond what either approach could provide independently.
* Mixed methods can be used for:

  * Elaboration
  * Clarification
  * Checking validity
  * Resolving paradoxes and contradictions
  * Developing new perspectives
  * Using different methods for different research questions
  * Instrument development
  * Sampling facilitation
  * Providing contextual understanding
  * Triangulation
* Triangulation involves convergence, corroboration and correspondence of findings.

## Convergent Design

* The convergent design is a **mixed methods research design**.
* In this design, the researcher:

  * Collects **quantitative data**.
  * Collects **qualitative data**.
  * Analyses the two databases separately.
  * Then merges the two databases.
  * Compares or combines the results.
* The main intention is to obtain **different but complementary types of data about the same topic**.
* It is particularly useful when the researcher wants to:

  * Compare quantitative statistical results with qualitative findings.
  * Obtain a more complete understanding of the research problem.
  * Corroborate findings.
  * Validate findings.
  * Use qualitative findings to illustrate quantitative results.
  * Use quantitative results to support or illustrate qualitative findings.
  * Examine relationships among variables.
* The essential components are:

  * **Quantitative**
  * **Qualitative**
  * **Point of integration** 

## Basic Structure of Convergent Design

* **Quantitative data collection and analysis** occur as one component.
* **Qualitative data collection and analysis** occur as another component.
* The results from both components are brought together.
* The results are **merged and compared**.
* The researcher then performs **interpretation** based on the combined findings.
* The key idea is that quantitative and qualitative evidence are initially handled separately but are eventually brought together at the point of integration. 

## Choice of the Convergent Design

* The convergent design can be selected when the researcher wants to compare quantitative and qualitative findings to obtain a better understanding of a problem.
* It is particularly useful when:

  * The researcher has **limited time for field data collection**.
  * Both types of data need to be collected during **one field visit**.
  * The researcher needs both quantitative and qualitative information from **every participant**.
  * The researcher has skills in both quantitative and qualitative research methods.
  * The mixed methods research team contains individuals with expertise in both quantitative and qualitative research.
* Therefore, the design can be especially practical when both forms of information are required within the same phase of research. 

## Convergent Design Procedures

* There are **four major steps** in the convergent design.

### Collection of Quantitative and Qualitative Data

* The researcher collects:

  * Quantitative data.
  * Qualitative data.
* Both types of data concern the **same topic of interest**.
* Data collection is **concurrent**, meaning both types of data are collected during approximately the same phase.
* The data collection processes are typically **separate**.
* One type of data collection does not normally depend on the results of the other.
* Thus, the quantitative and qualitative components can proceed independently during data collection. 

### Separate Analysis of the Two Data Sets

* After collection, the two data sets are analyzed **separately and independently**.
* Quantitative data are analyzed using quantitative analytical procedures.
* Qualitative data are analyzed using qualitative analytical procedures.
* Each type of data therefore retains the analytical approach traditionally associated with it. 

### Point of Interface and Merging

* After the initial analyses are completed, the researcher reaches the **point of interface**.
* At this stage, the results from the two data sets are brought together.
* The researcher **merges the quantitative and qualitative results**.
* This is the important integration stage of the convergent design. 

### Interpretation of the Merged Results

* The researcher interprets:

  * The extent to which quantitative and qualitative results **converge**.
  * The ways in which they **converge**.
  * The extent to which they **diverge**.
  * The ways in which they relate to one another.
  * How the combined findings can produce a better understanding of the research problem.
* Interpretation is made in relation to the **overall purpose of the study**.
* If the quantitative and qualitative findings diverge:

  * The researcher must investigate the reason for the difference.
  * The researcher may re-examine the results.
  * Additional data may be collected.
  * The quality of the databases may be reconsidered.
* Divergence is therefore not necessarily a failure. Differences between findings may provide **new insights** into the research topic. 

## Strengths of the Convergent Design

* Researchers who are new to mixed methods research often choose the convergent design.
* It was the design **first discussed in the mixed methods literature**.
* It has become a popular approach for understanding and conducting mixed methods research.
* It is an **efficient design**.
* Both quantitative and qualitative data can be collected during **one phase of the research** and at approximately the same time.
* Each type of data can be:

  * Collected separately.
  * Analyzed separately.
  * Analyzed independently using the techniques traditionally associated with that type of data.
* This makes the design suitable for **team research**.
* A research team can include:

  * Researchers with quantitative expertise.
  * Researchers with qualitative expertise.
* The design also facilitates direct comparison between different perspectives.
* For example:

  * Participants' perspectives can be obtained through **open-ended questioning**, such as a semi-structured interview.
  * Researchers' perspectives can be obtained through a structured instrument such as a survey.
* Comparing these different perspectives can provide a broader understanding of the research problem. 

## Challenges in Using the Convergent Design

### Different Sample Sizes

* Quantitative and qualitative components may have **different sample sizes**.
* Researchers must consider the consequences of having different samples when merging the two data sets.
* Differences in sample size can affect how the findings are compared and integrated. 

### Merging Textual and Numerical Databases

* Quantitative and qualitative data can be fundamentally different in form.
* Quantitative data are generally **numerical**.
* Qualitative data are generally **textual or descriptive**.
* Combining these two very different types of databases and their results in a meaningful way can be challenging.
* The researcher needs an appropriate strategy for integrating findings without losing the meaning of either data set. 

### Explaining Divergent Results

* Quantitative and qualitative findings may not always agree.
* When the findings differ, researchers need to determine why.
* Contradictory results can potentially reveal new insights.
* However, explaining and resolving these differences can be difficult.
* Additional data collection may sometimes be necessary to understand the divergence. 

## Key Points to Remember

* Convergent design is a **mixed methods design**.
* It involves both **quantitative and qualitative data**.
* Quantitative and qualitative data are collected during approximately the **same phase**.
* The two data sets are initially **analyzed separately**.
* The results are then **merged and compared**.
* The point where the two sets of results are brought together is the **point of interface/integration**.
* The purpose is to obtain **different but complementary information about the same topic**.
* It can be used for:

  * Comparison.
  * Corroboration.
  * Validation.
  * Illustration of findings.
  * Examination of relationships among variables.
* Divergence between findings must be investigated rather than simply ignored.
* Major challenges include:

  * Different sample sizes.
  * Difficulty merging textual and numerical data.
  * Difficulty explaining contradictory findings.

* Saha I, Paul B. *Essentials of Biostatistics & Research Methodology*. Third Edition, 2021, Academic Publishers.

# The Explanatory Sequential Design

* **Course:** Research Methods in Health Promotion
* **Lecturer:** Dr. Sweety Suman Jha, MBBS, MD
* **Designation:** Senior Resident (Community Medicine)
* **Institution:** Dr. B.C. Roy Multi-Speciality Medical Research Centre, Indian Institute of Technology Kharagpur
* **Email:** [ssjha@bcrmrc.iitkgp.ac.in](mailto:ssjha@bcrmrc.iitkgp.ac.in) 

## Overview of the Explanatory Sequential Design

* The explanatory sequential design is a **mixed methods design**.
* In this design, the researcher:

  * Begins with a **quantitative phase**.
  * Follows the quantitative phase with a **qualitative phase**.
  * Uses the qualitative phase to help explain specific quantitative results.
* It involves a **two-phase data collection project**:

  * First, quantitative data are collected.
  * The quantitative data are then analyzed.
  * The results of the quantitative phase are used to plan or build the second phase.
  * The second phase involves qualitative data collection and analysis.
* The key idea is that **qualitative data collection builds directly on the quantitative results**.
* The qualitative phase is conducted to explain the initial quantitative findings in greater depth.
* The term **“explanatory”** reflects the purpose of the qualitative phase:

  * Qualitative findings help explain the quantitative results.
  * They provide deeper insight into the findings obtained during the first phase.
* The **primary intent** of the explanatory sequential design is to use a **qualitative strand to explain initial quantitative results**.
* The qualitative phase can help explain the **mechanisms behind quantitative findings**.
* It can provide insight into:

  * Why particular quantitative results occurred.
  * How those results can be understood or explained. 

## Basic Flow of the Explanatory Sequential Design

* **Quantitative data collection and analysis**
  ↓
* **Interpretation**
  ↓
* **Qualitative data collection and analysis**
  ↓
* **Results are connected to and explained by the qualitative findings**. 

## Choice of the Explanatory Sequential Design

* The explanatory sequential design is particularly useful when:

* **The researcher and research problem are quantitatively oriented**

  * If the researcher is more comfortable with or focused on quantitative research, it makes sense to begin with the quantitative phase.

* **The important variables are already known**

  * The researcher knows which variables are important to the research problem.
  * The researcher also has access to quantitative instruments for measuring the constructs of primary interest.

* **The researcher can return to participants**

  * The researcher should have the ability to contact or return to the participants for a second round of qualitative data collection.

* **There is sufficient time**

  * The researcher needs enough time to conduct the research in two separate phases.

* **Resources are limited**

  * This design can be useful when resources are limited.
  * It may be particularly suitable when the researcher is the sole investigator.
  * Only one type of data is collected and analyzed at a time, which can make the research more manageable. 

## Explanatory Sequential Design Procedures

* **First phase – Quantitative phase**

  * The researcher designs and implements the quantitative phase.
  * Quantitative data are collected.
  * The collected quantitative data are analyzed. 

* **Second phase – Connecting to the qualitative phase**

  * After the quantitative results are obtained, the researcher identifies specific quantitative findings that require additional explanation.
  * This is the **point of integration for mixing**.
  * The identified quantitative results guide the development of the qualitative strand.
  * Therefore, the qualitative phase does not operate independently of the quantitative phase.
  * It is developed based on what was learned from the quantitative results. 

* **Development of the qualitative phase**

  * The researcher develops or refines:

    * Qualitative research questions.
    * Purposeful sampling procedures.
    * Data collection protocols.
  * These are developed so that they follow directly from the quantitative results.
  * The qualitative phase is therefore **connected to and dependent on the quantitative findings**. 

* **Third phase – Qualitative implementation**

  * The researcher implements the qualitative phase.
  * Qualitative data are collected.
  * Qualitative data are analyzed.
  * The researcher then interprets:

    * To what extent the qualitative results explain the quantitative results.
    * In what ways the qualitative results explain the quantitative findings.
    * What additional insights the qualitative results provide.
    * What is learned overall in response to the purpose of the study. 

## Point of Integration

* The **point of integration** is where the quantitative and qualitative strands are connected.
* Specific quantitative findings are selected because they require further explanation.
* Those findings then guide:

  * The qualitative research questions.
  * Purposeful sampling.
  * Data collection procedures.
* Thus, the quantitative results directly influence the design of the qualitative phase.
* The final interpretation brings the two strands together to understand the research problem more completely. 

## Strengths of the Explanatory Sequential Design

* The explanatory design has many advantages.

* These advantages make it one of the **most straightforward mixed methods designs**. 

* **Appeals to quantitative researchers**

  * The design often begins with a strong quantitative orientation.
  * Therefore, researchers who prefer quantitative approaches may find it suitable. 

* **Straightforward to implement**

  * The two phases are conducted separately:

    * Quantitative first.
    * Qualitative second.
  * Only one type of data is collected at a time.
  * This makes the design manageable, particularly for a single researcher. 

* **Straightforward reporting**

  * The final research report can contain:

    * A quantitative section.
    * Followed by a qualitative section.
  * This makes the report easier to write.
  * It also provides a clear distinction between the two strands for readers. 

* **Supports emergent approaches**

  * The second phase can be designed according to what is learned from the initial quantitative phase.
  * Therefore, the qualitative phase can emerge and develop from the quantitative findings rather than being completely fixed beforehand. 

## Challenges in Using the Explanatory Sequential Design

* Although the explanatory design is relatively straightforward, researchers must anticipate challenges that are specific to this approach. 

* **Extended time needed for completion**

  * The design requires a considerable amount of time because there are two research phases.
  * The researcher must implement both:

    * Quantitative research.
    * Qualitative research.
  * Participants also need to remain accessible for an extended period.
  * This can make the overall research process lengthy. 

* **The qualitative phase cannot be fully specified in advance**

  * It may be difficult to completely specify the qualitative phase before the quantitative results are available.
  * This creates difficulties when obtaining **Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval**.
  * The researcher may not be able to specify precisely:

    * Which participants will be selected for the second phase.
    * Which questions will be asked during the follow-up qualitative phase.
  * These decisions depend on the findings obtained from the initial quantitative phase. 

* **Quantitative results requiring follow-up must be identified**

  * The researcher has to determine which quantitative results need further explanation.
  * This cannot be determined with complete precision until the quantitative phase has been completed.
  * However, potential options can be considered while planning the study.
  * Possible results to follow up may include:

    * **Significant results**
    * **Strong predictors** 

* **Need to determine who can best provide the explanation**

  * The researcher must decide who should participate in the second, qualitative phase.
  * Appropriate criteria for selecting these participants must also be determined.
  * The selected participants should be capable of providing meaningful explanations for the quantitative findings. 

## Key Points to Remember

* The **explanatory sequential design is a mixed methods design**.
* It begins with **quantitative research** and is followed by **qualitative research**.
* The qualitative phase is specifically used to **explain the quantitative results**.
* Quantitative results guide the development of the qualitative phase.
* The qualitative research questions, purposeful sampling and data collection protocols can be developed or refined based on the quantitative findings.
* The **point of integration** occurs when specific quantitative results are identified for further explanation and are used to guide the qualitative strand.
* The design is particularly useful when:

  * The researcher and research problem are quantitatively oriented.
  * Important variables are known.
  * Appropriate quantitative measurement instruments are available.
  * Participants can be contacted again.
  * There is sufficient time for two phases.
  * Resources are limited and only one type of data needs to be handled at a time.
* Major strengths include:

  * Strong quantitative orientation.
  * Straightforward implementation.
  * Manageability for individual researchers.
  * Clear reporting structure.
  * Ability to develop the second phase from initial findings.
* Major challenges include:

  * Longer completion time.
  * Need for continued participant accessibility.
  * Difficulty fully specifying the qualitative phase in advance.
  * Potential difficulties with IRB approval.
  * Need to determine which quantitative findings require explanation.
  * Need to determine which participants can best explain those findings.

* Saha I, Paul B. *Essentials of Biostatistics & Research Methodology*. 3rd Edition. 2021. Academic Publishers.

# The Exploratory Sequential Design

* **Course:** Research Methods in Health Promotion
* **Lecturer:** Dr. Sweety Suman Jha, MBBS, MD
* **Designation:** Senior Resident (Community Medicine)
* **Institution:** Dr. B.C. Roy Multi-Speciality Medical Research Centre, Indian Institute of Technology Kharagpur

## Overview of the Exploratory Sequential Design

* The **exploratory sequential design** is a **three-phase mixed methods design**.
* The researcher begins with:

  * **Collection of qualitative data**
  * **Analysis of qualitative data**
* This is followed by a **development phase**.
* During the development phase:

  * Findings from the qualitative phase are translated into an **approach or tool**.
  * The developed approach or tool is then **tested quantitatively**. 
* The basic idea is:

  * **Explore first → Develop → Test quantitatively**
* The approach or tool developed is grounded in the **views of participants**.
* The emphasis on **exploring before development** is the reason for the name *exploratory sequential design*. 

### Intermediate Instrument Development

* In many applications of this iterative design, the researcher develops an **instrument** between the qualitative and quantitative phases.
* This instrument:

  * Is based on the **qualitative results**.
  * Acts as an intermediate product between the two phases.
  * Is subsequently used for **quantitative data collection**. 
* The development phase does not necessarily have to produce only an instrument.
* Researchers may also develop:

  * New variables
  * New measures
  * A survey
  * New experimental activities
  * An app
  * A digital tool 

### Primary Intent

* The primary intent of the exploratory design is to **develop and apply** a quantitative feature that is grounded in qualitative data.
* Possible products include:

  * **Quantitative measure**
  * **Survey**
  * **Intervention**
  * **Digital tool**
  * **New variables** 
* The quantitative feature is based on the **culture or setting of the participants**.
* Because the measure or instrument is developed in a culture-specific manner:

  * It is more likely to be relevant to the group being studied.
  * The participants' own views and context contribute to the development of the quantitative feature. 

## Basic Flow of the Exploratory Sequential Design

* The overall sequence is:

  **Qualitative data collection and analysis**
  ↓
  **Interpretation**
  ↓
  **Development of quantitative measure / instrument / intervention / app / website**
  ↓
  **Quantitative data collection and analysis**
  ↓
  **Results connected to and build on the qualitative findings** 

* Therefore, the design moves from a **qualitative exploration** toward a **quantitative testing/application phase**.

* The quantitative component is not developed independently.

* Instead, it is built from what was learned during the qualitative phase.

## Choice of the Exploratory Sequential Design

* The exploratory sequential design is most useful when the following conditions are present:

### Researcher and Problem Are Qualitatively Oriented

* The researcher and research problem are more **qualitatively oriented**.
* Therefore, it makes sense to begin with a more **inductive approach**.
* The researcher first explores the phenomenon rather than beginning with predefined quantitative measurements. 

### Need to Develop a Product

* The researcher needs to develop a product that is:

  * **Substantively relevant**
  * **Culturally sensitive**
* Examples of products include:

  * An instrument
  * Intervention materials
  * A digital tool 
* Qualitative findings help ensure that the product reflects the experiences and context of the target participants.

### Sufficient Time Is Available

* The researcher must have enough time to conduct the study in **three phases**:

  * Qualitative phase
  * Development phase
  * Quantitative phase 
* Since the design is sequential, one phase generally depends on what is learned or produced in the previous phase.

### Interest in Transferability and Generalizability

* The researcher may choose this design when interested in the:

  * **Transferability**
  * **Generalizability**
* This is particularly relevant for a **newly developed product**. 
* Qualitative research helps develop the product in the relevant context.
* Quantitative testing with a larger sample can then examine how broadly the findings or product apply.

### Emergent Research Questions

* Small-sample qualitative results may reveal **new or emerging research questions**.
* These questions may be better tested using a **large quantitative sample**.
* Thus, exploratory sequential design can move from:

  * Small-scale qualitative exploration
  * Identification of important or emerging issues
  * Large-scale quantitative testing 

## Exploratory Sequential Design Procedures

### Qualitative Phase

* The design begins with the **collection and analysis of qualitative data**.
* The purpose is to **explore a phenomenon**.
* The qualitative phase provides the initial understanding from which the later quantitative feature will be developed. 

### Point of Integration

* The **point of integration** occurs after the qualitative results are available.
* At this stage, the researcher identifies the qualitative results on which the **quantitative feature will be built**. 
* This is the important connection between:

  * The initial qualitative strand
  * The later quantitative strand

### Development Phase

* After identifying the relevant qualitative findings, the researcher undertakes a **development phase**.
* During this phase, the researcher may:

  * Develop an instrument.
  * Identify variables.
  * Design intervention or experimental activities.
  * Develop an app.
  * Develop a website intervention. 
* The development is therefore directly informed by the qualitative findings.

### Connection Between Qualitative and Quantitative Strands

* The developments produced during the development phase connect:

  * The **initial qualitative phase**
  * The **subsequent quantitative strand** of the study. 
* This means the quantitative phase is based on what was discovered during qualitative exploration.

### Quantitative Phase

* In the third step, the researcher implements the **quantitative strand**.
* The purpose is to examine the **salient variables**.
* The developed:

  * Instrument
  * Intervention
  * Or other quantitative feature
    is used with a **new sample of participants**. 
* The quantitative phase therefore allows the researcher to test or apply what was developed from the qualitative findings.

### Final Interpretation

* Finally, the researcher interprets:

  * **In what ways** the quantitative results generalize or extend the qualitative findings.
  * **To what extent** the quantitative results generalize or extend the initial qualitative findings. 
* The final interpretation therefore brings the two strands together.

## Strengths of the Exploratory Sequential Design

### Straightforward Structure

* The separate phases make the exploratory sequential design:

  * Straightforward to **describe**
  * Straightforward to **implement**
  * Straightforward to **report** 

### Makes Qualitative Research More Acceptable

* The design typically emphasizes the **qualitative aspect**.
* However, including a quantitative component can make the qualitative approach more acceptable to **quantitative-biased audiences**. 

### Quantitative Phase Can Emerge From Qualitative Findings

* The design is useful when the need for a second, quantitative phase becomes apparent based on what was learned during the first qualitative phase.
* Thus, the quantitative phase can directly respond to discoveries from the qualitative phase. 

### Production of a New Product

* The research process can result in the production of a new:

  * Instrument
  * Measure
  * Variable
  * Set of intervention activities
  * Digital tool 
* This makes the design particularly useful when the research goal includes **developing something new**.

## Challenges in Using the Exploratory Sequential Design

### Extended Time Requirement

* Researchers must plan for an **extended period of time** to complete the study.
* The sequential approach requires considerable time to implement.
* Additional time may be needed for a third phase to develop a feature such as a **new instrument**.
* Researchers must recognize this requirement and include sufficient time in the study plan. 

### Quantitative Phase Must Be Tentatively Specified in Advance

* The quantitative phase must be **tentatively specified in advance**.
* This creates difficulty when applying for the initial **IRB approval**.
* The exact quantitative procedures may depend on what is discovered during the initial qualitative phase, making them difficult to completely specify beforehand. 

### Two Different Samples May Be Needed

* Researchers may need to identify **two different samples**.
* A recommended approach is:

  * **First phase:** small, purposeful sample.
  * **Second phase:** larger sample consisting of different participants.
* Using a different and larger sample in the quantitative phase can enhance the **generalization of quantitative results**. 

### Deciding Which Qualitative Results to Use

* After the qualitative phase, the researcher must determine **which qualitative results should be used**.
* This becomes important when developing the new quantitative feature.
* The researcher needs to decide:

  * Which qualitative findings should be selected.
  * How those findings should be used.
  * How the findings can be converted into quantitative measures or materials. 

### Researcher Must Have Multiple Skills

* The design requires **expanded research skills**.
* The researcher needs proficiency in:

  * Qualitative research
  * Quantitative research
  * Mixed methods research
  * Instrument development
  * Or digital tool development 
* Therefore, successfully conducting the design can require expertise across several methodological areas.

## Key Points to Remember

* **Exploratory sequential design = three-phase mixed methods design.**
* The sequence is:

  * **Qualitative → Development → Quantitative**
* It starts with **qualitative data collection and analysis**.
* Qualitative findings are then translated into a:

  * Quantitative measure
  * Survey
  * Instrument
  * Intervention
  * Digital tool
  * App/website
  * New variables
* The developed feature is **grounded in qualitative data** and therefore can reflect the **culture and setting of participants**. 
* The **point of integration** is where the researcher identifies which qualitative results will be used to build the quantitative feature.
* The quantitative phase uses the developed feature with a **new sample**.
* The final interpretation examines how and to what extent quantitative findings **generalize or extend** the initial qualitative findings.
* Major strengths:

  * Clear separate phases
  * Useful for product development
  * Can make qualitative research more acceptable to quantitative-oriented audiences
  * Allows quantitative testing to emerge from qualitative findings
* Major challenges:

  * Requires considerable time
  * Quantitative procedures may be difficult to specify in advance
  * May require two different samples
  * Requires careful selection of qualitative findings
  * Requires broad methodological skills

## Quick Conceptual Flow

* **Explore**

  * Collect and analyze qualitative data.
* **Identify**

  * Determine the important qualitative findings.
* **Develop**

  * Convert findings into an instrument, measure, intervention, variable, app, website, etc.
* **Test**

  * Apply the developed feature quantitatively with a new sample.
* **Interpret**

  * Determine how far the quantitative findings generalize or extend the original qualitative findings.

## Overview of the Embedded Design

* The **Embedded Design** is a **mixed methods design**.
* In this design:

  * One data set has a **supportive, secondary role**.
  * The study is primarily based on the **other type of data**. 
* The basic premise of this design is that:

  * A **single data set may not be sufficient** to answer all the research questions.
  * Different questions may need to be answered within the same study.
  * Different types of questions may require **different types of data**. 
* Therefore, the researcher can use one main methodological approach while **embedding another type of data** to provide additional information.

## Choice of the Embedded Design

* Researchers use the embedded design when they need to include:

  * **Qualitative data** within a largely quantitative study, or
  * **Quantitative data** within a largely qualitative study.
* The additional data are included to answer a particular research question that cannot be adequately answered by the primary method alone. 

### Embedded Qualitative Component in a Quantitative Design

* The design is particularly useful when a researcher needs to embed a **qualitative component within a quantitative design**.
* An important example is an **experimental design**. 
* In an experimental study, qualitative data may be included for several reasons:

  * To **develop a treatment**.
  * To **examine the process of an intervention**.
  * To **follow up on the results of an experiment**. 

## The Embedded Experimental Model

* The **Embedded Experimental Model** is defined as having **qualitative data embedded within an experimental design**.
* The experimental design may be:

  * A **true experiment**, or
  * A **quasi-experiment**. 

### Priority of the Embedded Experimental Model

* The priority of this model is established by the **quantitative experimental methodology**.
* The qualitative data set is **subservient** to the quantitative experimental methodology. 
* This means:

  * The **quantitative experimental study is the main component**.
  * The qualitative component has a **secondary/supportive role**.
  * The qualitative data are used to provide additional understanding rather than replacing the experimental methodology.

### Purpose of Adding Qualitative Data

* Qualitative data collection is added to an experiment or intervention so that the **personal experiences of participants** can be included.
* The qualitative data therefore become a **secondary source of data embedded within the experiment**. 
* This allows the researcher to obtain information about participants' experiences alongside the quantitative experimental outcomes.

### One-Phase and Two-Phase Approaches

* The embedded experimental model can be used as:

  * A **one-phase approach**, or
  * A **two-phase approach**.
* The timing of qualitative data collection depends on the **purpose for including the qualitative data**. 

## Timing of Qualitative Data Collection

* The researcher must decide **when** qualitative data should be collected in relation to the experimental study.
* Qualitative data can be collected:

  * **Before** the intervention
  * **During** the intervention
  * **After** the intervention
* This decision should be based on the **intent for including the qualitative data**. 

### Before the Intervention

* Qualitative data can be collected **before an experiment**.
* The researcher must decide which qualitative results will subsequently be used in the quantitative phase.
* The qualitative data collection should be carefully designed according to its purpose. 
* One possible purpose is to:

  * **Develop an instrument**, or
  * **Shape the intervention**.
* Researchers collect qualitative information before the experiment so that the information can be used to plan **specific intervention activities**.
* These activities can then be made more:

  * **Appealing** to participants.
  * **Useful** to participants. 

### During the Intervention

* In a **one-phase approach**, qualitative data can be embedded **during the intervention phase**.
* This is useful when the researcher wants to qualitatively examine the **process of the intervention** in addition to measuring quantitative outcomes. 
* When qualitative data are collected during the experiment, researchers often ask **process questions**.
* These questions help identify:

  * How participants experience the intervention.
  * What happens during the intervention process. 

### After the Intervention

* Qualitative data can also be collected **after the intervention**.
* The researcher must decide:

  * Which aspects of the trial should be explored further.
  * The criteria that will be used to select participants for follow-up data collection. 
* The researcher may conduct in-depth follow-up with:

  * Participants who received the treatment.
  * Selected cases based on **positive treatment outcomes**.
  * Selected cases based on **negative treatment outcomes**. 
* Qualitative data collected after the intervention can:

  * Explore the outcome results in greater detail.
  * Help explain **why the intervention worked or did not work**. 

## How to Decide the Timing

* The timing of qualitative data collection should always be connected to the **purpose of including qualitative data**.

* **Before intervention**

  * Used when the researcher wants to **shape or develop the intervention**.
  * Can help develop an instrument.
  * Can help design activities that are appealing and useful to participants.

* **During intervention**

  * Used when the researcher wants to understand the **process of the intervention**.
  * Helps explore participants' experiences while receiving the intervention.
  * Uses process-oriented questions.

* **After intervention**

  * Used when the researcher wants to **follow up on experimental results**.
  * Helps explain positive or negative outcomes.
  * Provides a deeper understanding of why the intervention did or did not work.

## Strengths of the Embedded Design

### Useful When Time or Resources Are Limited

* The embedded design can be used when the researcher does not have sufficient:

  * **Time**, or
  * **Resources**
    to conduct extensive qualitative and quantitative data collection. 
* This is possible because one type of data is given **less priority** than the other.
* The researcher can therefore focus most resources on the primary methodology while collecting a smaller amount of secondary data.

### Logistically Manageable for Students

* The design may be particularly appealing and manageable for **students**.
* One method requires **less data** than the other method. 
* Therefore, students can use an additional method without having to conduct equally extensive qualitative and quantitative studies.

### Appealing to Funding Agencies

* The embedded design may be attractive to **funding agencies**.
* This is because the primary focus is traditionally quantitative.
* An example is an **experimental trial** where quantitative experimentation remains the main focus. 

## Challenges in Using the Embedded Design

### Purpose of the Additional Data Must Be Specified

* The researcher must clearly specify the **purpose** of collecting the secondary data.
* This applies when:

  * Qualitative data are part of a larger quantitative study, or
  * Quantitative data are part of a larger qualitative study. 
* The researcher needs to know why the additional data are necessary and what research question they are intended to address.

### Determining the Appropriate Point of Data Collection

* In an experimental study, it can be challenging to determine the appropriate point at which to collect qualitative data.
* The researcher must decide whether collection should occur:

  * Before the intervention
  * During the intervention
  * After the intervention 
* The decision should correspond to the intended purpose of the qualitative component.

### Requires Expertise in Different Methods

* Researchers need expertise in:

  * **Experimental research**
  * **Qualitative research** 
* This can make the design demanding because the researcher must be able to appropriately conduct both the primary experimental methodology and the embedded qualitative component.

### Limited Literature for Some Embedded Designs

* Few examples exist of certain forms of embedded design.
* In particular, relatively little has been written about **embedding quantitative data within traditionally qualitative designs**. 
* This limited methodological literature can make it more difficult for researchers to find established examples or guidance.

## Key Points to Remember

* **Embedded Design = one data type is primary + another data type plays a supportive/secondary role.**
* It is used because:

  * One data set may not be sufficient.
  * Different research questions require different types of data.
* The **Embedded Experimental Model** specifically embeds **qualitative data within a quantitative experimental design**.
* The quantitative experimental methodology has **priority**.
* Qualitative data are **secondary/subservient** to the experimental methodology.
* The qualitative component can capture the **personal experiences of participants**.
* Qualitative data may be collected:

  * **Before** intervention → to develop an instrument or shape the intervention.
  * **During** intervention → to understand the intervention process and participant experiences.
  * **After** intervention → to explore outcomes and explain why the intervention did or did not work.
* The design can be conducted as a **one-phase or two-phase approach**.
* Major strengths:

  * Saves time and resources.
  * Can be manageable for students.
  * May be attractive to funding agencies.
* Major challenges:

  * Purpose of the embedded data must be clearly specified.
  * Appropriate timing must be determined.
  * Requires expertise in multiple research methods.
  * Limited examples exist for some forms of embedded design. 

## Quick Comparison of the Embedded Experimental Model

* **Main study:** Quantitative experimental study.
* **Embedded component:** Qualitative data.
* **Priority:** Quantitative/experimental methodology.
* **Role of qualitative data:** Secondary/supportive.
* **Before intervention:** Helps shape or develop the intervention.
* **During intervention:** Explores the intervention process and participant experiences.
* **After intervention:** Explores outcomes in depth and explains why the intervention worked or did not work.
* **Possible experimental designs:** True experiment or quasi-experiment.
