# **Characteristics of a Good Health Promotion Message**

A good health promotion message should be:

* **Accurate**
* Focused on **a few key points**
* **Specific**
* **Clear**
* Written using **uncomplicated language**
* **Simple to understand**
* **Relevant**
* **Culturally appropriate and acceptable**
* Focused on:

  * Available options
  * Practical actions
  * Possible solutions 

### Easy memory

**Good message = Accurate + Focused + Specific + Clear + Simple + Relevant + Culturally appropriate + Practical**

---

# **Overall Design of a Health Promotion Message**

The overall design should:

* Be **consistent**.
* Avoid **clutter**.
* Provide a clear guide for finding **key information**.
* Clearly label:

  * Illustrations
  * Charts
* Offer explanations where required.
* Make **legends clear**.
* Place charts as close as possible to their **explanatory text**.
* Use consistent and easily recognized **headings**.
* Signal important points using:

  * **Bold**
  * Highlights
* **Break the monotony** of the material. 

---

# **Style of Writing**

* Write using the **active voice**.
* Use **everyday words**.
* Avoid:

  * Long sentences
  * Complex sentences
* Technical terms should be explained using **examples**.
* The reader should remain sufficiently **engaged**.
* Adding **contextuality** makes the message more relatable. 

---

# **Text Appearance**

## **Font Size**

* Font size affects readability.
* The lecture illustrates different font sizes:

  * 8 point
  * 10 point
  * 12 point
  * 14 point
  * 16 point
  * 18 point 

## **Type and Spacing**

* Use appropriate spacing between lines.
* Maintain good contrast between:

  * Paper/background
  * Text
* Prefer using **upper and lower case** rather than writing everything in only upper case or only lower case. 

## **Font Style**

* **Serif fonts** are usually easier to read than sans-serif fonts.
* For body text:

  * Prefer **serif fonts**.
* For headings/subheadings:

  * Use **sans-serif fonts**.
* Avoid:

  * Script fonts
  * Decorative fonts
* Limit the use of:

  * Underlining
  * Italics 

---

# **Approaches to Health Promotion Messages**

## **Informative Approach**

* Creates awareness about a **new idea**.
* Makes the new idea familiar to people.
* **Mass media** is mainly used for:

  * Wide coverage
  * Reaching a large audience
* **Print materials** and **interpersonal communication** are used to:

  * Reinforce the message
  * Inform people in greater detail. 

---

## **Educating Approach**

* Explains the new idea, including its:

  * Strengths
  * Weaknesses
* Appropriate when people are already aware of an idea but need:

  * More information
  * Clarification
* **Interpersonal communication** with individuals or small groups is probably the most appropriate method for detailed information.
* It can be reinforced with:

  * Books
  * Pamphlets
  * Films
  * Slide shows
  * Videos
  * Other multimedia approaches 

---

## **Persuasive Approach**

* Promotes a positive change in:

  * Behavior
  * Attitudes
* Encourages the audience to **accept a new idea**.
* Message development involves identifying what **appeals most to a particular audience**.
* **Interpersonal communication** is the most effective way to communicate persuasive messages according to the lecture. 

---

## **Prompting Approach**

* Messages are designed so they are:

  * Not easily ignored
  * Not easily forgotten
* They remind the audience about something.
* They reinforce earlier messages.
* Example:

  * **Waste disposal** messages. 

---

## **Entertaining Approach**

* Uses entertainment to attract the audience's attention to a new idea.
* Examples:

  * Posters
  * Songs
  * Humour
  * Puppets
  * Films 

---

  * Accurate

  * Clear
  * Simple

  * **Informative**
  * **Educating**
  * **Persuasive**
  * **Prompting**
  * **Entertaining**

# **Content of a Health Promotion Message**

Message content can be designed using different types of appeals and arguments:

* **Emotional or rational**
* **Positive or negative**
* **Mass or individual appeals**
* **Humorous or serious appeals**
* **One-sided or two-sided arguments**
* **Direct or indirect**
* **Repeated or “one time” appeals**
* **Definite conclusion or open conclusion** 

---

# **Making a Message Clear**

### **1. Give the Most Important Information First**

* Engage the audience quickly.
* State **why the information is important to them**.

### **2. Limit the Number of Messages**

* Create short lists.
* Prefer **3–7 items**.
* Use **bullets**, rather than putting items together using commas.

### **3. Tell the Audience What They Need to Do**

* Include a clear **call to action**.
* Use the **active voice**.

### **4. Explain the Benefit**

* Tell the audience what they will gain by:

  * Understanding the material
  * Using the material

### **5. Choose Words Carefully**

* Keep the message:

  * Short
  * Encouraging
  * Free of jargon
  * Unambiguous

### **6. Limit Statistics**

* Avoid excessive use of statistics.
* Where appropriate, use general words such as:

  * Most
  * Many
  * Half

### **7. Limit Symbols**

* Avoid unnecessary use of symbols.

### **8. Limit Quotation Marks**

* Do not overuse quotation marks. 

---

# **Using Visuals**

## **1. Choose the Best Type of Visual**

### Photographs

* Work best for showing:

  * Real-life events
  * People
  * Emotions
* Tend to be **more compelling** to audiences.

### Simple Illustrations / Line Drawings

* May be better in certain situations.
* Particularly useful for:

  * Showing procedures, e.g. drawing blood
  * Depicting socially sensitive issues, e.g. drug addiction
  * Explaining invisible or difficult-to-see events, e.g. airborne transmission of tuberculosis
* Keep drawings **simple**.
* Avoid unnecessary details.

### Cartoons

* Can be useful for:

  * Conveying humour
  * Creating a more casual tone 

---

## **2. Use Visuals to Communicate the Message**

* Present **one message per visual**.
* Several messages in one visual may cause audiences to miss some or all of them.
* Label visuals with **captions**.
* Place:

  * Visuals
  * Captions
  * Related text
    close to one another.
* Visuals should:

  * Emphasize the text
  * Explain the text
* Consider:

  * Available space
  * Intended use of the visual
* Avoid visuals that:

  * Merely decorate
  * Are too abstract
* Show the **actions you want the audience to take**.
* Avoid images showing actions the audience **should not do**. 

---

## **3. Make Visuals Culturally Relevant and Sensitive**

* Use images and symbols familiar to the audience.
* When people are shown:

  * Images should be relatable to the intended audience.
  * The lecture recommends trying to represent the same racial or ethnic group as the intended audience.
* Choose images that the audience can:

  * Recognize
  * Relate to 

---

## **4. Make Visuals Easy to Follow**

* Place visuals near the text to which they refer.
* Ensure visuals connect directly to written messages.
* Use **brief captions** containing the key message.
* A caption can repeat a sentence from the body to reinforce the message.
* When showing a sequence:

  * **Number the images**.
* Use cues such as:

  * Arrows
  * Circles
* These point the audience toward key information. 

---

## **5. Drawings / Pictographs**

* **Pictographs** are pictures representing:

  * Words
  * Ideas
* They can:

  * Convey information quickly
  * Help people understand the intended message
  * Help people remember the message
* They are most effective when focusing on a **specific action**.
* They require **thorough pretesting** to ensure effectiveness.
* Photographs can also function as pictographs. 

---

## **6. Realistic Images**

* Use realistic images when illustrating:

  * Internal body parts
  * Small objects
* For small objects:

  * Draw them larger to show detail.
* Include something familiar to provide a **sense of scale**. 

---

## **7. High-Quality Visuals**

Visuals should have:

* Sharp resolution
* True colour
* Good contrast
* Good composition

High-quality visuals make the message **more credible**.

* Professional design assistance can be sought to create materials that:

  * Attract the audience
  * Inform the audience 

---

## **Lecture 2 — Key Takeaways**

* Message content should consider:

  * Appeals
  * Arguments
  * Culture
  * Audience
* Messages should be:

  * Clear
  * Concise
  * Simple
  * Engaging
* Visuals are an **integral part** of a good health promotion message.
* Visuals should be:

  * Relevant
  * Understandable
  * Culturally appropriate
  * High quality
  * Properly pretested.

# **Different Formats of Counselling**

### **1. In-Person Counselling**

* Face-to-face counselling.
* Conducted:

  * In health facilities
  * During household visits

### **2. Group Counselling**

* Conducted for people with **similar health issues or problems**.

### **3. Telephonic Counselling**

* Useful for busy individuals.
* Especially useful for people who may find it difficult to visit health facilities.

### **4. Online Counselling**

* Useful for people who:

  * Do not want face-to-face interaction
  * Want to protect their anonymity 

---

# **Five-Stage Model of Counselling**

### **Stage 1 — Initial Disclosure**

* **Relationship building**

### **Stage 2 — In-depth Exploration**

* **Problem assessment**

### **Stage 3 — Commitment to Action**

* **Goal setting**

### **Stage 4**

* **Counselling intervention**

### **Stage 5**

* **Evaluation**
* **Termination**
* Or **Referral** 

### ⭐ Easy sequence

**Relationship → Assessment → Goals → Intervention → Evaluation/Termination/Referral**

---

# **Core Counselling Skills**

## **1. Attending**

* Active listening.
* Give full attention to what the client:

  * Says
  * Does
* Value the client as a worthy individual.

## **2. Silence**

* Gives the client control over:

  * Content
  * Pace
  * Objectives

## **3. Reflecting and Paraphrasing**

* Repeat the client's story in a shorter form.
* This is called **paraphrasing**.

## **4. Clarifying and Using Questions**

* Use **open questions** to clarify understanding of what the client is feeling.
* Avoid **leading questions** because they can impair the counselling process.

## **5. Focusing**

* Decide which issues should receive priority.
* Clients may present several issues/problems.
* Focusing allows counsellor and client to concentrate on the **priority issues**.

## **6. Building Rapport**

* Develop a sense of **connection** with the client.

## **7. Summarizing**

* A summary is a longer paraphrase.
* Captures the:

  * Essence of what the client says
  * Feelings expressed
* Sums up the **main themes**. 

---

  * Including crisis counselling
* **Psychological counselling**

* Provide advice.
* Be judgmental.
* Create emotional attachment with the client.

### Important

* Counselling technique should be adjusted according to the **different phases of a health problem**.

# **Group Discussion**

* A very effective **two-way method of health communication**.
* People learn by:

  * Exchanging views
  * Exchanging experiences
* Effective group size:

  * **Not less than 6**
  * **Not more than 12**
* As group size increases:

  * Participation decreases
  * Discussion decreases
* Adequate time should be allowed for:

  * Questions
  * Discussion 

---

# **Steps in Conducting a Group Discussion**

### **Step 1 — Select Topic**

* Select a topic based on the **needs of the group**.
* Example:

  * Timely, adequate and appropriate complementary feeding for infants.

### **Step 2 — Collect Information**

* Collect **correct and up-to-date information** about the topic.

### **Step 3 — Prepare Lesson Plan**

The lesson plan should specify:

* What participants should:

  * Know
  * Understand
* Main points to be discussed.
* Sequence of discussion.
* Visual/audiovisual aids to be used.

### **Step 4 — Select Time and Place**

* Select a time convenient for participants.
* Choose a place free from barriers such as:

  * Noise
  * Extreme temperature

### **Step 5 — Conduct and Check Understanding**

* Conduct the discussion.
* Ask questions at the end to **check understanding**. 

---

# **Panel Discussion**

* Panel consists of:

  * A **chairperson/moderator**
  * **4–8 speakers/panelists**
* Panelists sit together and discuss a given problem.
* Discussion takes place in front of:

  * A group
  * An audience
* There are **no set speeches**.
* Discussion is:

  * Spontaneous
  * Natural
* After panelists discuss the subject:

  * Audience is invited to participate. 

---

# **Symposium**

* A symposium consists of a **series of speeches**.
* Speeches are given by **experts**.
* All speeches focus on a **selected subject**.
* Experts do not have further discussion among themselves on the subject.
* At the end:

  * Audience may ask questions.
  * Audience may contribute to the symposium. 

### Difference from Panel Discussion

* **Panel:** spontaneous discussion among panelists.
* **Symposium:** series of expert speeches.

---

# **Workshop**

* Oxford English Dictionary definition in the lecture:

  * A meeting where a group engages in **intensive discussion and activity on a particular subject**.
* Participants are divided into **small groups**.
* Each group selects:

  * A leader
  * A rapporteur
* Each group:

  * Solves part of the problem
  * Or provides recommendations
* Experts assist the groups.
* Focus is on:

  * Participatory learning
  * Hands-on learning
  * Small-group activity
  * Problem solving
  * Pair discussions
  * Small-group discussions
* Experience is **active rather than passive**. 

---

# **Role Play / Socio-Drama**

* Role play means **acting out real-life situations and problems**.
* Purpose:

  * Helps people understand causes of their problems.
  * Helps people understand the results of their own behavior.
* Particularly useful for:

  * Attitudes
  * Values
* Usually conducted with **small groups**.
* This ensures all participants can observe the role play.

### Time

* Role play should last approximately **20 minutes**.
* It should be stopped when:

  * Players have solved the problem
  * Players are becoming confused
  * Audience becomes bored
* An additional **20–30 minutes** should be allowed for discussion.
* Discussion should focus on important issues reflected through the play. 

### ⭐ Important numbers

* Role play: **~20 min**
* Discussion afterwards: **20–30 min**

---

# **Demonstrations**

* Demonstrations combine:

  * Theoretical teaching
  * Practical work
* They are a lively mix of both.
* Used to teach **new skills**.

### Examples

* Preparing ORS
* Positioning and attaching an infant to the mother's breast for breastfeeding

### Group Size

* Usually conducted with **small groups**.
* Allows participants to:

  * Practice skills
  * Ask questions
* Adequate time should be provided for:

  * Questions
  * Practice 

---

# **Steps in Conducting a Demonstration**

### **Step 1 — Explain**

* Explain the ideas and skills to be demonstrated.
* Use:

  * Photographs
  * Pictures
  * Real objects

### **Step 2 — Demonstrate Slowly**

* Perform the demonstration:

  * Slowly
  * One step at a time
* Ensure everyone can see.
* Explain and repeat steps as necessary.

### **Step 3 — Participant Repeats**

* Ask one participant to repeat the demonstration.
* The group then comments.

### **Step 4 — Everyone Practices**

* Everyone gets an opportunity to practice.
* Participants are:

  * Observed
  * Given suggestions
* Working in pairs can be useful because participants can:

  * Help each other
  * Give suggestions

### **Step 5 — Debriefing**

* Check that **everyone can correctly practice the skill** before leaving the demonstration venue. 

---

# **Appropriate Delivery of a Health Promotion Message**

Three important points:

* **Choose the right time**
* **Choose a convenient place**
* **Involve people**

# **What is Pretesting?**

* Pretesting is the process of determining a **target group's reaction to and understanding of health messages or behavior-change information** before materials are produced in final form.
* Members of the target group are asked to react to **draft BCC/intervention materials**.
* Their responses are:

  * Analyzed
  * Used to revise the materials
* Pretesting may be conducted **several times** before final materials are produced.
* It can be a **cyclical process** involving recurring feedback. 

### ⭐ Simple definition

**Pretesting = Test the draft intervention material with the target audience → collect reactions → analyze → revise → test again if needed.**

---

# **Why Pretest?**

Pretesting helps determine whether:

* Messages are appropriate.
* Materials are appropriate.
* Language is understandable.
* Illustrations are appropriate.
* Messages are effective.
* Material is suitable for the intended audience.

It also:

* Increases the impact of materials.
* Helps determine whether the design is suitable for the audience.
* Can save:

  * Money
  * Time
  * Energy
* This is because the resulting material is more likely to be effective. 

---

# **Who Should Conduct Pretesting?**

* A small, focused team of key program staff.
* Usually around **3–4 people**.
* People conducting the actual pretesting should preferably be:

  * Similar to the priority audience
  * Trained in pretesting
* Having people similar to the audience encourages:

  * Honesty
  * Openness during the process 

---

# **When Should Pretesting Be Conducted?**

* Pretesting should be completed **before communication campaign components are finalized, produced and disseminated**.
* Pretesting takes time.
* Time required depends on:

  * Objectives
  * Needs of the pretest 

---

### **Step 1**

* Outline pretest objectives.

### **Step 2**

* Choose the pretest method.

### **Step 3**

* Prepare for pretesting.

### **Step 4**

* Develop the pretesting guide.

### **Step 5**

* Develop questions for the target audience.

### **Step 6**

* Analyze pretesting data and interpret results.

### **Step 7**

# **Step 1 — Outline Pretest Objectives**

Example used in the lecture:

### Communication Material

* Posters
* Two alternative versions

### Target Audience

* Young mothers
* **18–25 years**
* From a village
* Basic literacy levels

### Promoted Behavior

* **Spacing pregnancies by at least two years**

### Objectives

Determine:

* Whether language, readability and expressions are appropriate for young rural mothers with basic literacy.
* Whether:

  * Content
  * Font size
  * Source
    are appealing and believable.
* Whether the poster motivates young rural mothers to practice **birth spacing**.
* Which poster version best meets the **change objectives**. 

---

# **Step 2 — Choose the Pretest Method**

The choice depends on several factors.

## **Purpose**

* Individual interviews provide more **detailed individual input**.
* FGDs provide **broader insights**.

## **Type of Material**

* Long materials may be difficult to test through individual interviews.

## **Audience Characteristics**

* Some audiences may be uncomfortable expressing themselves in a group.
* One-to-one interviews may therefore be used for:

  * Low-literacy audiences
  * People difficult to gather into peer groups
  * Local leaders

## **Available Resources**

* Ideally, testing continues until **no new information** is obtained.
* However, this depends on:

  * Time
  * Resources

## **Complex / Sensitive Messages**

* People may be less honest or frank about sensitive issues in groups.
* This is particularly true in mixed groups.
* Participants may also feel embarrassed about saying that they do not understand certain:

  * Words
  * Concepts

## **How Material Will Be Used**

* Material used in **group settings → test through FGDs**.
* Material used in **individual counselling → test through individual interviews**. 

---

# **Common Pretesting Methods**

## **Individual Interviews**

* One interviewer + one participant.
* Conducted in a:

  * Private
  * Confidential
    setting.

## **Focus Group Discussions (FGDs)**

* Small-group sessions.
* Around **8–10 people per session**.
* Materials and messages are discussed collectively.

## **Other Methods**

### Readability Assessment

* Determines the **reading difficulty level** of written material.
* Conducted during material development **before pretesting with the target audience**.

### Expert Review

* Experts review draft materials.
* Provide:

  * Comments
  * Suggestions
    for improvement. 

---

# **Step 3 — Preparation for Pretesting**

Preparation includes:

* Preparing materials to be tested.
* Preparing the discussion guide.
* Selecting and briefing:

  * FGD leaders
  * Individual interviewers
* Selecting and briefing **note-takers**.
* Selecting the test site.
* Arranging:

  * Transportation
  * Beverages
  * Snacks
* Selecting test participants.
* Making necessary provisions for participants. 

---

# **Step 4 — Develop the Pretesting Guide**

The guide should include:

* **Background information**
* **Pretest objectives**
* **Pretest plan**

  * Pretest method
  * Location
  * Participants
  * Facilitators/moderators
  * Note-takers
* **Pretest questions**
* **Plan for use of information gathered** 

---

# **Step 5 — Develop Questions**

### Purpose

* Questions should help understand the **value of the materials**.

### Example

* “How effective are the posters in influencing young parents to practice birth spacing?”

### Question Type

* Use a series of **open-ended questions**.
* These gather detailed information about:

  * Audience preferences
  * Reactions

### Avoid

* Close-ended **Yes/No questions**.
* Leading questions that direct participants toward a particular response.

### Also Collect

* Demographic information:

  * Age
  * Education level
  * Marital status
  * Number of children
* Details about how participants spend their day:

  * Media use
  * Social gatherings 

---

# **Step 6 — Analyze Pretesting Data and Interpret Results**

During analysis:

* Identify **trends in responses**.
* If a particular problem/change is mentioned repeatedly:

  * It needs to be addressed.
* Identify flaws in:

  * Design
  * Messages
  * Format
* Redesign or revise the material accordingly. 

---

# **Step 7 — Summarize the Results**

A report should be prepared describing the **process and findings**.

### **Background**

Describe:

* What was tested?
* What were the pretest objectives?
* Which audience participated?
* Why was that audience selected?
* How was the pretest conducted?
* How many participants were involved?

### **Highlights**

* Summarize the main points emerging from testing.

### **Findings**

* Provide a complete report of findings.
* Where appropriate:

  * Describe participant reactions.
  * Include important quotes.
  * Identify which messages/materials worked.
  * Identify which were not appealing or effective.

### **Conclusions**

* List:

  * Response patterns
  * Major differences across individuals/groups

### **Recommendations**

* Suggest and prioritize revisions to:

  * Ideas
  * Messages
  * Materials
* Recommendations should be based on:

  * Findings
  * Conclusions

# **Aspects of an Intervention Material to Pretest**

## **1. Attractiveness**

### What to assess

* Whether the message/material **commands attention**.

### Sample questions

* What do you like about this message/material?
* What do you not like?
* What was the first thing that caught your eye? 

---

## **2. Comprehension**

### What to assess

* Whether the information is understood **as intended**.

### Sample questions

* What does the message/material say?
* Who do you think the message/material is speaking to?
* Which words, sentences or images are difficult to understand? 

---

## **3. Acceptance**

### What to assess

* Whether the material is **culturally and socially acceptable**.

### Sample questions

* Is anything in the material:

  * Offensive?
  * Inappropriate?
* Is there anything someone in your community might find offensive or inappropriate? 

---

## **4. Relevance**

### What to assess

* Whether the information is of **interest to the intended audience**.

### Sample questions

* What type of people should read/watch this material?
* In what ways are those people:

  * Different from you?
  * Similar to you? 

---

## **5. Call to Action**

### What to assess

* Whether the audience understands **what action the message asks them to take**.

### Sample question

* What does the message/material ask the audience to do? 

---

## **6. Persuasion**

### What to assess

* Whether the key benefit is:

  * Persuasive
  * Appealing
    to the intended audience.

### Sample questions

* Why do you think you should do what the message asks?
* How likely are you to do it, and why? 

---

## **7. Improvement**

### What to assess

* Whether/how the material needs improvement.

### Sample questions

* What would you change to make the material more appealing?
* What information is missing?
* What else would you like included?

# **Message Development → Pretesting → Revision**

Message development should be based on **audience analysis**.

### Process

**1. Develop message concepts**

* Preliminary:

  * Illustrations
  * Words
  * Phrases
  * Theme lines
  * Slogans

**2. Pretest concepts**

* Test with groups or representatives of the intended audience.
* Pay particular attention to:

  * Pictures
  * Other visual materials
* These can easily be misunderstood.

**3. Create complete messages/materials**
Examples:

* Radio programs
* Posters
* Drama
* Puppet shows

**4. Pretest complete materials**
Assess:

* Comprehension
* Recall
* Strong points
* Weak points
* Personal relevance

Use representatives of the intended audience **before final production**.

**5. Re-test existing materials**

* Existing materials should be **retested before reproduction**.

## **Lecture 5 — Key Takeaways**

* Pretesting determines the target audience's:

  * Reaction
  * Understanding
    of health messages before final production.
* It can be a **cyclical process**.
* It should occur before materials are:

  * Finalized
  * Produced
  * Disseminated
* Common methods:

  * Individual interviews
  * FGDs
* Readability is important.
* Materials should be **retested before reproduction**.

# **Lecture 5 — Pretesting**

### Definition

**Pretesting = target audience reacts to draft material → responses analyzed → material revised → repeated if necessary.**

### Why?

* Check appropriateness
* Check language
* Check illustrations
* Check effectiveness
* Improve impact
* Save time, money and energy

### Who?

* Small focused team
* **3–4 program staff**
* Preferably similar to target audience
* Trained in pretesting

### When?

* Before finalization
* Before production
* Before dissemination

### 7 Steps

**Objectives → Method → Preparation → Guide → Questions → Analysis → Summary**

### Methods

* Individual interviews
* FGDs (**8–10 people/session**)
* Readability assessment
* Expert review

### Seven things to pretest

**Attractiveness → Comprehension → Acceptance → Relevance → Call to action → Persuasion → Improvement**

### Final cycle

**Audience analysis → Message concepts → Pretest → Complete material → Pretest → Final production → Retest before reproduction**
